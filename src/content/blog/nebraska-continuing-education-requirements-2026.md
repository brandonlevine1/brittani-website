## Understanding Nebraska Continuing Education Requirements 2026

The Nebraska Real Estate Commission (NREC) governs all licensing and education mandates under the Nebraska Real Estate License Act, codified at Neb. Rev. Stat. Sections 76-2201 through 76-2256. If you hold an active salesperson or broker license, the Nebraska continuing education requirements 2026 cycle demands your attention now—not in November when the deadline is breathing down your neck. Failure to meet these requirements does not simply pause your career; it exposes you, your brokerage, and your clients to real legal and financial risk.

Nebraska operates on a biennial renewal cycle. Licenses expire on November 30 of even-numbered years, which means the current cycle closes on November 30, 2026. Every licensee must file a renewal application and demonstrate completed CE hours before that date, or the license lapses automatically the following day.

The NREC has tightened its audit processes in recent cycles. Random audits now pull roughly 10 percent of renewal applications for documentation verification, up from the informal reviews of years past. Agents who assume a checkbox on a renewal form is sufficient are discovering that assumption can cost them months of active practice.

## Hour Requirements and Course Breakdown

**Nebraska requires 18 hours of approved continuing education for salespersons and broker associates during each two-year renewal period.** Designated brokers face a higher bar: 24 total hours, which includes a mandatory 6-hour broker management course. These numbers are non-negotiable and cannot be waived except under narrow hardship provisions outlined in Title 299, Chapter 4 of the Nebraska Administrative Code.

The 18-hour salesperson requirement breaks down into two categories. At least 12 hours must come from designated core courses that the NREC approves each cycle—topics like agency law updates, fair housing compliance, and contract changes. The remaining 6 hours may be filled with approved elective courses covering subjects such as property management, commercial real estate, or technology ethics.

| License Type | Total CE Hours | Core Hours | Elective Hours | Additional Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salesperson | 18 | 12 | 6 | None |
| Broker Associate | 18 | 12 | 6 | None |
| Designated Broker | 24 | 12 | 6 | 6-hour broker mgmt course |

One distinction that catches agents off guard: hours earned before the cycle start date of December 1, 2024, do not count toward the 2026 renewal. Courses completed during a prior cycle satisfy only that prior cycle's requirement, even if the content remains identical.

## The Renewal Process and Key Forms

Renewal applications are submitted through the NREC's online licensing portal or via paper Form 1010—the official Nebraska Real Estate License Renewal Application. Agents must attest under penalty of perjury that CE requirements have been met. The designated broker must also sign a certification on behalf of each affiliated licensee when processing renewal through the brokerage, using Form 1011 (Broker Certification of Licensee Activity).

**Agents do not submit CE certificates with the renewal form itself.** Instead, the NREC relies on approved education providers to report completion data electronically. However, agents are required to retain certificates of completion for a minimum of five years from the date of course completion, per NREC regulation. If selected for audit, you must produce documentation within 30 days of the Commission's written request.

The renewal fee for the 2024-2026 cycle is $200 for salespersons and $225 for brokers, payable at the time of application. Late renewals filed between December 1 and December 31 incur a $100 penalty. After December 31, reinstatement follows a different process entirely, requiring a new application, additional fees, and in some cases retaking portions of the licensing exam.

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

A lapsed license is not a slap on the wrist—it is a full stop. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. Section 76-2236, any person who engages in real estate activities without a valid license commits a Class II misdemeanor, punishable by up to six months in jail and a $1,000 fine. In practice, criminal prosecution is rare. The more immediate consequences hit your wallet and your transactions.

Any commission earned during a lapse period is not legally enforceable. A seller or buyer who discovers their agent's license was inactive at the time of contract execution can petition to void the agreement. Brokerages face civil liability for allowing unlicensed activity to occur under their roof, and E&O carriers routinely deny claims arising from transactions handled by lapsed licensees.

The NREC can also impose disciplinary action beyond the lapse itself. Repeated failures to complete CE, or falsely attesting to completion on Form 1010, can result in license revocation, civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation, and public disciplinary records that follow you indefinitely. The Commission publishes all formal disciplinary orders on its [public records page](https://nrec.nebraska.gov/).

## Common Mistakes Nebraska Agents Make

Five errors show up again and again in NREC audit findings and disciplinary proceedings. Avoiding them is straightforward if you know where agents typically stumble.

**First, agents confuse "enrolled" with "completed."** Registering for a course or watching 80 percent of video content does not count. The NREC requires passage of a final assessment and issuance of a completion certificate. Partial progress earns zero credit.

Second, agents take courses from non-approved providers. The NREC maintains a list of approved continuing education providers and courses, updated annually. A course approved in Kansas or Iowa does not automatically qualify in Nebraska. Before paying tuition, verify the course number appears on the NREC's current approved course list for the 2024-2026 cycle.

Third, agents wait until the final two weeks of November to complete all 18 hours. Provider systems crash, proctored exam slots fill up, and technical glitches happen. The Commission does not grant extensions for procrastination-related technology failures. Spreading coursework across the two-year window eliminates this entirely predictable crisis—something we explored in [why agents miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines).

Fourth, designated brokers neglect the broker management course. They complete 18 hours of standard CE and assume they have met the requirement, forgetting the additional 6-hour broker-specific mandate. This error triggers an automatic lapse of the broker license, which in turn suspends every salesperson license held under that brokerage.

Fifth, agents lose their completion certificates. Five years of retention sounds simple until you change email providers, switch computers, or leave a brokerage that stored records on its server. Download certificates immediately, store them in at least two locations, and confirm your provider reported completion to NREC electronically.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Designated brokers carry a supervisory obligation that extends well beyond their own CE compliance. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. Section 76-2232, the designated broker is responsible for ensuring that all licensees affiliated with the brokerage maintain active, valid licenses at all times. Ignorance is not a defense.

| Broker Audit Item | Frequency | Consequence of Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Verify each agent's CE completion status | Quarterly recommended, mandatory before Nov 30 | Brokerage liability for unlicensed activity |
| Confirm approved provider and course numbers | At time of agent's reported completion | CE hours may be disallowed in audit |
| Retain copies of agent CE certificates | Ongoing, 5-year minimum | Commission sanctions, audit failure |
| Submit Form 1011 broker certification | At renewal | Agents cannot renew without it |
| Track broker management course (own) | Each cycle | Broker license lapse, cascading agent suspensions |

**Brokers who fail to monitor agent compliance risk having every transaction conducted by a lapsed licensee retroactively challenged.** That exposure multiplies quickly in a brokerage with 20 or 50 agents. Building a systematic tracking process—whether spreadsheet-based, calendar-driven, or software-assisted—is not optional. It is part of the job.

Many brokerages assign this tracking to a transaction coordinator or office manager. The key is establishing accountability: someone must own the calendar, verify documentation, and flag agents who fall behind. ## Hardship Exemptions and Military Provisions

Nebraska law provides limited exceptions for licensees facing documented hardships. Under Title 299, Chapter 4, Section 005, an agent may petition the NREC for a CE extension due to serious illness, military deployment, or other extraordinary circumstances. The petition must be filed before the November 30 deadline—not after. Extensions are granted at the Commission's sole discretion and are not automatic.

Active-duty military members and their spouses receive additional protections under Neb. Rev. Stat. Section 76-2237.01. Licenses held by service members on active duty outside Nebraska do not lapse during deployment, and the CE completion deadline extends 180 days beyond the service member's return to the state. Supporting documentation—military orders and discharge or reassignment records—must accompany the extension request.

These provisions are narrow. A busy transaction season, a family vacation, or a technology issue does not constitute hardship under NREC standards. Agents who believe they qualify should contact the Commission directly and file their petition with supporting documentation no later than October of the renewal year.

## Planning Ahead: A Timeline That Works

Starting CE coursework early in the cycle gives agents a buffer against unexpected complications. A reasonable pace means completing roughly 9 hours per year, or one 3-hour course every four months. That cadence prevents the November panic and ensures you have time to remedy any provider-reporting errors.

The NREC typically publishes its approved course list for the new cycle by January of the first year—meaning the 2024-2026 list has been available since early 2025.
