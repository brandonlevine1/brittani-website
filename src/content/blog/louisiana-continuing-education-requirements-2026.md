## Understanding Louisiana Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Every active real estate licensee in Louisiana faces the same annual question: have I completed enough continuing education hours to keep my license current? The Louisiana continuing education requirements 2026 remain governed by the Louisiana Real Estate Commission (LREC) under Title 37, Chapter 17 of the Louisiana Revised Statutes and the administrative rules codified at Louisiana Administrative Code Title 46, Part LXVII. Failing to track these requirements is not a minor administrative hiccup—it can cost you active deals, your income stream, and potentially your license altogether.

The LREC does not grant grace periods the way some states do. If your CE is incomplete when your license term expires, you are inactive the moment that clock runs out. This reality catches more agents off guard than you might expect, particularly those juggling closings, listing appointments, and the assumption that "I still have time."

## Hour Breakdown and Mandatory Topics

**Louisiana requires 12 hours of approved continuing education annually for both salespersons and brokers.** Of those 12 hours, 4 are mandatory-topic hours selected by LREC each renewal cycle, and the remaining 8 are elective hours from any LREC-approved course catalog.

For the 2025-2026 license year, the commission-mandated topics include updates to Louisiana property disclosure law, changes to the LREC rules and regulations, and a module on Louisiana agency relationships. These mandatory topics shift periodically, so agents cannot recycle last year's completion certificates and assume compliance.

Brokers seeking to maintain their qualifying-broker status face an additional layer. Under LAC 46:LXVII.1509, qualifying brokers must complete a broker-management module as part of their mandatory hours, separate from the general salesperson requirements.

| License Type | Total CE Hours | Mandatory Hours | Elective Hours | Broker-Management Module |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salesperson | 12 | 4 | 8 | Not required |
| Broker (non-qualifying) | 12 | 4 | 8 | Not required |
| Qualifying Broker | 12 | 4 (includes broker module) | 8 | Required |
| Inactive (reactivating) | 12 per missed year | All delinquent mandatory topics | Remaining balance | If applicable |

Agents transferring from another state under a reciprocity agreement should note that Louisiana does not waive its CE requirements simply because you hold an active license elsewhere. The LREC's [official CE page](https://www.lrec.gov/continuing-education) lists approved providers and current mandatory topics for each cycle.

## Deadlines and the Renewal Calendar

**The license renewal deadline in Louisiana aligns with your original issue date, not a universal calendar date.** This is where many agents trip up—there is no single "December 31" cutoff for the entire state. Your license has a unique expiration date, and your 12 hours of CE must be completed before that date each year.

LREC sends renewal notices approximately 60 days before expiration. However, relying on that mailer is risky. The commission's position, stated plainly in their administrative rules, is that failure to receive a notice does not excuse non-compliance.

If your license expires while you have a pending transaction, the legal consequences are immediate. Under La. R.S. 37:1455, performing any act of real estate brokerage without a current active license is a violation that can result in fines up to $2,000 per occurrence, civil liability, and potential deal rescission by the buyer or seller.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Louisiana CE

Mistakes in this area are surprisingly consistent year after year. Here are the errors that generate the most enforcement actions and panicked calls to brokerages.

**First, agents complete courses from non-approved providers.** Louisiana maintains a specific list of approved CE providers, and a real estate course offered in Texas or a generic national webinar does not automatically qualify. The course and the provider must both hold active LREC approval. Agents discover this only when their hours fail to appear in the LREC system, often days before expiration.

Second, agents confuse post-licensing education with continuing education. New licensees in Louisiana must complete 45 hours of post-licensing education within their first year (per LAC 46:LXVII.1503), and those hours are separate from the annual 12-hour CE requirement. Completing post-licensing work does not exempt you from CE during that same period if your renewal date falls within the window. Many first-year agents learn this lesson the hard way—and end up needing 57 hours in a single year.

Third, agents wait until the final week before expiration to enroll. Online courses still require proctored exams, and results take 24-72 hours to process and transmit to LREC. If there is a technology glitch, a failed exam attempt, or a provider backlog, you have no buffer. This is also why keeping your [weekly pipeline review](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) updated with personal compliance deadlines—not just deal milestones—matters.

Fourth, agents fail to retain completion certificates. LREC requires that you maintain proof of CE completion for at least one full renewal cycle. If the electronic transmission from your provider fails and you have no certificate, you are the one who bears the burden of proof.

Fifth, brokers assume LREC is solely responsible for monitoring agent compliance. As discussed below, the regulatory framework places a dual obligation on the qualifying broker.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Under LAC 46:LXVII.1501, the qualifying broker holds supervisory responsibility for every licensee operating under their license. This is not a suggestion—it is a regulatory mandate with teeth. If an agent conducts a transaction while their license is expired due to incomplete CE, the broker faces potential discipline alongside the agent.

Brokers should implement a quarterly audit of every sponsored agent's CE status. The LREC's online license verification tool allows brokers to confirm whether a licensee's status reads "Active" or "Inactive" in real time. Waiting for LREC to notify you of an agent's lapse is not a defensible compliance strategy.

| Broker Responsibility | Regulatory Basis | Frequency Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Verify agent license status | LAC 46:LXVII.1501 | Quarterly minimum |
| Confirm CE completion certificates on file | LAC 46:LXVII.1507 | Annually at renewal |
| Remove inactive agents from MLS and marketing | LREC Rules, MLS bylaws | Immediately upon lapse |
| Report agent termination to LREC | La. R.S. 37:1447 | Within 5 days |
| Maintain supervision records | LAC 46:LXVII.1501 | Ongoing |

**Brokers who discover an agent's lapse mid-transaction face an ugly choice: disclose the issue to all parties or risk a later claim of fraud.** The cleaner path is preventing the situation entirely through proactive auditing. ## Consequences of Non-Compliance

The penalties for practicing without completed CE are not theoretical. LREC's enforcement division actively investigates complaints, and the consequences escalate depending on the severity and duration of the violation.

An agent whose license lapses due to CE non-completion is immediately prohibited from listing property, showing property, negotiating contracts, or collecting compensation. Any commission earned during an inactive period may be subject to disgorgement—meaning you could be forced to return fees already deposited in your account.

Beyond financial penalties, LREC can impose license suspension or revocation under La. R.S. 37:1455. The commission's disciplinary hearings are public record, and a formal action on your license history follows you through every future renewal and any state where you seek reciprocity. The reputational damage alone can dismantle a career built over decades.

For brokers, harboring an agent who practices while inactive constitutes a supervisory failure. LREC can fine the brokerage, suspend the broker's license, or impose conditions on the brokerage's operations. This is precisely why [understanding why checklists beat memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) in compliance management is more than productivity advice—it is risk mitigation.

## How to Verify Your CE Status and Plan Ahead

LREC maintains a public license lookup tool at lrec.gov where any licensee can verify their current status, renewal date, and recorded CE hours. Agents should check this portal at least 90 days before their renewal date to confirm that all completed coursework has been properly transmitted by the provider.

If you discover a discrepancy, contact your CE provider first and request a re-transmission. If the issue persists, file a written inquiry with LREC's education division, referencing your license number and attaching your completion certificate. Do not assume the problem will resolve itself before your deadline.

Planning your CE early in the license year—rather than the final month—eliminates the most common failure mode. Some agents complete all 12 hours in January and spend the remaining 11 months focused entirely on production. That front-loaded approach removes CE from the list of things competing for attention during peak transaction season.
