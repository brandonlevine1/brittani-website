## Understanding Kentucky Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026

Missing a single deadline in a Kentucky residential transaction can unravel a deal, expose an agent to a Kentucky Real Estate Commission (KREC) complaint, or trigger a breach-of-contract claim. Kentucky buyer contingency deadlines 2026 carry the same statutory weight as in prior years, but the enforcement environment has shifted. KREC disciplinary actions related to negligent deadline management increased in recent reporting cycles, and brokers are paying closer attention to compliance audits.

The Kentucky Association of REALTORS (KAR) standard forms govern how most residential deals are structured across the state. KAR Form 205 — the standard Purchase Contract — contains the primary contingency framework that agents must master. Understanding when deadlines start, how they're calculated, and what remedies exist when they expire is not optional knowledge; it is the baseline expectation for licensure.

## The KAR Form Framework and Statutory Authority

**Kentucky's contingency deadlines are contractual, not statutory, but they carry legal force through KRS Chapter 324 and the administrative regulations under 201 KAR Chapter 11.** The Kentucky Real Estate Commission, established under [KRS 324.281](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=35753), has authority to discipline agents for incompetence or negligence in handling transactions, which includes failure to properly manage contractual timelines.

KAR Form 205 is the workhorse contract. It contains blanks where agents fill in negotiated timeframes for the inspection contingency, the financing contingency, and the appraisal contingency. KAR Form 220 (the Inspection Resolution) and KAR Form 215 (the FHA/VA Addendum) also contain deadline-sensitive provisions that interact with the base contract.

The distinction matters: because these are negotiated deadlines rather than legislatively mandated ones, a poorly drafted timeline is the agent's error — not a gap in the law. KREC views agents who leave contingency blanks empty or who insert unrealistic timeframes as potentially violating 201 KAR 11:121, which addresses professional standards of conduct.

## How Calendar Day Counting Works in Kentucky Contracts

Day-count errors are the single most common source of contingency disputes in Kentucky. KAR Form 205 specifies that time periods begin the day after the triggering event — typically mutual acceptance — and run consecutively through calendar days. If a deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or Kentucky state holiday, the deadline extends to the next business day.

This seems simple until agents encounter scenarios involving counter-offers signed late on a Friday, holidays unique to Kentucky (such as state-observed holidays not on the federal calendar), or situations where the "triggering event" for one contingency is the satisfaction of another. The KAR Legal Hotline fields hundreds of these calls annually.

**Agents must document the exact date and time of mutual acceptance with precision, because every downstream deadline derives from that moment.** Using the KAR Form 270 (Notice of Receipt) helps establish the record, but many agents skip this step and rely on email timestamps that later prove ambiguous.

## Consequences of Missed Deadlines: Liability and Deal Collapse

When a buyer fails to act within a contingency window, the consequences depend on the specific contract language. Under KAR Form 205, some contingencies automatically waive upon expiration — meaning the buyer proceeds without that protection. Others require affirmative action by the seller to declare a default.

The inspection contingency under Section 9 of KAR Form 205 is instructive. If the buyer does not deliver a written inspection response (using KAR Form 220) before the deadline expires, the buyer is deemed to have accepted the property in its current condition. The agent who fails to remind the buyer of this deadline has not violated the contract itself, but has likely violated their fiduciary duty under KRS 324.160.

KREC can impose sanctions ranging from formal reprimand to license suspension under KRS 324.160(4). Civil liability is a separate track: a buyer whose agent negligently allowed a contingency to expire without informed consent may pursue damages through the courts. Kentucky's standard of care for real estate professionals, while not codified in a single statute, is established through KREC disciplinary precedent and civil case law interpreting KRS Chapter 324.

For agents concerned about where deals commonly fall apart, the pattern is [remarkably consistent across states](/blog/where-deals-break-down) — and Kentucky is no exception.

## Five Common Mistakes Agents Make With Kentucky Contingency Deadlines

**The first recurring error is leaving contingency timeframes blank on KAR Form 205.** An empty blank does not mean "no contingency" — it creates ambiguity that courts interpret against the drafter. KREC has cited agents for this under the negligence standard of 201 KAR 11:121.

The second mistake is confusing "days from acceptance" with "days from delivery." KAR forms generally trigger deadlines from mutual acceptance, but certain addenda — particularly KAR Form 225 (Sale of Buyer's Property Contingency) — may use different trigger points. Agents who apply the wrong start date miscalculate every subsequent deadline.

Third, agents routinely fail to deliver written notice of contingency satisfaction or waiver. A verbal conversation with the other agent does not constitute performance under the KAR contract framework. Section 17 of KAR Form 205 requires written notice delivered in accordance with the contract's notice provisions.

The fourth error involves the financing contingency and appraisal timelines colliding. When a low appraisal comes in near the financing deadline, agents assume the buyer has additional time to negotiate. Unless the contract provides a separate appraisal contingency window (which KAR Form 205 does through Section 8), the buyer's remedy expires with the financing deadline.

Fifth, agents neglect to use KAR Form 270 (Notice of Receipt) to memorialize when documents were delivered. Without a receipt record, disputes about whether a deadline was met become a credibility contest — the worst possible position for an agent facing a KREC complaint. This problem is [exactly why memory alone fails agents](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) and why systematic tracking matters.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Kentucky brokers carry supervisory liability under KRS 324.160 and 201 KAR 11:105, which require principal brokers to maintain oversight of all transactions conducted under their license. A broker who fails to implement systems for tracking contingency deadlines may face discipline alongside the agent who missed the date.

| Broker Audit Point | What to Check | Risk If Missed |
|---|---|---|
| Mutual acceptance date recorded | Confirmed on file within 24 hours | All deadline calculations unreliable |
| Contingency blanks completed | Every KAR Form 205 reviewed at intake | Ambiguous contracts; KREC citation |
| Written notices filed | KAR Forms 220, 270 in transaction file | No proof of compliance |
| Deadline calendar maintained | Documented system (digital or physical) | Agent negligence imputed to broker |
| Expired contingencies flagged | Broker or TC confirms status before closing | Potential unauthorized practice claims |

**Brokers who rely solely on agents self-reporting deadline compliance are accepting unnecessary risk.** The smarter approach is implementing transaction coordination protocols that independently verify each deadline event. Some Kentucky brokerages assign this function to an in-house coordinator, while others outsource it — but the responsibility remains with the principal broker regardless of delegation.

Under 201 KAR 11:105(2), the principal broker must maintain a system of records that demonstrates supervisory review. This means a broker cannot claim ignorance if an agent's contingency failure results in a client complaint. The Commission has consistently interpreted this regulation to require proactive oversight, not merely reactive discipline after a problem surfaces.

## The Financing Contingency: Kentucky's Most Litigated Deadline

The financing contingency in KAR Form 205, Section 7, requires the buyer to apply for financing within a specified number of days and to notify the seller if financing is denied. The deadline-sensitive element is the buyer's obligation to provide written notice of inability to obtain financing before the contingency expires. Failure to do so can result in the buyer forfeiting earnest money.

Kentucky courts have examined this provision in multiple reported cases and consistently held that the buyer bears the burden of timely notification. The agent's role is to ensure the buyer understands the deadline and to facilitate written communication — but when an agent fails to send the notice on time, the agent becomes a potential defendant in the resulting earnest money dispute.

Lenders frequently delay loan denials or conditional approvals until after the contingency window has passed. Experienced buyer's agents in Kentucky address this by building buffer days into the financing contingency or by requesting extensions in writing before expiration using KAR Form 260 (Amendment to Contract).

## Protecting Your License Under KREC Standards

KREC's disciplinary process begins with a complaint filed under KRS 324.160. The Commission investigates, holds hearings, and can impose penalties including fines up to $5,000 per violation, license suspension, and mandatory education. Deadline-related complaints typically fall under the "incompetence" or "negligence" categories — both grounds for discipline under KRS 324.160(4)(a).

The best defense against a KREC complaint is documentation. Every notice sent, every deadline confirmed, every client communication about contingency status should exist in the transaction file. Agents who track deadlines through [systematic weekly reviews](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) rather than memory alone rarely face these complaints.
