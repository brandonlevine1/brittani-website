## South Carolina Continuing Education Requirements 2026: The Full Breakdown

Every two years, the South Carolina Real Estate Commission (SCREC) puts a hard deadline in front of roughly 45,000 active licensees. If you hold an active real estate license in the state, South Carolina continuing education requirements 2026 apply to you — no exceptions, no grace periods worth relying on, and no ambiguity about what noncompliance costs.

The SCREC operates under Title 40, Chapter 57 of the South Carolina Code of Laws, which grants the Commission authority to set CE standards, approve providers, and discipline licensees who fall short. The renewal cycle runs on a biennial schedule tied to your individual license anniversary date. Missing it does not just mean paperwork headaches — it means your license goes inactive and every deal you touch while lapsed is legally vulnerable.

This post covers what you owe the Commission, how to avoid the most common mistakes agents make, and what brokers-in-charge must enforce within their offices.

## Hour Requirements and Course Structure for 2026

South Carolina mandates 10 total hours of continuing education per renewal cycle for active salespersons and brokers. Those 10 hours break down into two distinct buckets.

The first bucket is the 4-hour mandatory core course. SCREC redesigns this course each cycle to address recent statutory changes, Commission enforcement trends, and updated rules adopted under SC Code Section 40-57-145. You cannot substitute the core course with elective hours, and you cannot carry over core course credit from a previous cycle.

The second bucket is 6 hours of elective courses approved by the Commission. Elective topics range from contract law and property management to fair housing updates and technology ethics. The full list of approved courses and providers is published on the [South Carolina Real Estate Commission website](https://llr.sc.gov/re/).

| Requirement | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory Core Course | 4 | Must be cycle-specific; no carryover |
| Approved Electives | 6 | Any SCREC-approved provider |
| Total Per Cycle | 10 | Due by license anniversary date |
| Carryover Allowed | 0 | No excess hours transfer to next cycle |
| Online Delivery Permitted | Yes | Must be from SCREC-approved provider |

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

**A lapsed license is not a technicality — it is a legal prohibition on practicing real estate.** Under SC Code Section 40-57-150, performing any act that requires a license while your license is inactive or expired constitutes unlicensed practice. The Commission can impose fines up to $1,000 per violation, issue formal reprimands, or suspend or revoke licenses entirely.

The downstream consequences extend beyond Commission discipline. If a buyer or seller discovers their agent practiced on a lapsed license, they may have grounds to rescind the transaction or seek damages. Title companies regularly run license verification checks, and a lapsed license flagged at closing can stall or kill a deal within hours of the scheduled signing.

Brokers-in-charge carry additional exposure. Under SC Regulation 105-10, a broker who allows an agent with an inactive license to conduct real estate activities faces independent disciplinary proceedings. The Commission treats broker oversight failures as a separate violation — not merely a derivative of the agent's mistake.

## The Five Most Common Mistakes Agents Make

**Waiting until the last week of the cycle is the single most frequent cause of lapsed licenses in South Carolina.** Approved course providers have capacity limits, and online platforms occasionally experience outages during peak renewal periods. Agents who procrastinate lose their margin for error.

The second mistake is completing courses from providers not currently approved by SCREC. A course approved in a prior cycle or approved in another state does not automatically count. The Commission maintains a current provider list, and it is the agent's responsibility — not the provider's — to verify approval status before enrollment.

Third, many agents confuse post-license education with continuing education. New licensees in South Carolina must complete 30 hours of post-license education within the first renewal period under SC Code Section 40-57-115. Those 30 hours replace the standard 10-hour CE requirement only for the first cycle. Agents entering their second cycle sometimes believe they are still on the post-license track and miss their CE entirely.

Fourth, agents assume their broker or team admin will handle CE tracking for them. While brokers have supervisory obligations, the license belongs to the individual. SCREC holds the licensee personally responsible for timely completion.

Fifth, some agents complete the correct number of hours but fail to ensure their provider transmitted completion records to the Commission. A course completed but not reported is, from the Commission's perspective, a course not completed. Agents should verify their CE status through the SCREC online portal at least 30 days before their renewal date.

## What Brokers-in-Charge Must Audit and Enforce

SC Regulation 105-10 places affirmative duties on brokers-in-charge (BIC) that go beyond general mentoring. A BIC must maintain current records of every licensee practicing under their supervision, including CE compliance status. The Commission interprets this as requiring proactive verification — not passive reliance on agents self-reporting.

**Brokers should run a quarterly audit of all associated licensees' renewal dates and CE completion status.** The SCREC online portal allows brokers to look up individual licensees, and many BIC offices build a spreadsheet tracking each agent's anniversary date, required hours, and completion confirmations. Failing to catch a lapsed agent before they list a property or submit an offer creates liability for the brokerage.

When the Commission investigates a complaint involving an unlicensed agent, one of its first questions is whether the BIC had a system in place to monitor compliance. Documenting your audit process — even if simple — is the difference between a warning and a formal proceeding. Brokers who treat compliance as a "set it and forget it" exercise are the ones most likely to face Commission scrutiny. The regulatory expectation is ongoing supervision, not annual check-ins.

## Forms and Filings You Should Know

South Carolina agents encounter several Commission forms during the renewal process. Form REC 2.02 is the standard renewal application, and agents filing to reactivate a lapsed license use Form REC 2.03. Brokers transferring an agent's license use Form REC 2.08, which includes a certification that the licensee is in good standing — meaning CE obligations are current.

If your license lapses for more than one year but fewer than five years, reinstatement under SC Code Section 40-57-150(C) requires completion of all delinquent CE, payment of back renewal fees, and a reinstatement fee set by the Commission. Beyond five years, you must retake the state exam. These are not negotiable timelines, and the Commission rarely grants exceptions.

Agents moving between brokerages mid-cycle should confirm that their CE records transferred cleanly. A brokerage change does not reset your renewal clock or alter your CE obligation. The anniversary date stays fixed to your original license issuance date.

## Delivery Formats and Approved Providers

South Carolina approves both classroom and online delivery for all 10 hours of CE. The pandemic-era expansion of online options has persisted, and most agents now complete the majority of their hours through distance learning. However, SCREC requires that online providers use proctoring or verification measures — a course that allows you to click through without identity checks may not meet Commission standards.

The Commission publishes its approved provider list annually and updates it throughout the year. Major providers operating in South Carolina include the SC Association of Realtors, several community colleges, and national CE platforms with state-specific approval. Before enrolling, confirm the provider's SCREC approval number and verify the course is designated for the current renewal cycle.

## How This Connects to Transaction Compliance

CE is not just about keeping your license active. The mandatory core course frequently covers updates to disclosure requirements, agency law, and contract forms that directly affect active transactions. Agents who treat CE as a box-checking exercise miss substantive legal updates that could expose them to liability on the next deal they write.

South Carolina's residential property condition disclosure (Form 4341) and the agency disclosure requirements under SC Code Section 40-57-370 are regularly addressed in core course updates. Missing these updates means you might be using outdated practices in your day-to-day business without realizing it. The Commission does not accept "I didn't know about the change" as a defense in disciplinary proceedings.
