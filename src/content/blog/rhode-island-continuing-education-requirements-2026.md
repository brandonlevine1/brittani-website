## Understanding Rhode Island Continuing Education Requirements 2026

If you hold an active real estate license in Rhode Island, the clock is already ticking on your next renewal cycle. The **Rhode Island continuing education requirements 2026** mandate a total of 24 credit hours of approved coursework within each two-year licensing period—no exceptions and no grace periods. Missing even a single hour means your license lapses and every pending deal you touch becomes a liability minefield.

The Rhode Island Department of Business Regulation (DBR), specifically the Real Estate Section under the Division of Commercial Licensing and Regulation, administers these requirements. The governing statute is Rhode Island General Laws Title 5, Chapter 20.5, which grants the DBR authority to set continuing education standards and approve course providers. Agents renewing in 2026 must meet the same 24-hour threshold that has been in place since the last regulatory update, but the composition of those hours has shifted slightly to reflect new fair housing enforcement priorities and agency disclosure changes.

## The Credit Hour Breakdown: What Counts and What Doesn't

**Not all 24 hours are interchangeable.** Rhode Island separates the requirement into two categories: a mandatory 3-hour core course and 21 hours of elective coursework from approved providers. The core course changes content each renewal cycle and typically addresses legislative updates, ethics, and fair housing enforcement actions relevant to Rhode Island practitioners.

Elective hours can cover topics ranging from contracts and property management to environmental hazards and technology in real estate. However, the DBR publishes an approved course list each year, and only courses appearing on that list generate valid credit. Taking a NAR designation course or an out-of-state CE class does not automatically count unless the DBR has specifically approved it for Rhode Island credit.

| Requirement Category | Hours Required | Notes |
|---------------------|---------------|-------|
| Mandatory Core Course | 3 hours | Covers legislative updates, ethics, fair housing |
| Elective Coursework | 21 hours | Must be DBR-approved; online or in-person |
| Total Per Renewal Cycle | 24 hours | Two-year cycle; no carryover of excess hours |
| Broker Additional Requirement | 0 extra hours | Same 24-hour total, but different core content |

One thing that trips up agents who hold licenses in multiple states: Rhode Island does not allow hour-for-hour reciprocity on CE credits. If you completed 24 hours in Massachusetts or Connecticut, you still owe Rhode Island its own 24 hours from its own approved providers. The state's [license reciprocity rules](/blog/wisconsin-license-reciprocity-portability-2026) differ from the CE framework entirely.

## Renewal Deadlines and the Forms You Need

Rhode Island real estate licenses expire on a staggered basis depending on when you were first licensed. Your specific expiration date appears on your license and in the DBR's online licensing portal. Renewal applications must be submitted using the **RE-3 form** (Application for License Renewal) along with proof of CE completion and the required fee.

The renewal fee for salespersons is currently $100, while brokers pay $150. Late renewals incur an additional penalty fee of $50 if submitted within 30 days of expiration. After 30 days, the license is classified as lapsed rather than expired, which triggers a more complex reinstatement process.

Agents must also submit a **CE Certification Form** (provided by approved course providers upon completion) that verifies the date, course title, provider name, and approval number for each course taken. The DBR does not accept transcripts or certificates that lack the Rhode Island-specific approval number. This is one of the most common reasons renewals get rejected.

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

**The consequences of practicing on a lapsed license extend far beyond a fine.** Under R.I. Gen. Laws Section 5-20.5-12, engaging in real estate activity without a valid license is a misdemeanor carrying potential fines of up to $1,000 per violation. Each transaction conducted while unlicensed can be treated as a separate violation.

From a civil liability standpoint, clients who discover they were represented by an unlicensed agent can seek rescission of the purchase agreement. Courts in Rhode Island have historically sided with buyers and sellers seeking to void contracts where the agent's license was not in good standing at the time of the transaction. Your brokerage's errors and omissions insurance may also deny coverage for acts committed while your license was lapsed.

The DBR can also impose disciplinary action including formal reprimand, additional CE requirements, probation, or permanent revocation. Brokers who knowingly allow unlicensed agents to conduct business face their own disciplinary exposure under Section 5-20.5-18, which holds the broker responsible for supervising licensee compliance.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Rhode Island CE

Years of processing renewals reveal the same errors cycling through the DBR's rejection queue. Here are the five most frequent mistakes Rhode Island agents make when approaching their continuing education obligations.

**First, agents wait until the final month of their renewal period to start coursework.** Approved providers sometimes fill classes or experience website outages during peak renewal months. Starting 90 days before expiration gives you a buffer if a provider's system fails to report your hours promptly. The DBR's processing time for verifying CE transcripts can run two to three weeks during high-volume periods.

Second, agents confuse NAR ethics courses with Rhode Island's mandatory core course. The NAR Code of Ethics training satisfies NAR membership requirements but does not substitute for Rhode Island's state-specific 3-hour core course. You need both if you want to maintain your NAR membership and your state license.

Third, agents fail to verify that their chosen provider holds current DBR approval. Providers must renew their own approval annually, and some fall off the approved list mid-year. Taking a course from a lapsed provider means those hours will not count, and you will not discover the problem until your renewal application is rejected.

Fourth, agents lose track of their renewal date entirely. Rhode Island does send reminder notices, but they go to the address on file with the DBR. If you moved and forgot to update your address using the **RE-7 form** (Change of Address Notification), you may never receive the reminder. Keeping your contact information current is a separate regulatory obligation under Section 5-20.5-7.

Fifth, agents assume excess hours carry forward to the next cycle. Rhode Island does not permit rollover credits. If you completed 30 hours this cycle, only 24 count—and the extra 6 disappear when your new cycle begins.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers bear direct supervisory liability for agents operating under their license. Under R.I. Gen. Laws Section 5-20.5-18, the principal broker must maintain records confirming that every affiliated licensee holds a current, active license. This is not optional housekeeping—it is a statutory mandate with enforcement teeth.

| Broker Audit Item | Frequency | Source Document |
|-------------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Agent license status verification | Quarterly minimum | DBR online portal lookup |
| CE completion confirmation | 60 days before each agent's renewal | CE Certification Form from provider |
| Address and contact info accuracy | Annually | RE-7 form confirmation |
| Disciplinary action check | Quarterly | DBR public disciplinary records |
| E&O insurance coverage verification | At renewal | Carrier confirmation letter |

**Brokers should build a tracking calendar that flags each agent's renewal date at least 90 days in advance.** A simple spreadsheet is better than nothing, but the volume of dates and forms across a mid-size brokerage demands a more systematic approach. When even one agent's license lapses, every transaction they touched during the lapsed period becomes a potential liability for the brokerage.

The Rhode Island Real Estate Commission has shown increased willingness to audit brokerages for compliance records. During a 2024 sweep, the Commission identified multiple brokerages that could not produce documentation proving their agents' CE was current. Fines in those cases ranged from $500 to $2,500 per agent depending on the duration of the lapse. You can review the [DBR's full regulatory framework](https://dbr.ri.gov/divisions/commercial-licensing/real-estate) for current enforcement guidance.

## Planning Your 2026 CE Strategy

Rhode Island's 24-hour requirement breaks down to roughly one hour of coursework per month over a two-year cycle. Agents who batch their CE into a single marathon weekend often retain less information and report lower satisfaction with the process. Spacing courses across the cycle also reduces the risk of a last-minute scramble if personal emergencies or market demands eat into your schedule.

Look for providers that offer modular courses in 3-hour blocks, which align naturally with Rhode Island's core course length. Many agents pair their mandatory core course with one elective block per quarter, finishing well ahead of their deadline. This approach also lets you choose electives that directly support your current business—negotiation skills if you are expanding into luxury, or environmental hazard courses if you work in older Providence neighborhoods with lead paint concerns.

The DBR updates its approved provider list on a rolling basis, and new providers appear throughout the year. Check the list at least twice during your renewal cycle to ensure new options have not displaced your planned provider, and confirm that your chosen courses still carry active approval numbers before you register.
