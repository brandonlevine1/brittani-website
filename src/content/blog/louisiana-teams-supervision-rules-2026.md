The Louisiana Real Estate Commission has steadily tightened its grip on team operations over the past two years, and the Louisiana teams & supervision rules 2026 framework now carries enforcement teeth that most agents have never seen before. If you run a team, belong to one, or supervise licensees as a broker, the regulatory environment demands more documentation, clearer advertising disclosures, and direct broker oversight than at any point in the last decade. Ignoring these rules does not just invite fines — it threatens active deals and license status.

## How Louisiana Defines a Real Estate Team

The LREC distinguishes teams from brokerages under Louisiana Administrative Code Title 46, Part LXVII, Chapter 55. A team is a group of two or more licensees affiliated with the same broker who market collectively under a name other than the brokerage's registered name. This definition matters because it triggers specific advertising, supervision, and registration requirements that solo agents never face.

**Teams must register their team name with LREC using the Team Name Registration Form before any public-facing marketing goes live.** The team name cannot contain words like "realty," "real estate," "brokerage," or "company" unless those words also appear in the qualifying broker's registered name. Violation of this rule is treated the same as unlicensed activity advertising under La. R.S. 37:1455.

The distinction between a team and a brokerage is not cosmetic. A team cannot hold escrow funds, cannot independently sponsor licensees, and cannot operate without the qualifying broker's direct supervisory chain. Every transaction file produced by the team ultimately belongs to — and must be stored by — the broker's office.

## Broker Supervision Obligations Under La. R.S. 37:1455

Louisiana statute places the broker at the center of all team activity. La. R.S. 37:1455(A) states that a qualifying broker is responsible for the real estate activities of every licensee associated with the brokerage, including those operating as part of a registered team. This is not a loose suggestion — the Commission has used this statute to fine brokers for team members' advertising errors, missed disclosure deadlines, and unauthorized dual agency.

Under LAC 46:LXVII.3101, the broker must maintain a written policy manual that addresses team operations, advertising approval workflows, and transaction file review timelines. The 2025 LREC rule update expanded this to require that brokers document supervisory reviews at least once per transaction milestone — listing execution, offer acceptance, and pre-closing — for any file handled by a team.

Brokers who rely on [weekly pipeline reviews](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) already have a framework that satisfies the milestone review requirement. The key is documenting those reviews in writing, whether through a signed checklist or an electronic system that timestamps broker access to each file.

## Advertising and Disclosure Rules for Louisiana Teams

LREC advertising rules under LAC 46:LXVII.5505 require every team advertisement — print, digital, social media, signage — to prominently display the qualifying broker's name or the brokerage's registered trade name. The team name may appear, but it cannot be more prominent than the brokerage name in font size, placement, or visual weight.

| Advertising Element | Requirement | Common Violation |
|---|---|---|
| Team name | Must be registered with LREC | Using unregistered name on social media |
| Broker/brokerage name | Must appear on every ad, no smaller than team name | Broker name in fine print below team logo |
| Individual agent name | Must include license status (salesperson or broker) | Omitting "Licensed Real Estate Agent" on business cards |
| Phone number | Must not be a team-only number without broker attribution | Team voicemail with no brokerage mention |
| Website/URL | Must display brokerage name on landing page | Team landing page with zero broker identification |

Social media posts count as advertising under LREC's interpretation. An Instagram story promoting a listing under the team name without the brokerage identifier is a violation — and the Commission has issued written warnings for exactly this in 2025 enforcement actions.

**Agents commonly assume that a brokerage name in their Instagram bio satisfies the per-post requirement, but LREC has clarified that each individual post or story must be independently compliant.** This standard mirrors what [Wisconsin implemented for its teams](/blog/wisconsin-teams-supervision-rules-2026), though Louisiana's enforcement fines are steeper.

## Forms and Documentation Every Louisiana Team Must Use

Louisiana does not prescribe a single "team disclosure form," but several mandatory forms interact with team operations. The Property Disclosure Document (PDD), required under La. R.S. 9:3198, must identify the licensee handling the transaction — and that licensee's team affiliation must not obscure the brokerage relationship.

The Dual Agency Disclosure (Form LREC 301) becomes particularly dangerous in team settings. If one team member represents the buyer and another represents the seller in the same transaction, the broker must execute a dual agency disclosure under La. R.S. 37:1455.2. Many teams misunderstand this: designated agency within a team still constitutes dual agency at the brokerage level in Louisiana.

The Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement (Form LREC 200) and the Exclusive Right to Sell Agreement (Form LREC 100) must both name the brokerage — not the team — as the party to the agreement. Teams that insert their team name as the contracting party risk rendering the agreement voidable, which leaves commission claims unenforceable.

> "A listing agreement or buyer agency agreement that names a team rather than the licensed brokerage as the contracting entity is not valid under Louisiana law." — LREC Advisory Opinion 2024-03

## Common Mistakes That Trigger LREC Enforcement

Five errors appear repeatedly in LREC disciplinary actions involving teams. Understanding them is cheaper than paying the fines.

**First, teams frequently fail to register their team name before going to market.** The registration process through the [LREC online portal](https://www.lrec.gov) takes less than a week, but agents who form a team and immediately print yard signs skip this step. Fines start at $500 per violation occurrence.

Second, team leaders often conduct independent broker-like activities — holding earnest money, signing listing agreements in the team's name, or sponsoring new licensees. None of these are permitted. The qualifying broker must perform or explicitly authorize each function, and authorization must be documented.

Third, teams neglect internal agency disclosures when representing both sides of a transaction. Even when two different team members handle buyer and seller respectively, LREC treats this as a brokerage-level dual agency requiring written consent from all parties using Form LREC 301. Failure to disclose gives the aggrieved party grounds to void the transaction.

Fourth, advertising non-compliance remains the single most common citation. Teams that build brand identity around their team name and minimize the brokerage name in marketing materials face LAC 46:LXVII.5505 violations carrying fines of up to $2,000 per instance.

Fifth, missing the requirement to include license status in advertising. Every licensee's ad must identify whether they hold a salesperson license or a broker license. "Realtor" or "agent" alone does not satisfy this requirement.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers carry vicarious liability for team violations under La. R.S. 37:1455, so passive oversight is a regulatory and financial risk. A quarterly audit framework should address the following areas with documented findings.

| Audit Area | What to Check | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Team name registration | Confirm active registration with LREC | Annually or at team formation |
| Advertising compliance | Review social media, signage, print ads for brokerage name prominence | Quarterly |
| Listing/buyer agreements | Verify brokerage (not team) is named as contracting party | Every transaction |
| Dual agency disclosures | Confirm Form LREC 301 executed when team members represent both sides | Every applicable transaction |
| Transaction file completeness | Milestone documentation per LAC 46:LXVII.3101 | Per-transaction at three milestones |
| Earnest money handling | Confirm team members are not holding client funds | Monthly |

Brokers should maintain a written audit log that identifies the reviewer, date, findings, and any corrective actions taken. LREC auditors have accepted electronic audit trails from transaction management platforms as compliant documentation, provided those systems produce timestamped records.

The days of a broker simply "being available" for questions are over. The regulatory expectation in 2026 is active, documented, recurring supervision — and brokers who cannot produce evidence of that supervision during an LREC audit face personal liability regardless of whether the team member's violation was intentional.

## Penalties and Liability When Teams Break the Rules

LREC's disciplinary authority under La. R.S. 37:1456 allows the Commission to impose fines, suspend licenses, revoke licenses, or require additional education. For team-related violations, the enforcement pattern in recent years shows escalating severity.

First-time advertising violations typically result in a written warning or a fine between $500 and $1,000. Repeat offenders or those who ignore initial warnings face fines up to $2,000 per occurrence and potential license suspension. Unauthorized brokerage activities — like a team leader holding escrow — can trigger immediate license suspension pending a hearing.

Beyond LREC action, civil liability compounds the problem. A buyer who discovers that their "team's" listing agreement was legally void because it named the team rather than the brokerage may have grounds to rescind the transaction or pursue damages. The same applies to undisclosed dual agency situations where Form LREC 301 was never presented. These scenarios do not just kill one deal — they generate complaints that trigger Commission investigations into every open file the team touches.

## How to Build a Compliant Team Structure in 2026

Start with the LREC Team Name Registration. Confirm the name does not contain restricted terms. Establish a written team operating agreement that the qualifying broker signs, outlining advertising approval chains, transaction file submission timelines, and dual agency procedures.

Build advertising templates that lock the brokerage name into a non-editable position at legally compliant size and prominence. Review every team member's social media profiles quarterly. Document that review.
