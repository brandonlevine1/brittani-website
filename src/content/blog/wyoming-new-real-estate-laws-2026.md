## Wyoming New Real Estate Laws 2026: The Statutory Changes That Matter Most

The Wyoming Real Estate Commission (WREC) has been busy. A package of legislative updates and rule revisions that took effect on July 1, 2026, has reshaped the compliance obligations of every licensee in the state, and the enforcement posture is noticeably sharper than in prior cycles. If you hold a Wyoming license and have not reviewed the Wyoming new real estate laws 2026 changes line by line, you are already behind.

These updates touch buyer representation agreements, seller disclosures, earnest money handling, wire fraud prevention, and dual agency documentation. Several of the changes mirror post-NAR settlement shifts seen in other states, but Wyoming added its own statutory teeth. The consequences for noncompliance range from deal rescission to license suspension, and the WREC has publicly stated it intends to audit brokerages starting in Q4 2026.

This article breaks down the specific statutes, the revised forms, the most common mistakes agents are already making, and what managing brokers must do before those audits begin.

## Buyer-Broker Agreement Mandate Under W.S. 33-28-303(d)

**Wyoming now requires a signed, written buyer-broker agreement before an agent shows any property to a prospective buyer.** This is codified in Wyoming Statute 33-28-303(d), which was amended during the 2026 legislative session and signed by the governor on March 14, 2026. The statute mirrors but goes further than the NAR settlement terms by imposing state-level penalties rather than relying solely on MLS-level enforcement.

The WREC released an updated model form, WREC Form 200-BBA, in May 2026. This form must specify the duration of the agreement, the compensation structure (including whether the buyer or seller pays and the exact dollar amount or percentage), and the scope of services the agent will provide. Open-ended duration clauses are explicitly prohibited; the maximum term is 180 days unless the buyer provides a separate written extension.

Agents who show property without a signed WREC Form 200-BBA face disciplinary action that can include a formal reprimand, fines of up to $2,500 per occurrence, or license suspension. The buyer also gains the right to void any resulting purchase agreement within 10 days of discovering the omission. If you handle buyer representation, this is the single most consequential change in the 2026 cycle—and the one most likely to trip up agents accustomed to informal touring arrangements. ## Seller Disclosure Overhaul: New Environmental Section on WREC Form 100-SD

The [Wyoming seller disclosure form](https://realestate.wyo.gov) has been expanded. WREC Form 100-SD now includes a mandatory Section 12 covering environmental hazards beyond the federal lead-based paint requirements. Sellers must disclose known radon test results, prior methane mitigation, proximity to active or abandoned well sites, and any history of PFAS contamination on the property.

Wyoming's energy-producing economy makes these additions particularly relevant. The new section does not require sellers to conduct testing—only to disclose what they already know. But agents who fail to present the updated form, or who allow sellers to submit the pre-2026 version, bear liability under W.S. 33-28-306. The statute makes the listing agent responsible for ensuring the correct form version is used.

**Penalties for using the outdated form include fines up to $5,000 and potential rescission of the sale if the buyer can show they would have acted differently with proper disclosure.** This is not hypothetical. WREC's spring 2026 enforcement bulletin cited three pending cases involving outdated disclosure forms from late 2025 transactions. ## Earnest Money Timeline Tightened to Two Business Days

The revised WREC Rule Chapter 3, Section 8 shortened the earnest money deposit deadline from three business days to two business days after mutual acceptance. This applies to all residential transactions where earnest money is part of the contract terms.

The rule change also clarified that "business days" excludes state-recognized holidays and weekends, but does not exclude days when a brokerage is voluntarily closed. If your office shuts down on Fridays, that Friday still counts. Agents must deliver the earnest money check or initiate the wire transfer within the two-day window, and the receiving escrow entity must confirm receipt.

Here is how the old and new timelines compare:

| Requirement | Pre-July 2026 | Post-July 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit deadline after mutual acceptance | 3 business days | 2 business days |
| Accepted delivery methods | Check, wire, cashier's check | Check, wire, cashier's check, ACH |
| Broker trust account holding period | Until closing or termination | Until closing or termination |
| Penalty for late deposit | Informal warning, possible complaint | Fine up to $1,000 + buyer right to void |
| Holiday exclusion | State holidays only | State holidays only |

Late deposits now trigger an automatic right for the opposing party to void the contract without forfeiture. Agents who [miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) on earnest money delivery are handing the other side a free exit ramp.

## Wire Fraud Prevention: Mandatory Broker Protocols

Wyoming joined the growing list of states codifying wire fraud prevention at the regulatory level. WREC Rule Chapter 5, Section 3, effective July 1, 2026, requires every brokerage to maintain a written wire fraud prevention policy and to train all licensees on that policy annually.

The rule mandates that agents verbally verify wire instructions with the title company or closing agent via a known phone number—not a number provided in an email—before transmitting any instructions to clients. Agents must also include a wire fraud warning on all written communications that contain financial details. WREC provided model warning language in its June 2026 guidance bulletin, and the commission expects agents to use it verbatim or adopt substantially similar language.

**Brokerages that cannot produce a written wire fraud policy during a WREC audit face a $3,000 fine and a mandatory corrective action plan.** Individual agents who forward unverified wire instructions can be held personally liable for resulting losses, a standard that aligns with the WREC's expanded interpretation of the fiduciary duty of care under W.S. 33-28-301. ## Dual Agency Documentation Gets More Specific

Wyoming continues to permit dual agency, but the 2026 amendments to W.S. 33-28-304 impose stricter documentation requirements. The updated WREC Form 150-DA must now be signed by both buyer and seller before the agent drafts or presents any offer in a dual agency scenario. Previously, the form could be signed at any point before closing.

The form itself has been revised to include a plain-language explanation of what the dual agent cannot do—specifically, that the agent cannot advise either party on price negotiation, cannot share one party's motivation or financial position with the other, and cannot recommend whether to accept or reject an offer. These prohibitions existed in statute, but the prior form did not spell them out.

| Dual Agency Requirement | Pre-2026 | 2026 Forward |
|---|---|---|
| Timing of signed consent | Before closing | Before offer drafted or presented |
| Form version | WREC Form 150 (2019) | WREC Form 150-DA (2026) |
| Plain-language prohibitions listed | No | Yes |
| Both parties must initial each prohibition | No | Yes |
| Penalty for noncompliance | Reprimand or fine | Fine up to $2,500 + voidable transaction |

Agents who represent both sides without the updated 150-DA risk the transaction being voided entirely, plus personal disciplinary action. This is one area where [checklists genuinely outperform memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory).

## Common Mistakes Agents Are Already Making

Five months into the new rules, WREC enforcement staff have already flagged recurring errors. Here are the most frequent ones.

**First, agents are using the old WREC Form 100-SD without Section 12.** Many brokerages pre-loaded disclosure packets at the start of the year before the July effective date and never swapped out the forms. Every listing taken after July 1, 2026, must use the current version. Using a stale form is not a technicality—it is a violation with dollar-figure consequences.

Second, buyer agents are obtaining verbal agreement to the buyer-broker terms but not getting the written WREC Form 200-BBA signed before the first showing. A handshake or text message does not satisfy W.S. 33-28-303(d). The statute explicitly requires a written instrument bearing the buyer's signature.

Third, agents are still calculating earnest money deadlines using the old three-day window. Two business days is tight, especially on transactions that reach mutual acceptance late on a Thursday. Calendar math errors are turning into contract termination opportunities for unhappy buyers.

Fourth, dual agency forms are being signed at the wrong time. Agents wait until the offer is already written, or worse, already presented, before pulling out the 150-DA. The statute says "before." Not "contemporaneously with." Not "shortly after."

Fifth, wire fraud warnings are being omitted from email signatures and transaction correspondence. Some agents added the warning to their initial listing or buyer communications but strip it from later emails. The WREC rule requires the warning on all written communications containing financial details, not just the first one.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce Now

Managing brokers carry supervisory liability under W.S. 33-28-201, and the 2026 changes expand what WREC expects them to monitor. Here is what should be on every broker's audit checklist before the anticipated Q4 2026 compliance reviews.

Verify that every active listing file contains the current WREC Form 100-SD with a completed Section 12. Pull a random sample of at least 20 percent of your files and check the form date. If you find old versions, correct them immediately and document the correction in your file notes.

Confirm that every buyer-side file includes a signed WREC Form 200-BBA dated on or before the first showing date. Cross-reference the showing date from MLS access logs or lockbox records. If the showing predates the agreement, you have a compliance gap that needs a remediation plan.

Review your brokerage's written wire fraud prevention policy. It must exist as a standalone document, not just a line item in your general office manual. Make sure every licensee has signed an acknowledgment that they received and read the policy. Keep those acknowledgments in a central compliance file.

Audit dual agency transactions for properly timed WREC Form 150-DA execution. The form must be signed before the offer is drafted. If you find files where the form was signed on the same date as the offer or later, flag them and consult your brokerage attorney.

Finally, confirm that all agents have completed the wire fraud training mandated by WREC Rule Chapter 5. The training must be documented with a date, the trainer's name, and the topics covered. A brokerage that cannot produce this documentation during an audit faces the $3,000 fine regardless of whether any wire fraud incident actually occurred.
