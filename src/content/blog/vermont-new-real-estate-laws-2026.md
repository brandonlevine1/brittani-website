## Vermont New Real Estate Laws 2026: The Statutory Changes That Matter Most

The Vermont legislature and the Vermont Real Estate Commission (VREC) have been unusually active this cycle. Multiple bills signed into law during the 2025-2026 session take effect at staggered dates, and **agents who miss the deadlines face real consequences — not just sternly worded letters.** If you hold a Vermont license, the phrase "Vermont new real estate laws 2026" should already be bookmarked in your browser.

This article breaks down the specific statutes, updated forms, compliance traps, and broker-level audit requirements you need to act on before December 31, 2026. Everything below is based on enacted legislation and VREC rulemaking as of September 2026.

## Mandatory Buyer Agency Agreements: 26 V.S.A. Section 2297

Vermont's biggest procedural shift mirrors a national trend, but the state's implementation has its own quirks. Starting July 1, 2026, every licensee representing a buyer must execute a written buyer agency agreement before engaging in "substantive real estate services," a term the statute defines as anything beyond casual conversation about market conditions. The required form is VRC Form 700-BA, released by the Vermont Real Estate Commission in May 2026.

**The form must specify the duration of the agreement, the scope of services, and the compensation structure — including whether the buyer's agent fee is negotiable.** A blank or "TBD" entry in the compensation field does not satisfy the statute. VREC guidance bulletin 2026-03 explicitly states that leaving compensation open-ended is a violation.

Agents who previously operated under informal verbal understandings are now exposed. Showing a property without a signed 700-BA in the file gives the buyer grounds to void the agreement, and VREC can initiate disciplinary proceedings that range from a formal reprimand to license suspension. The [buyer agency rules in Wisconsin](/blog/wisconsin-buyer-agency-agreement-requirements-2026) followed a similar trajectory, and early enforcement there offers a preview of how seriously commissions treat this.

## Updated Disclosure and Commission Transparency Forms

Vermont has revised three core transaction forms effective for any purchase agreement executed on or after August 1, 2026. The changes are not cosmetic — field requirements have changed, and using outdated versions will flag your file.

| Form Number | Form Name | Key 2026 Change | Effective Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| VRC Form 400-CD | Commission Disclosure | Must now itemize all parties paying any portion of agent compensation, including seller concessions applied to buyer-agent fees | August 1, 2026 |
| VRC Form 610-LP | Lead Paint Disclosure | Expanded acknowledgment section requiring buyer initials on each hazard category, not just a single signature block | July 1, 2026 |
| VRC Form 500-SD | Seller Property Disclosure | New mandatory fields for known flooding history, short-term rental permit status, and Act 250 land use permit history | August 1, 2026 |
| VRC Form 700-BA | Buyer Agency Agreement | Entirely new form (see Section 2297 above) | July 1, 2026 |

The commission disclosure form (400-CD) deserves special attention. Under the amended 26 V.S.A. Section 2295(b), the listing broker must ensure the 400-CD is signed before the purchase and sale agreement is fully executed. Agents on both sides of the transaction should treat this as a gating document — no signed 400-CD, no binding deal.

The seller disclosure changes on Form 500-SD reflect Vermont's increasing focus on environmental and land use transparency. Act 250, Vermont's landmark land use and development law, now generates its own disclosure line item. If the property has ever been subject to an Act 250 permit — even a historical one that was closed — the seller must disclose it. Agents who work in rural markets with older development histories should not assume their sellers know the answer. Ask, document, and attach.

## Earnest Money Deposit Timeline: What Changed and Why It Matters

**Vermont shortened the earnest money deposit window from five business days to three business days after the binding agreement date.** This change, codified in the amended 26 V.S.A. Section 2296, took effect June 1, 2026, and it has already caught agents off guard.

The statute requires that the deposit be in the escrow account — not merely mailed or wire-initiated — within three business days. The definition of "business day" excludes state-recognized holidays and weekends, but the clock starts the day after the agreement becomes binding. Late deposits give the non-depositing party a contractual right to void the agreement, and VREC treats repeated late deposits as evidence of incompetent practice.

Wire transfers typically settle same-day, but personal checks deposited at a local credit union can take longer to clear. Agents should advise buyers to use certified funds or wire transfers to avoid accidental violations. ## Common Mistakes Agents Are Already Making Under the 2026 Rules

Five months into the first wave of changes, VREC complaint data and brokerage audit reports are revealing clear patterns. These are not theoretical risks — they are happening in active transactions right now.

**First, agents are using outdated form versions.** The most frequent error is pulling a pre-2026 Form 500-SD from a personal template folder instead of downloading the current version from the VREC website. The old form lacks the Act 250 and short-term rental fields, which means the disclosure is legally incomplete even if every existing field is filled out correctly.

Second, agents are executing buyer agency agreements after showing property, not before. The statute says "before substantive real estate services." An agent who shows three houses and then asks the buyer to sign the 700-BA at the fourth has already violated the law for the first three showings. VREC bulletin 2026-03 makes no exception for "getting to know the client" periods.

Third, agents are failing to initial the new lead paint acknowledgment boxes on Form 610-LP. The updated form requires buyer initials on five separate hazard categories. A single missing initial makes the disclosure defective, which reopens federal liability under 42 U.S.C. Section 4852d. The [lead paint disclosure requirements in Wisconsin](/blog/wisconsin-lead-paint-disclosure-2026) imposed a similar multi-initial format, and agents there reported the same early compliance stumble.

Fourth, some listing agents are treating the 400-CD commission disclosure as optional when the buyer is unrepresented. It is not. The amended statute requires the form in every transaction, regardless of whether a cooperating broker is involved. An unrepresented buyer still must receive and sign the 400-CD before the purchase agreement is executed.

Fifth, agents are miscalculating the three-business-day earnest money window by counting the agreement date as day one. Day one is the day after the binding agreement. Starting the count wrong means you think you have an extra day, which is exactly how deposits land late.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers carry supervisory liability under 26 V.S.A. Section 2255, and VREC has signaled through its 2026 enforcement priorities memo that it intends to hold brokers accountable for systemic failures — not just isolated agent errors. If your brokerage has more than two agents, you should treat the following as non-negotiable audit items.

**Audit every active file for the presence of the current-version VRC Form 700-BA, Form 400-CD, and Form 500-SD.** A quarterly file review is the minimum defensible standard. VREC's 2024 enforcement actions included two brokerages fined for "pattern and practice" failures where more than 30 percent of reviewed files were missing a required disclosure. The fines ranged from $1,000 to $5,000 per violation.

| Broker Audit Item | What to Check | Risk if Missing |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer agency agreement (700-BA) | Signed before first showing; compensation field completed | Commission forfeiture; agent discipline; broker supervisory liability |
| Commission disclosure (400-CD) | Signed before P&S execution; all compensation sources itemized | Deal rescission risk; VREC fine up to $5,000 |
| Seller disclosure (500-SD) | Current version; Act 250 and STR fields completed | Buyer rescission right; agent and broker liability |
