## Understanding West Virginia Teams & Supervision Rules 2026

The **West Virginia teams & supervision rules 2026** framework creates specific obligations for agents forming teams and the brokers who oversee them. If you operate in a team structure—or plan to build one—the West Virginia Real Estate Commission (WVREC) holds both you and your designated broker accountable under WV Code Chapter 30, Article 40. Ignorance of these standards has cost agents their licenses, voided transactions, and triggered five-figure fines.

West Virginia does not issue a separate "team license." Instead, every person performing licensed activities must hold an individual license affiliated with a single brokerage. The team itself functions as a marketing identity, not a legal entity with independent licensing authority. This distinction trips up agents who migrate from states with more permissive team structures.

The WVREC operates under Legislative Rule [Title 174, Series 1](https://wvrec.wv.gov/) and enforces standards through complaint investigations, audits, and formal hearings. Brokers are the linchpin—when a team member violates commission rules, the broker's supervision record is the first thing investigators examine.

## Licensing Requirements for Team Members

**Every individual who negotiates, lists, sells, or leases real property for compensation must hold an active West Virginia real estate license.** There are no exceptions for "team assistants" who happen to discuss pricing with clients or show property independently. WV Code 30-40-3 defines licensed activity broadly, and the WVREC has issued advisory opinions confirming that unlicensed activity within a team context carries the same penalties as any other unlicensed practice.

Team leaders holding a salesperson license cannot supervise other licensees. Only a licensed broker or associate broker with proper brokerage authorization may exercise supervisory authority. If your team leader is a salesperson, the designated broker retains direct supervisory responsibility over every team member—not the team leader.

New licensees joining a team must submit their license transfer using the WVREC's [online portal](https://wvrec.wv.gov/) and ensure their affiliation reflects the correct brokerage. The commission requires that license status be current before any team advertising goes live or any client interactions begin.

## Advertising and Team Name Registration

West Virginia's advertising rules for teams sit within Legislative Rule 174-01-7, which governs how licensees present themselves to the public. A team name cannot imply that it is an independent brokerage. Every advertisement—digital, print, yard sign, or social media profile—must include the legal name of the licensed brokerage in a reasonably prominent manner.

The WVREC does not maintain a formal team name registry, but brokers must approve all team names and ensure they do not mislead consumers. A name like "Mountain State Realty Group" would likely violate commission rules if the actual brokerage is "ABC Real Estate, LLC" and consumers could confuse the team for a standalone firm. The broker's name must appear in equal or greater prominence than the team name in all advertising.

| Advertising Element | Required Content | Common Violation |
|---|---|---|
| Yard signs | Brokerage name, agent name | Team name only, no brokerage |
| Social media profiles | Brokerage affiliation visible | Team branded as independent entity |
| Email signatures | Licensed name, brokerage | Nickname only, no brokerage |
| Business cards | Brokerage name, license status | Missing broker affiliation |
| Website headers | Brokerage name on every page | Brokerage buried in footer only |

Failing to include proper brokerage identification is not a mere technicality. The WVREC treats it as misleading advertising under WV Code 30-40-19(a)(4), which can trigger fines, license suspension, and mandatory corrective advertising at the agent's expense.

## Broker Supervision Duties Under WV Code 30-40

**Designated brokers carry personal liability for the actions of every licensee affiliated with their brokerage, including team members they may never meet in person.** WV Code 30-40-17 spells out the broker's duty to supervise, train, and maintain records. The statute does not distinguish between solo agents and team-based agents—the broker's obligation is identical.

Supervision means more than collecting paperwork after closing. The WVREC expects brokers to maintain written policies governing team operations, including protocols for file management, client communication standards, and advertising review. During audits, investigators look for documented evidence that the broker actively reviewed team transactions rather than passively collecting files.

Brokers must also ensure that team-held trust funds comply with WV Code 30-40-16. Earnest money and security deposits must be deposited into the brokerage's trust account—not a team-specific account. Any team that maintains a separate escrow account without proper brokerage authorization is in direct violation, exposing both the broker and team leader to disciplinary proceedings. ## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Team Compliance

Three to five errors appear repeatedly in WVREC disciplinary actions involving teams. Understanding these patterns can prevent career-ending violations.

**First, teams frequently allow unlicensed assistants to perform licensed activities.** A transaction coordinator or administrative assistant may schedule showings, prepare CMAs, or discuss offer terms with clients—all of which require a license. The line between licensed and unlicensed activity is defined in WV Code 30-40-3, and crossing it exposes the entire team to liability. Understanding [what a transaction coordinator actually does](/blog/what-is-a-transaction-coordinator) versus what requires a license is a critical distinction.

Second, team leaders act as de facto brokers without holding a broker's license. They conduct training sessions, review contracts, and provide guidance that constitutes supervision—yet they lack the legal authority to supervise. When a problem surfaces, the WVREC holds the designated broker responsible for allowing unauthorized supervision to occur.

Third, teams fail to update advertising when membership changes. An agent who leaves a team may still appear on the team website, yard signs, or social media for weeks or months. During that gap, consumers may believe the departed agent still operates under that team's umbrella, creating agency confusion and potential liability for transactions initiated under false pretenses.

Fourth, teams neglect to include proper agency disclosure forms at first substantive contact. West Virginia requires the Agency Disclosure Pamphlet and the appropriate agency election—designated agency, dual agency, or single agency—to be presented using WVREC-prescribed forms. Teams sometimes assume the team leader's initial disclosure covers all members, but each licensee who interacts substantively with a client must confirm their agency relationship independently.

Fifth, internal referral fee splits within teams go undocumented. While compensation arrangements between licensees affiliated with the same brokerage are generally permissible, the broker must have written agreements on file. Undocumented splits create audit failures and can look like unlicensed compensation to investigators.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers who oversee teams should maintain a recurring audit checklist that goes beyond standard file review. The WVREC's investigative process frequently targets supervision failures, and brokers who can produce documentation of proactive oversight fare far better in disciplinary proceedings.

| Audit Area | What to Check | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising compliance | All team ads include brokerage name | Monthly |
| License status | Every team member active and affiliated | Quarterly |
| Trust account deposits | All earnest money in brokerage account | Per transaction |
| Agency disclosures | Proper forms signed at first contact | Per transaction |
| Unlicensed activity | Assistants not performing licensed tasks | Monthly |
| Written team agreements | Compensation splits, roles documented | Annually |

**Brokers who fail audits face escalating consequences.** A first offense under WV Code 30-40-19 may result in a reprimand and mandatory corrective action. Repeated violations trigger fines up to $5,000 per occurrence, license suspension, or revocation. The WVREC also publishes disciplinary actions publicly, which creates reputational damage that outlasts the formal penalty.

Document retention matters enormously. West Virginia requires brokers to maintain transaction records for a minimum of three years after closing or termination of a listing agreement. For teams with high transaction volume, this means robust file management systems—not shoeboxes in a storage unit. ## Penalties for Non-Compliance: What Is Actually at Stake

The consequences for violating team supervision rules extend beyond administrative fines. WV Code 30-40-20 authorizes the WVREC to deny, suspend, or revoke any license for violations of the licensing act or commission rules. Criminal penalties also apply—practicing without a license is a misdemeanor under WV Code 30-40-27, carrying potential jail time and fines.

From a transaction standpoint, agency disclosure failures can void contracts. If a buyer or seller discovers that proper agency relationships were never established, they may have grounds to rescind under common law principles of fraud or misrepresentation. The practical result: deals collapse, commissions disappear, and lawsuits follow.

Errors and omissions insurance may not cover violations arising from willful non-compliance with licensing laws. Carriers routinely exclude coverage for intentional regulatory violations, leaving agents and brokers personally exposed to damage claims. A single unlicensed activity complaint within a team can cascade into six-figure liability.

## Building a Compliant Team Structure in 2026

The path to compliance starts with written agreements. Every team should operate under a formal team agreement that specifies roles, compensation, supervisory authority, and advertising standards. The designated broker must review and approve this document before the team begins operating.

Training is non-negotiable. The WVREC's continuing education requirements—under Legislative Rule 174-01-4—mandate that all licensees complete their CE hours, but team-specific training on advertising rules, agency disclosure protocols, and trust fund handling should supplement standard CE. Brokers who provide documented team training create a defensible record if complaints arise.
