## Connecticut Continuing Education Requirements 2026: The Full Breakdown

**If you hold a real estate license in Connecticut, the Connecticut continuing education requirements 2026 cycle is already in motion, and the window for completion is shorter than most agents assume.** The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection (DCP) oversees all licensing and renewal activity under the authority of Chapter 392, Connecticut General Statutes, Sections 20-311 through 20-329. Missing your obligations does not just mean an awkward phone call to your broker. It means your license lapses, your active deals face legal exposure, and your brokerage may absorb liability it never agreed to carry.

This is not a topic to push to the final month of your renewal period. The 2026 renewal cycle follows the same biennial structure Connecticut has enforced for years, but enforcement and auditing have tightened since the DCP expanded its compliance staff in 2024.

## How Many Hours and What Subjects

Connecticut mandates 12 hours of continuing education per renewal cycle. The cycle runs on a two-year basis tied to your individual license anniversary date, not a calendar year. Those 12 hours break down into required and elective components with no flexibility on the mandatory segments.

| Category | Hours Required | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-------|
| Fair Housing | 3 | Must cover federal and Connecticut fair housing law |
| Real Estate Law Update | 3 | Covers statutory changes from the prior cycle |
| Elective Topics | 6 | Must be DCP-approved; agency, ethics, contracts, etc. |
| **Total** | **12** | All must be completed before renewal date |

The fair housing requirement is not optional and cannot be substituted with a general ethics course. Connecticut treats these as distinct obligations. The law update course is redesigned each cycle by approved providers to reflect recent legislative changes, including 2025's amendments to Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 20-325d regarding dual agency disclosure timelines.

## The Renewal Process and Form Requirements

**Agents renew through the eLicense portal maintained by the Connecticut DCP, and the relevant form is the Real Estate License Renewal Application (DCP Form RES-2).** This form requires you to attest under penalty of perjury that you have completed all required CE hours. The DCP does not require you to submit certificates at the time of renewal, but they retain the right to audit your records for up to three years post-renewal.

You must keep original certificates of completion from each course for a minimum of three years. If audited, you will receive a notice via the address on your license file and have 30 days to produce documentation. Brokers filing on behalf of their agents using the Broker Certification of Salesperson Renewal (DCP Form RES-3) are certifying that they have reviewed and verified completion.

The renewal fee for salespersons is $285 and for brokers is $455. Late renewals within the 12-month grace period add a $100 penalty on top of the standard fee.

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

The consequences of non-compliance in Connecticut are tiered but none of them are trivial. The DCP's enforcement arm does not issue warnings for lapsed licenses. Here is the escalation path:

First, if your license expires without CE completion, you are immediately prohibited from conducting any licensed activity. Any commission earned on transactions that close during a lapse period may be subject to clawback by the brokerage or challenge by the opposing party. Second, the DCP can impose fines of up to $500 per violation under Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 20-325b for practicing without a valid license.

Third, if you fail to renew within 12 months of your expiration date, your license is considered abandoned. Reinstatement then requires retaking and passing the state licensing examination, completing the full 12 hours of CE, and submitting a new application with fees. Any active listings or buyer representation agreements you held become void, potentially exposing your former brokerage to breach-of-duty claims.

For deals already in contract, a lapsed license can trigger rescission rights for either party. Connecticut courts have held that representation by an unlicensed agent constitutes a material defect in the transaction, creating grounds for contract cancellation even after closing in extreme cases. This risk alone should motivate compliance well ahead of any deadline, and it underscores [why agents miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) more often than they care to admit.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Connecticut CE

Mistake one: completing courses from non-approved providers. The DCP maintains a specific list of approved continuing education providers, and courses from other states or generic national platforms that lack Connecticut DCP approval do not count. Agents discover this only when audited, at which point their renewal is retroactively invalid.

**Mistake two: assuming the fair housing requirement can be satisfied by NAR's Code of Ethics training.** These are different obligations. NAR's ethics requirement satisfies your membership duty but does not count toward Connecticut's statutory 3-hour fair housing mandate unless the specific course has separate DCP approval for that category.

Mistake three: losing certificates and having no backup documentation. Digital copies are acceptable for audit purposes, but "I completed it, I just can't prove it" is not a defense the DCP recognizes. Agents who rely on memory alone rather than a systematic filing approach consistently fail audits. There is a reason [why checklists beat memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) in every compliance context.

Mistake four: waiting until the final 30 days of the renewal cycle. Course availability tightens, online platforms experience enrollment caps, and any technical issue with eLicense becomes an emergency instead of an inconvenience. The DCP does not grant extensions for portal outages or provider scheduling conflicts.

Mistake five: confusing the broker renewal cycle with the salesperson cycle. Brokers have additional supervisory obligations and a different fee structure. A broker who completes only 12 hours of standard CE without verifying their agents' compliance has met their personal obligation but failed their statutory duty under Section 20-325a.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Connecticut places affirmative supervisory duties on brokers that go beyond simply reminding agents about deadlines. Under Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 20-325a, the broker is the responsible party for ensuring all licensees operating under their license are in active, valid status. This is not a suggestion. It is a legal obligation.

| Broker Responsibility | Frequency | Documentation |
|----------------------|-----------|---------------|
| Verify agent CE completion | Before each renewal | Copies of certificates on file |
| Confirm license active status via eLicense | Monthly recommended | Screenshot or printout |
| File DCP Form RES-3 for salesperson renewals | Each renewal cycle | Signed attestation |
| Respond to DCP audit requests | Within 30 days of notice | All agent records for prior 3 years |
| Remove lapsed agents from active listings | Immediately upon expiration | Written notice to agent and MLS |

**Brokers who allow agents with lapsed licenses to continue practicing face their own disciplinary action, including potential suspension of the brokerage license itself.** The DCP has increased random audits of mid-size brokerages in recent years, specifically targeting firms with 10 to 50 agents where compliance tracking tends to be informal.

A quarterly internal audit of license status and CE progress is the minimum defensible standard. Larger firms may need monthly checks. ## Approved Course Formats and Providers

The DCP approves courses delivered in three formats: live classroom, live webinar (synchronous), and online self-paced (asynchronous). All three formats satisfy the requirement equally, but the course must display the DCP approval number at the time of enrollment. If you cannot find the approval number, contact the provider before purchasing.

Providers must submit course curriculum to the DCP for review and receive written approval before advertising the course for Connecticut credit. Popular approved providers include the Connecticut Association of Realtors Education Foundation, CE Shop (Connecticut-specific catalog), and Kaplan Real Estate Education. Always verify current approval status on the [Connecticut DCP eLicense website](https://www.elicense.ct.gov/) before enrolling.

Courses taken in other states under reciprocity agreements do not automatically transfer as Connecticut CE credit. Connecticut does not participate in a multi-state CE reciprocity compact. If you hold licenses in multiple states, you must complete Connecticut-specific approved courses for your Connecticut renewal regardless of what you completed elsewhere.

## Planning Your 2026 Compliance Calendar

The most effective approach is distributing your 12 hours across the two-year cycle rather than concentrating them in a single sprint. Completing one course per quarter keeps you ahead of deadlines without requiring a dedicated "CE week" that conflicts with closings, listing appointments, and the general rhythm of production.

Mark your license anniversary date in your CRM and set alerts at 12 months, 6 months, and 90 days before expiration. At the 90-day mark, you should have all 12 hours completed or have firm enrollment dates for remaining courses. This buffer accounts for provider delays, illness, or the inevitable closing that demands your full attention during the final weeks.
