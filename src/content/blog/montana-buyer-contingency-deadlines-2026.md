## Understanding Montana Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026

Montana buyer contingency deadlines 2026 represent one of the highest-risk areas for agent liability in the state. A missed deadline can collapse a transaction, expose your brokerage to claims, or leave your buyer without recourse on a property defect discovered too late. The Montana Association of Realtors (MAR) forms and Montana Code Annotated (MCA) Title 37, Chapter 51, set the framework agents must follow—but the details trip up even experienced licensees.

The Board of Realty Regulation, operating under the Montana Department of Labor and Industry, enforces licensing standards that include competent handling of contract timelines. When a buyer's agent fails to track a financing contingency or an inspection objection window, the consequences go beyond an unhappy client. They extend to formal complaints, license discipline, and civil liability for damages.

This post breaks down how Montana contingency deadlines function, what forms control them, where agents consistently make errors, and what managing brokers need to enforce to keep their offices out of trouble.

## The Core Forms That Govern Montana Contingency Timelines

Montana agents working under the MAR umbrella rely on a suite of standardized forms. The MAR Residential Real Estate Purchase Agreement is the anchor document, and its contingency sections establish the timelines that bind both parties. The 2025-2026 revision of this form includes specific blank fields where agents fill in the number of days allocated for inspection, financing, appraisal, and sale of buyer's property contingencies.

**The MAR Buyer's Inspection Response form is where the majority of deadline failures occur.** This form requires the buyer to deliver written objections within the inspection period stated in the purchase agreement—typically 10 to 15 calendar days from mutual acceptance. If the buyer's agent fails to deliver this response by the deadline, the seller can treat the contingency as satisfied.

Additional forms agents must know include the MAR Contingency Release/Removal form, the MAR Extension of Deadlines Addendum, and the MAR Notice to Perform. Each of these documents either starts, stops, or extends a contingency clock. Agents who rely on verbal agreements or email confirmations without following up with the proper MAR form are inviting disputes.

## How Montana Calculates Contingency Days

Calendar day counting in Montana follows the terms written into the MAR Purchase Agreement. The general rule: Day 1 begins the day after mutual acceptance (the date both parties have signed the agreement and delivery has occurred). If a deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or state-recognized holiday, the deadline moves to the next business day.

| Contingency Type | Typical Default Period | Starts Counting From | Form Used to Object/Remove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inspection | 10-15 calendar days | Day after mutual acceptance | MAR Buyer's Inspection Response |
| Financing | 21-30 calendar days | Day after mutual acceptance | Written notice per contract |
| Appraisal | Tied to financing deadline | Day after mutual acceptance | MAR Contingency Release |
| Sale of Buyer's Property | Negotiated (often 30-60 days) | Day after mutual acceptance | MAR Contingency Release |
| Title Review | 10-15 calendar days | Day after title commitment delivery | Written objection per contract |

Montana Code Annotated 28-1-2004 governs computation of time for contracts generally, and this applies when the purchase agreement is silent on a specific calculation method. Agents should never assume a deadline—they should read the contract they drafted.

## Consequences of Missing a Contingency Deadline

The fallout from a blown deadline varies depending on which party suffers harm, but none of the outcomes are good for the agent responsible. If a buyer's inspection contingency expires without a timely objection, the buyer loses the right to request repairs or terminate based on inspection findings. That buyer may then be forced to close on a property with undisclosed defects—or forfeit earnest money if they walk away.

**From a regulatory standpoint, Montana's Board of Realty Regulation can discipline a licensee under MCA 37-51-321 for incompetence or negligence in performing licensed activities.** Failure to track and enforce contingency deadlines qualifies. Discipline ranges from a letter of warning to license suspension, depending on the harm caused and the agent's disciplinary history.

Civil liability adds another layer. If a buyer loses their earnest money deposit because their agent missed a financing contingency deadline, the buyer has grounds for a negligence claim. Brokerages carry errors and omissions insurance for exactly this reason, but repeated claims drive up premiums and trigger carrier audits. The [Montana Board of Realty Regulation](https://boards.bsd.dli.mt.gov/realty-regulation) publishes enforcement actions that serve as cautionary examples for practicing agents.

Sellers are not immune from exposure either. A listing agent who fails to deliver a Notice to Perform when the buyer's contingency deadline has passed may cost the seller weeks of market time—time that translates directly into financial loss in a shifting market.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Montana Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026

Five errors appear consistently in Montana transactions, and they account for the majority of deadline-related disputes escalated to brokers and attorneys.

**First, agents miscalculate the start date.** Mutual acceptance requires both signatures and delivery. If the buyer signs on Monday but the seller does not receive the signed contract until Wednesday, Day 1 is Thursday. Agents who start counting from the buyer's signature date—rather than the date of delivery to all parties—are working with the wrong deadline.

Second, agents rely on email delivery without confirming receipt. The MAR Purchase Agreement specifies acceptable methods of delivery, and while electronic delivery is permitted, the burden of proving delivery rests with the sending party. An unread email sitting in a spam folder does not constitute delivery under Montana contract law.

Third, agents fail to calendar the appraisal contingency separately from the financing contingency. These are related but distinct deadlines. A buyer can satisfy financing conditions while the appraisal remains unresolved, and agents who conflate the two risk waiving one contingency while protecting the other.

Fourth, agents neglect to issue written removal of contingencies when their buyer is satisfied. This omission leaves the seller uncertain about the transaction's status and can delay closing or trigger a Notice to Perform from the listing side. The MAR Contingency Release form exists for this purpose and takes minutes to complete.

Fifth, agents on teams delegate deadline tracking to unlicensed assistants without oversight. Under MCA 37-51-321 and the administrative rules governing team supervision, the licensee remains personally responsible for contract compliance. Delegation without supervision is not a defense to a Board complaint. For more on [why agents miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines), the pattern is remarkably consistent across states.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Managing brokers in Montana carry supervisory liability under MCA 37-51-321. The statute requires that a broker "exercise reasonable supervision" over the activities of licensees associated with the brokerage. When it comes to contingency deadlines, this means brokers cannot simply trust that their agents are tracking dates correctly.

| Broker Audit Item | Frequency | What to Check | Risk If Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mutual acceptance date verified | Every transaction at intake | Signatures, delivery confirmation | Incorrect deadline calculations |
| Contingency dates calendared | Within 24 hours of acceptance | Calendar system matches contract | Blown deadlines, liability exposure |
| Inspection response delivered on time | At deadline date | Delivery confirmation on file | Waived contingency, client harm |
| Financing contingency status | Weekly after Day 14 | Lender communication, form filed | Earnest money forfeiture risk |
| Contingency removal forms on file | At each removal event | Signed MAR form in transaction file | Ambiguous contract status |

Brokers should implement a file review system that flags transactions approaching contingency deadlines. A manual spreadsheet works for small offices. Larger brokerages need transaction management software that sends automated alerts. The key is documentation: if a dispute arises, the broker must demonstrate that a reasonable supervision system was in place.

Brokerages that have faced Board complaints over missed deadlines often share a common trait—they relied entirely on their agents' self-reporting rather than building independent verification into their workflow. The difference between a brokerage that survives an audit and one that faces sanctions is the paper trail. ## The Role of the Notice to Perform in Montana

Montana's MAR Notice to Perform is the enforcement mechanism sellers use when a buyer's contingency deadline has passed without action. This form gives the buyer a specified number of days (typically 2-3 business days) to either remove the contingency in writing or terminate the contract. If the buyer does neither, the seller gains the right to cancel.

Listing agents should understand that the Notice to Perform is not automatic. The seller must affirmatively choose to issue it through their agent. Some sellers prefer to wait, especially in slower markets where backup offers are scarce. But the listing agent's duty is to inform the seller of their rights once a deadline passes—not to make the strategic decision for them.

Buyer's agents receiving a Notice to Perform must act immediately. The compressed response window leaves no room for delayed communication with the buyer. Agents who are unavailable—on vacation, at a conference, or simply not checking their messages—put their clients at serious risk of contract termination. ## Protecting Your License and Your Clients in 2026

Montana's regulatory environment expects licensees to handle contingency management as a core competency. The Board does not treat missed deadlines as minor administrative oversights. When harm results—lost earnest money, forced closings on defective properties, or collapsed transactions—the Board views the responsible agent's conduct through the lens of professional competence.

Agents who want to reduce their exposure should build three habits: verify mutual acceptance dates independently, calendar every contingency deadline on the day of contract acceptance, and confirm delivery of every deadline-sensitive document with a read receipt or written acknowledgment. These are not optional best practices. They are the minimum standard of care Montana expects from its licensees.
