## Why Wyoming Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026 Demand Your Full Attention

Missing a single contingency deadline can kill a deal, expose you to a license complaint, or land your brokerage in a lawsuit. Wyoming buyer contingency deadlines 2026 carry the same risks they always have, but updated form language from the Wyoming Association of REALTORS (WAR) and evolving enforcement patterns from the Wyoming Real Estate Commission (WREC) make this year's compliance landscape worth a hard look. Every agent holding an active Wyoming license should understand exactly which deadlines apply, how they are calculated, and what falls apart when they are missed.

Wyoming is a relatively low-regulation state compared to neighbors like Colorado, but that informality cuts both ways. Fewer prescriptive statutes mean more responsibility shifts to the contract itself — and to the agent who drafts or advises on it. The WAR forms are where most of the deadline structure lives, and misreading a single paragraph can cost your client thousands.

## The Forms That Control Wyoming Contingency Deadlines

The primary contract form in most residential transactions is the **WAR Purchase and Sale Agreement (Form PSA)**. This is the document where buyer contingencies — inspection, financing, appraisal, title review, and sale of prior home — are created, defined, and given specific calendar-day deadlines. Agents should also be familiar with the WAR Inspection Addendum (Form IA) and the WAR Financing Addendum (Form FA), both of which overlay additional deadline requirements on top of the base PSA.

**Wyoming statute does not prescribe specific contingency periods the way some states do.** Instead, Wyo. Stat. Ann. Section 33-28-303 establishes the duties of licensees, which include competent handling of all contract terms. The WREC's Rules and Regulations, Chapter 3, further specify that agents must act with reasonable skill and care when advising clients on contractual deadlines. Failure to do so is a violation that WREC can investigate.

The practical result is that the WAR PSA's blank fields — where you fill in the number of days for each contingency — become the enforceable deadlines. There is no statutory backstop that says "if you forgot to fill in the inspection deadline, the buyer gets 10 days by default." If the field is blank, the contingency may be unenforceable, or worse, a court may imply a "reasonable time" standard that neither party intended.

## How Each Major Contingency Deadline Works

Understanding the mechanics of each deadline is not optional. Here is how the most common buyer contingencies function under the current WAR forms and Wyoming law.

**Inspection contingency.** The WAR PSA allows the buyer a negotiated number of calendar days from the binding agreement date to complete all inspections and deliver written objections to the seller. If the buyer does not deliver objections within that window, the contingency is waived. The seller then typically receives a separate negotiated period to respond — either agreeing to repairs, offering a credit, or declining.

**Financing contingency.** Under the WAR Financing Addendum, the buyer commits to applying for financing within a set number of days and must notify the seller if financing is denied by the contingency deadline. If the buyer cannot obtain a commitment letter by the deadline and fails to provide timely notice, the seller may have the right to terminate and retain earnest money depending on the contract language.

**Appraisal contingency.** This is sometimes embedded in the financing contingency and sometimes written as a standalone addendum. The deadline typically mirrors or falls within the financing contingency window. If the property appraises below the purchase price and the buyer does not invoke the contingency by the deadline, the buyer is obligated to proceed at the contract price.

| Contingency Type | Typical Deadline Range | Governed By | What Happens If Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inspection | 10-15 calendar days | WAR Form PSA, Section 8 | Contingency waived; buyer accepts property as-is |
| Financing | 21-30 calendar days | WAR Financing Addendum (Form FA) | Seller may terminate; earnest money at risk |
| Appraisal | Within financing window or standalone | WAR PSA or custom addendum | Buyer obligated to proceed at contract price |
| Title Review | 10-15 calendar days from title commitment delivery | WAR Form PSA, Section 9 | Buyer deemed to accept title as-is |
| Sale of Prior Home | Negotiated; often 30-60 days | Custom addendum or WAR form | Seller may invoke kick-out clause |

Note that all deadlines in WAR forms are calculated in calendar days, not business days. Weekends and holidays count. The only exception is if the deadline falls on a legal holiday or weekend, in which case some forms extend the deadline to the next business day — but agents must verify this in the specific form version being used.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Wyoming Contingency Deadlines

Mistakes in this area are not theoretical. WREC complaint files and brokerage E&O claims reveal recurring patterns that agents should memorize and avoid.

**Leaving deadline fields blank on the PSA.** This is the single most common and most dangerous error. When an agent submits a signed PSA with empty contingency deadline fields, the parties have no enforceable timeline. Courts may impose a "reasonable time" standard, but reasonable to a judge in Cheyenne may look very different from what your buyer in Jackson expected. WREC has cited agents under Chapter 3 violations for submitting materially incomplete contracts.

**Confusing the start date for deadline calculations.** The binding agreement date is not always the date the buyer signed. Under the WAR PSA, the binding agreement date is typically the date the last party signs and the fully executed contract is delivered. If the buyer signs on Monday but the seller does not countersign until Wednesday, the deadline clock starts Wednesday. Agents who count from the wrong date can blow a contingency by days without realizing it.

**Failing to deliver objection notices in writing.** A phone call to the listing agent saying "my buyer has inspection concerns" does not satisfy the written notice requirement in the WAR forms. The objection must be delivered in writing within the deadline. Verbal communications do not preserve the contingency, and agents who rely on them are [making the same mistake that causes deals to fall apart](/blog/where-deals-break-down) across every state.

**Not confirming receipt of the notice.** Delivery and receipt are not the same thing. The WAR PSA specifies acceptable methods of delivery, and an agent who emails an objection notice at 11:58 PM on the deadline date without confirming receipt is gambling. Best practice is to use a delivery method that provides confirmation — and to document it in the file.

**Ignoring the kick-out clause timeline on sale-of-home contingencies.** When a seller accepts a contingent offer with a kick-out clause, the buyer typically has 48 to 72 hours to waive the contingency or withdraw after receiving notice of a competing offer. Agents routinely miss this compressed window because they treat it like a standard deadline rather than an emergency response requirement.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers hold supervisory responsibility under Wyo. Stat. Ann. Section 33-28-303 and [WREC Rules Chapter 2](https://rules.wyo.gov/Search.aspx?mode=1), which defines the duties of employing brokers. If an agent under your supervision botches a contingency deadline, WREC can hold both the agent and the broker accountable.

**Every transaction file should be audited for complete deadline fields before the binding agreement date.** This means the broker or a designated transaction coordinator reviews the PSA and all addenda to confirm that every contingency deadline is filled in, mathematically correct, and consistent across documents. A financing deadline on the PSA that says 25 days but a Financing Addendum that says 30 days creates an ambiguity that can void the contingency entirely.

Brokers should also enforce a standardized deadline-tracking system across the office. Whether that is a spreadsheet, a transaction management platform, or an automated tool, the key requirement is that every active contingency deadline is visible and monitored. Agents who track deadlines in their heads or on sticky notes are the ones [who miss them most often](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines).

| Broker Audit Item | What to Check | Risk If Missed |
|---|---|---|
| PSA deadline fields | All contingency fields completed and consistent | Unenforceable contingencies; WREC complaint |
| Binding agreement date | Correct date used for all calculations | Deadlines off by days; waived contingencies |
| Written objection notices | In file, timestamped, delivery confirmed | Lost contingency rights; E&O claim |
| Addendum consistency | Deadlines match across PSA and all addenda | Ambiguity; litigation exposure |
| Kick-out clause responses | Documented within required window | Buyer loses right to proceed; seller liability |

Quarterly file audits are the minimum standard for any Wyoming brokerage that wants to stay ahead of WREC investigations. Brokers who wait until a complaint is filed to review their agents' deadline compliance are already behind.

## Wyoming Agency Disclosure and Its Relationship to Deadlines

Contingency deadlines do not exist in a vacuum. They intersect directly with Wyoming's [agency disclosure requirements](/blog/wyoming-agency-disclosure-requirements-2026), which obligate agents to act in their client's best interest when advising on or enforcing contractual timelines. A buyer's agent who fails to calendar a financing deadline is not just sloppy — that agent may have breached a fiduciary duty.

Wyoming's agency disclosure framework, governed by Wyo. Stat. Ann. Section 33-28-304, requires agents to disclose their agency relationship before the buyer enters into a contract. The duty of care that flows from that relationship includes competent management of all deadlines tied to the buyer's contractual rights. If an agent advises a buyer that "we have plenty of time" on an inspection deadline without verifying the actual calendar math, that agent is exposed.

**The [seller disclosure requirements](/blog/wyoming-seller-disclosure-requirements-2026) in Wyoming can also interact with contingency deadlines in unexpected ways.** If a seller delivers a property condition disclosure after the binding agreement date, some agents mistakenly believe the inspection contingency clock restarts. It does not, unless the contract explicitly provides for that. The inspection deadline runs from the binding agreement date as written in the PSA, regardless of when disclosures arrive.

## Liability When Deadlines Are Missed

The consequences of a missed contingency deadline fall into three categories: deal-level, regulatory, and financial.

At the deal level, a missed inspection deadline means the buyer waives the right to object. A missed financing deadline may allow the seller to terminate and keep the earnest money. A missed appraisal deadline can force the buyer to close at a price above fair market value. Every missed deadline shifts leverage — and sometimes money — from one party to the other.

At the regulatory level, WREC has the authority to investigate complaints, issue fines, require additional education, place licenses on probation, or revoke licenses entirely. Chapter 3 of the WREC Rules and Regulations defines incompetence and negligence as grounds for disciplinary action. An agent who repeatedly misses contingency deadlines is building a record that WREC will treat as a pattern, not an isolated incident.

At the financial level, errors and omissions insurance may cover some claims, but E&O policies typically exclude intentional acts and may not cover claims arising from repeated negligence. Brokerages that fail to supervise are jointly exposed. The cost of defending a single deadline-related lawsuit in Wyoming can easily exceed $50,000 before any judgment is reached.
