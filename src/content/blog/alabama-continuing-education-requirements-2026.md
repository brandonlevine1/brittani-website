## Understanding Alabama Continuing Education Requirements 2026

The Alabama Real Estate Commission (AREC) does not give agents much room for ambiguity when it comes to renewal obligations. Alabama continuing education requirements 2026 remain anchored to the same two-year cycle that has governed license renewal for over a decade, but enforcement patterns and audit frequency have shifted in ways that demand attention. Every active salesperson and associate broker must complete 15 hours of approved CE before their license expiration date, and the consequences for falling short extend well beyond a lapsed credential.

AREC operates under the authority of the Alabama Real Estate License Law, codified in Title 34, Chapter 27 of the Code of Alabama (1975). The Commission's administrative rules—found in Chapter 790-X-1 of the Alabama Administrative Code—spell out the exact mechanics of continuing education approval, course delivery, and licensee compliance. Agents who assume their brokerage handles all of this on their behalf often learn otherwise at the worst possible moment.

## Hour Breakdown and Mandatory Coursework

**Alabama's 15-hour CE requirement is not a buffet where you pick any 15 hours of real estate content.** Three of those hours must come from a Commission-approved Risk Management course, which covers errors and omissions exposure, fair housing updates, and agency pitfalls specific to Alabama practice. The remaining 12 hours are elective, but all courses must carry AREC approval numbers.

The Risk Management course changes content periodically, and AREC publishes the approved syllabus through its website and official provider list. Agents cannot substitute a generic risk course from another state or a national association webinar unless that specific offering has received an AREC course approval number. This catches out-of-state practitioners with Alabama licenses more often than you might expect.

| Requirement | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Risk Management (mandatory) | 3 | Must be AREC-approved syllabus |
| Elective CE courses | 12 | Any AREC-approved provider |
| Total per renewal cycle | 15 | Two-year cycle tied to birth month |
| Post-license (first renewal only) | 30 | Separate from CE; not interchangeable |

Renewal cycles in Alabama are tied to the licensee's birth month. Your license expires on the last day of the month in which you were born, every two years from the date of initial issuance. Missing this date—even by a single day—triggers automatic inactive status.

## What Happens When an Agent Fails to Comply

The consequences of non-compliance in Alabama are layered. First, your license lapses to inactive status. You cannot show property, write offers, negotiate contracts, or collect a commission on any pending transaction. Second, any deals you participated in while unknowingly or knowingly operating on an expired license may face legal challenges from opposing parties.

Under Section 34-27-36 of the Code of Alabama, practicing real estate without an active license constitutes a Class A misdemeanor. Fines can reach $2,000 per violation, and AREC has the authority to deny future reinstatement altogether. Third-party claims against agents who closed transactions while non-compliant are not theoretical—they show up in Alabama circuit court dockets every year.

**Brokers face their own liability when affiliated agents operate on lapsed licenses.** AREC Rule 790-X-1-.13 places supervisory responsibility squarely on the qualifying broker's shoulders. If a broker knowingly—or through negligence—allows an agent to practice without a current license, the Commission can suspend or revoke the broker's own license, independent of any action taken against the agent. This is not a warning letter scenario; it is a formal disciplinary proceeding.

Reinstatement after a lapse requires completing all delinquent CE hours, paying a late fee, and submitting a reinstatement application on [AREC Form 6](https://arec.alabama.gov). The Commission reviews reinstatement applications on a case-by-case basis, and processing can take 30 to 60 days—during which the agent remains unable to transact.

## Common Mistakes Alabama Agents Make with CE Compliance

Patterns emerge from AREC disciplinary records and from the questions that flood brokerage compliance desks every renewal season. These five errors account for the majority of CE-related problems in Alabama.

**First, agents confuse post-license education with continuing education.** The 30-hour post-license requirement applies only to the first renewal period after initial licensure. Those hours do not carry forward, and completing them does not exempt you from the 15-hour CE requirement in subsequent cycles. Agents who earned their license in 2024 and renewed in 2026 after completing post-license coursework will still owe 15 hours of CE when their next renewal arrives.

Second, agents complete courses from non-approved providers. Online platforms that offer real estate education in multiple states sometimes advertise "Alabama-approved" courses that have not actually received an AREC course number. Always verify the course approval number on AREC's official provider list before investing time and money. A certificate from an unapproved provider is worthless for renewal purposes.

Third, agents wait until the final week of their renewal month. AREC's online reporting system processes CE completions from approved providers, but transmission delays of 5 to 10 business days are common. An agent who finishes a course on September 28 with a September 30 expiration may find that the hours have not posted in time. Fourth, agents assume their broker will notify them of an impending expiration. While many brokerages track renewal dates, this is a courtesy—not a legal obligation on behalf of the agent.

Fifth, agents take the same Risk Management course from a prior cycle and expect it to count again. AREC updates the mandatory syllabus periodically, and only the current-cycle version satisfies the requirement. Repeating a 2024 Risk Management course for a 2026 renewal will not work unless AREC has explicitly re-approved that course for the current cycle.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Qualifying brokers in Alabama carry a supervisory burden that many underestimate. AREC Rule 790-X-1-.13 does not merely suggest that brokers monitor their agents' license status—it requires it. A broker who cannot demonstrate reasonable oversight faces the same disciplinary exposure as the non-compliant agent.

| Broker Audit Task | Frequency | Source of Verification |
|---|---|---|
| Verify each agent's license expiration date | Quarterly minimum | AREC online license lookup |
| Confirm CE completion before renewal deadline | 60 days before expiration | AREC transcript or provider certificate |
| Document supervisory review | Each renewal cycle | Internal compliance file |
| Remove non-compliant agents from active roster | Immediately upon lapse | AREC notification or internal tracking |
| Report license status changes to AREC | Within 10 days | AREC Form 4 (Transfer/Termination) |

**Brokers should build a recurring calendar system that flags agent renewal dates no later than 90 days before expiration.** A verbal reminder is not sufficient documentation if AREC investigates. Written notices—email is acceptable—create a paper trail that demonstrates the broker's good-faith supervisory effort. If an agent refuses to complete CE and the broker terminates the affiliation, the broker must file AREC Form 4 within 10 days of the separation.

Failure to terminate an agent whose license has lapsed is one of the fastest routes to a broker disciplinary hearing. AREC auditors review active rosters against license status databases, and discrepancies trigger automatic investigation notices. This is not something you want to discover during a transaction audit or, worse, during litigation over a deal that closed with a non-licensed affiliated agent.

For brokerages juggling dozens of agents and [multiple deadlines across active transactions](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines), a manual spreadsheet approach breaks down quickly. The operational cost of tracking renewals, CE transcripts, and supervisory documentation is real, and it compounds as team size grows.

## Alabama-Specific Forms and Regulatory References

Alabama's renewal and reinstatement process involves several specific forms that agents and brokers should know by number. AREC Form 6 is the reinstatement application for lapsed licenses. AREC Form 4 handles transfers and terminations of agent affiliations. The license renewal itself is processed through AREC's online portal, but agents without internet access may request a paper renewal form directly from the Commission's Montgomery office.

The Alabama Real Estate License Law (Title 34, Chapter 27, Code of Alabama 1975) is the statutory foundation for all CE requirements. Administrative rules in Chapter 790-X-1 provide the procedural details—course approval standards, provider requirements, and enforcement mechanisms. Agents can access the full text of both the statute and administrative rules through AREC's website or the [Alabama Legislature's online code database](https://arec.alabama.gov).

Section 34-27-35 specifically addresses the Commission's authority to establish CE requirements, including the power to mandate specific subject areas. This is the statutory basis for the Risk Management course requirement. Section 34-27-36 covers penalties for practicing without an active license, and Section 34-27-32 outlines grounds for license suspension or revocation, which include failure to comply with Commission rules on education.
