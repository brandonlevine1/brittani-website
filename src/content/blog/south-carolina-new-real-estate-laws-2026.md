## South Carolina New Real Estate Laws 2026: The Statutory Changes That Matter

The South Carolina legislature and the SC Real Estate Commission have enacted a wave of regulatory updates that reshape how agents handle buyer representation, property disclosures, and transaction documentation. If you hold an active license in the Palmetto State, ignoring these **South Carolina new real estate laws 2026** changes means risking deal cancellations, personal liability, and disciplinary action. This is not a soft advisory—several provisions carry automatic penalties that kick in without warning.

The bulk of these changes trace back to two sources: amendments to SC Code Title 40, Chapter 57 (the Real Estate License Law) passed during the 2025 legislative session, and updated administrative rules from the [SC Real Estate Commission](https://llr.sc.gov/re/) effective between January and July 2026. Together, they touch buyer agency agreements, seller disclosure timelines, wire fraud prevention duties, and continuing education mandates.

## Buyer Agency Agreements Are Now Mandatory Before Substantive Services

**SC Code Section 40-57-370, as amended, now requires every licensee representing a buyer to execute a written buyer representation agreement before providing any substantive real estate services.** That includes showing properties, writing offers, or providing a comparative market analysis tailored to a buyer's search criteria. The old practice of delaying the agreement until offer-writing is dead.

The Commission has approved Form 540, the SC Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement, and Form 541, the SC Non-Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement, as the standard forms agents must use or substantially replicate. Both forms must specify the term of representation, the scope of services, and the compensation structure—including whether the buyer may be responsible for any portion of the agent's fee not covered by a seller's offer of cooperation.

Agents who provide substantive services without a signed agreement face two consequences. First, the Commission may impose fines up to $5,000 per transaction and suspend or revoke the license under SC Code Section 40-57-150(A)(14). Second, any compensation claimed by the agent becomes unenforceable in court, meaning you work for free if the buyer disputes payment.

## Seller Disclosure Form Updates and New Delivery Timelines

The SC Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement—historically governed by SC Code Section 27-50-40—has been updated with an expanded list of required disclosures effective March 1, 2026. The revised form (Form 312-R) adds mandatory fields for prior insurance claims related to water intrusion, the presence of polybutylene plumbing, and known encroachments or boundary disputes.

Delivery timelines have also tightened. Previously, sellers had to provide the disclosure "before acceptance of a real estate purchase contract." The 2026 amendment mandates delivery no later than five calendar days after the property is listed on any MLS or public marketing platform. If a seller fails to deliver Form 312-R within this window, the listing agent must document the refusal in writing and provide the buyer's agent with notice using the new Form 312-N (Notice of Non-Disclosure).

| Requirement | Pre-2026 Rule | 2026 Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure delivery deadline | Before contract acceptance | Within 5 days of listing |
| Required form version | Form 312 | Form 312-R |
| New mandatory fields | N/A | Water intrusion claims, polybutylene plumbing, encroachments |
| Seller refusal documentation | Informal | Form 312-N required |
| Buyer rescission window if late delivery | 3 business days | 5 business days |

This change matters because late or incomplete disclosures now give buyers an expanded five-business-day rescission right after receipt. Deals can collapse simply because a listing agent dragged their feet.

## Wire Fraud Prevention Duties for Agents

South Carolina's new wire fraud prevention statute, SC Code Section 40-57-375, imposes affirmative duties on licensees that go beyond the typical "be aware of scams" training. Every agent involved in a transaction must now provide clients with a written wire fraud advisory—Form 800—within three business days of contract execution. The form warns buyers and sellers to independently verify all wiring instructions by calling the closing attorney's office at a number obtained independently of any email communication.

**Agents who fail to deliver Form 800 may face contributory negligence claims if their client suffers a wire fraud loss.** The statute does not make the agent a guarantor against fraud, but it creates a rebuttable presumption of negligence if the form was never delivered or delivered late. This is a significant shift from prior law, where agents had no codified duty regarding wire instructions.

## Continuing Education: The New 2-Hour Buyer Representation Module

The SC Real Estate Commission added a mandatory 2-hour CE module titled "Buyer Representation Standards and Compliance" to the renewal requirements for all active licensees. This module must be completed before your next renewal date falling on or after July 1, 2026. It covers the buyer agency agreement requirements, permissible compensation structures, and the anti-steering provisions embedded in the amended statute.

Failure to complete the module by your renewal date results in automatic license inactivation—not a grace period, not a warning letter. You cannot practice until the module is completed and a $200 reinstatement fee is paid. The Commission's online portal lists approved providers, and most courses run approximately $45 to $75.

This is separate from the standard 10-hour CE requirement for biennial renewal. Think of it as an additional, one-time mandate that will likely be folded into future renewal cycles once the Commission confirms universal compliance.

## Common Mistakes Agents Are Already Making

Even though several provisions took effect in early 2026, disciplinary filings from the first two quarters reveal a pattern of avoidable errors.

**First, agents are using outdated buyer agency forms.** The old Form 530 (SC Buyer Agency Agreement) is no longer compliant because it lacks the required compensation disclosure language mandated by amended Section 40-57-370. Using Form 530 after January 1, 2026 is treated as if no agreement exists, exposing the agent to the same penalties as having no agreement at all.

Second, listing agents are treating the new disclosure delivery deadline as optional. The five-day-from-listing requirement applies to the moment a property hits the MLS or any public platform—including social media posts with a price and call to action. Agents who list on a Thursday and wait until the following week to collect the disclosure are already in violation.

Third, agents are delivering Form 800 (wire fraud advisory) via email attachment without confirming receipt. The statute requires documented delivery, which the Commission interprets as either a signed acknowledgment or a delivery receipt from a transaction management platform. An unconfirmed email does not satisfy the standard.

Fourth, some agents are conflating the non-exclusive buyer agreement (Form 541) with an open buyer arrangement that requires no form at all. There is no exemption. Even if you are showing a single property one time to an unrepresented buyer who contacted you through a sign call, you must execute either Form 540 or Form 541 before unlocking the door.

Fifth, agents are failing to update their pre-listing checklists to include Form 312-N when a seller refuses to complete the disclosure. Without the refusal form, the listing agent bears personal liability for the missing disclosure under SC Code Section 27-50-80. ## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers-in-charge carry heightened supervisory responsibility under SC Code Section 40-57-137, and the 2026 amendments expand what counts as a "failure to supervise." The Commission has signaled through advisory opinions that brokers will be held accountable if their agents systematically fail to use current forms or miss disclosure deadlines.

| Audit Item | What to Check | Enforcement Action if Missing |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer agency agreement (Form 540/541) | Signed before first showing; correct form version | Broker reprimand + agent fine up to $5,000 |
| Seller disclosure (Form 312-R) | Delivered within 5 days of listing activation | Broker warning; agent liable for buyer rescission costs |
| Wire fraud advisory (Form 800) | Delivered within 3 days of contract execution with documented receipt | Contributory negligence exposure for agent and brokerage |
| CE compliance tracking | Module completion before renewal date | License inactivation cascades to E&O coverage gaps |
| Form 312-N (seller refusal) | On file whenever seller declines disclosure | Listing agent assumes personal disclosure liability |

**Brokers should run a quarterly audit of every active listing and pending transaction file to verify form currency and delivery timestamps.** The Commission's random audit program now specifically targets buyer agency documentation and wire fraud advisory records—two categories that were not flagged in prior audit cycles.

If your brokerage uses a transaction management platform, configure it to reject file submissions missing Form 540/541, Form 312-R, or Form 800. ## Liability Exposure: What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

The penalty structure under the 2026 amendments operates on three levels. Administrative penalties from the Commission range from written reprimands to license revocation, with fines of $1,000 to $10,000 depending on severity and pattern. Civil liability arises when a buyer rescinds a contract due to late disclosure or when wire fraud losses are attributed to agent negligence. Criminal referral is possible under SC Code Section 16-13-430 if an agent's omission was willful and resulted in financial harm exceeding $10,000.

Deal cancellation is the most immediate practical risk. A buyer who receives Form 312-R after contract execution—rather than before or within the mandated window—can walk away within five business days and recover their earnest money without penalty. For a listing agent whose seller refuses to sign Form 312-N, that agent becomes the de facto disclosure source, and any material omission attaches personally to them.

The Commission's disciplinary process is not hypothetical. In the first quarter of 2026 alone, seven agents received formal complaints related to buyer agency agreement failures, and two brokerages received supervisory citations for systemic non-compliance. The message is clear: enforcement is active, not aspirational. For agents wondering [why missing deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) creates compounding risk, the 2026 amendments have essentially converted administrative sloppiness into statutory violations with financial teeth.

Every South Carolina licensee needs to internalize that these **South Carolina new real estate laws 2026** provisions are not suggestions waiting for a future effective date—they are live, enforceable, and being actively monitored. The agents who treat compliance as a systems problem rather than a memory test will be the ones still closing deals without disciplinary shadows hanging over their files.
