## Utah Continuing Education Requirements 2026: The Rules That Catch Agents Off Guard

Missing a continuing education deadline in Utah does not result in a polite reminder letter. It results in a lapsed license, forfeited commissions, and potential disciplinary action from the Utah Division of Real Estate. Understanding Utah continuing education requirements 2026 is not optional — it is the baseline for keeping your livelihood intact.

The Utah Division of Real Estate, operating under the Department of Commerce, governs all licensee education mandates through [Utah Code Title 61, Chapter 2f](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title61/Chapter2F/61-2f.html) and the associated administrative rules in R162-2f. Every sales agent and associate broker renewing in 2026 faces the same 18-hour requirement, but how those hours break down — and what counts versus what does not — trips up more agents than you might expect.

## How the 18-Hour Requirement Breaks Down

Utah mandates 18 hours of approved continuing education during each two-year license renewal cycle. Those hours are split into two categories: 9 hours of mandatory core coursework and 9 hours of approved electives.

The 9 mandatory hours include three specific modules that cannot be substituted. First, the 3-hour Utah Real Estate Core course covers recent legislative changes, Division rule updates, and enforcement trends. Second, a 3-hour module on Utah-specific contracts and forms — including updates to the Real Estate Purchase Contract (REPC) Form 24 and the Seller Disclosure Form — ensures agents stay current on transactional documents. Third, a 3-hour ethics or professional standards course rounds out the core requirement.

**The 9 elective hours must come from courses pre-approved by the Division of Real Estate.** Not every online CE provider holds Utah approval, and completing a course from a non-approved provider means those hours will not count toward renewal. Agents should verify provider approval status directly through the Division's online licensing portal before enrolling.

| Category | Hours Required | Topics Covered |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| Core Course | 3 hours | Legislative updates, Division rule changes |
| Contracts and Forms | 3 hours | REPC Form 24, Seller Disclosure updates |
| Ethics/Professional Standards | 3 hours | Agency duties, fair housing, fiduciary obligations |
| Approved Electives | 9 hours | Agent's choice from Division-approved providers |
| **Total** | **18 hours** | Completed per two-year renewal cycle |

## Renewal Deadlines and the Grace Period Trap

Utah real estate licenses expire on the last day of the licensee's birth month, every two years from the date of initial licensure. This means renewal deadlines are staggered across the calendar year — there is no single "renewal season" for the entire state.

The Division does allow a limited grace period for late renewals, but calling it a grace period is generous. If you miss your expiration date, you have up to six months to reinstate by completing all CE, submitting a reinstatement application, and paying a late fee that is currently set at 100% of the renewal fee (effectively doubling your cost). During this window, your license is inactive — you cannot list properties, write offers, or collect compensation.

**After six months without renewal, your license is terminated entirely.** Reinstatement at that point requires reapplication, potentially retaking the state licensing examination, and completing the full pre-licensing education hours again. The Division does not grant extensions based on workload, personal circumstances, or "I forgot."

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

The consequences of practicing on an expired or lapsed license extend far beyond a fine. Under Utah Code 61-2f-301, engaging in real estate activity without an active license constitutes a violation that can result in civil penalties up to $5,000 per transaction, criminal misdemeanor charges, and a permanent mark on your regulatory record.

From a transactional standpoint, any commission earned while unlicensed is subject to forfeiture. Brokerages that discover an affiliated agent practiced without a valid license face their own exposure — the listing or buyer agency agreement may be voidable, and the brokerage's errors and omissions insurance may deny coverage for those transactions. This is not a theoretical risk; the Division's enforcement reports show multiple cases annually where agents lose commissions retroactively.

Beyond monetary penalties, the Division can issue a formal citation, require additional education hours as a condition of reinstatement, or suspend the license for a defined period even after the agent completes all outstanding CE. The disciplinary record becomes public through the Division's license lookup tool, which means prospective clients and referring agents can see it.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Utah CE

Five errors account for the majority of compliance problems the Division encounters during renewal audits.

**First, agents assume that any online real estate course counts toward Utah CE.** It does not. The course provider must hold specific Utah Division of Real Estate approval, and the course itself must be listed on the Division's approved course roster. National Association of Realtors designation courses, for example, do not automatically qualify unless they have been separately approved by Utah.

Second, agents complete their CE hours but fail to verify that the provider transmitted completion records to the Division. Utah relies on electronic reporting from approved providers. If the provider's system glitches or the agent's license number was entered incorrectly, the Division's records will show incomplete CE even though the agent did the work. Agents should log into the Division's [RELMS portal](https://realestate.utah.gov/) at least 30 days before expiration to confirm all hours appear.

Third, agents confuse their NAR membership ethics requirement with the state-mandated ethics module. The NAR Code of Ethics training satisfies the national association's membership obligation but may not satisfy the Division's 3-hour ethics core requirement unless the specific course holds state approval. These are two separate compliance tracks with two separate governing bodies.

Fourth, new licensees miscalculate their first renewal cycle. Utah requires that agents licensed for less than one year before their first renewal date complete only 12 hours (the 9 core hours plus 3 elective hours) rather than the full 18. Agents who over-complete in their first cycle cannot bank extra hours for the next period.

Fifth, agents wait until the final week before expiration to start their CE. When course platforms crash, providers experience delays in reporting, or the agent realizes they enrolled in a non-approved course, there is no time buffer. The Division does not accept "I tried to complete it" as a valid excuse for expired CE.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Under Utah Admin. Code R162-2f-401a, principal brokers bear direct supervisory responsibility for ensuring every affiliated licensee maintains an active, compliant license. This is not a suggestion — it is an enforceable regulatory duty that carries its own disciplinary consequences for the broker.

Brokers should implement a quarterly audit of all affiliated agents' license expiration dates. The Division's RELMS system allows brokers to view the status of every licensee affiliated with their brokerage. Waiting until an agent's renewal date to check compliance is too late; by that point, a lapsed agent may have already conducted transactions on an inactive license, exposing the brokerage to liability.

| Broker Responsibility | Frequency | Source of Authority |
|----------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Verify agent license status | Quarterly minimum | Utah Admin. Code R162-2f-401a |
| Confirm CE completion records | 60 days before agent expiration | Division RELMS portal |
| Remove lapsed agents from listings and MLS | Immediately upon expiration | Brokerage policy + MLS rules |
| Report known unlicensed activity | Upon discovery | Utah Code 61-2f-401 |
| Maintain records of agent compliance checks | Ongoing | Best practice for E&O defense |

**Brokers who fail to remove an expired agent from active listings and the MLS face their own potential citation from the Division.** The standard of care here is strict — ignorance of an affiliated agent's lapse is not a valid defense when the broker had access to the RELMS verification tool. If you are managing a team of agents, building a [systematic review process](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) that includes license status checks protects both the brokerage and individual transactions.

## The 2026 Core Course: What Changed This Cycle

The 2026 mandatory core course includes updated content reflecting legislative changes from the 2025 and 2026 Utah legislative sessions. Key areas of focus include revised disclosure obligations related to water rights and availability (a growing concern along the Wasatch Front), updated wire fraud prevention requirements for licensees handling earnest money references, and changes to the Division's advertising rules for social media and digital marketing.

The contracts and forms module for 2026 incorporates revisions to the REPC Form 24, particularly around the due diligence period timeline and the buyer's right to terminate provisions. Agents who have not yet taken the 2026 core course should expect exam-style knowledge checks on these form changes, as the Division has increased testing rigor in response to complaints about agents passing courses without engaging with the material.

## Tracking Your Hours Without Losing Your Mind

The Division does not send reminder postcards. It does not call you 90 days before expiration. The entire burden falls on the licensee to track deadlines, verify completed hours, and submit renewal paperwork on time.

Many agents rely on their brokerage admin or transaction coordinator to flag upcoming deadlines, which works until that person leaves or the system fails. A better approach is setting calendar reminders at 120, 90, 60, and 30 days before your renewal date. At 120 days, confirm how many hours you have completed. At 90 days, enroll in remaining courses. At 60 days, verify that all completed hours appear in RELMS. At 30 days, submit your renewal application and payment.

If you are already using a system that tracks [deadlines across transactions](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines), adding CE renewal dates to that same workflow takes minimal effort and eliminates the single biggest reason agents lapse: they simply forgot.
