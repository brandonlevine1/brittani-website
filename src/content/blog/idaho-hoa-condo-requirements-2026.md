# Idaho HOA & Condo Disclosure Rules 2026: What Every Agent Must Deliver Before Closing

Getting the **Idaho hoa & condo disclosure rules 2026** wrong does not just annoy the other side of the transaction — it can unwind a deal days before closing or expose your brokerage to liability claims that no E&O policy will cheerfully cover. Idaho's statutory framework splits HOA and condominium disclosures into separate code sections, and agents who conflate the two requirements routinely miss deadlines or deliver incomplete packets. This post breaks down the specific statutes, the forms you should be using, and the consequences of getting it wrong.

## The Statutory Foundation: Idaho Code Title 55, Chapters 15 and 32

Idaho governs condominiums under the **Idaho Condominium Property Act** (Idaho Code, Title 55, Chapter 15), with the resale disclosure requirements concentrated in [Section 55-1516](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title55/T55CH15/SECT55-1516/). Planned community associations — the non-condo HOAs covering single-family subdivisions, townhome developments, and mixed-use projects — fall under the **Idaho Planned Community Act** (Idaho Code, Title 55, Chapter 32), specifically Section 55-3214 for resale certificates.

The distinction matters because the two statutes impose different content requirements on the disclosure documents and slightly different procedural obligations on the parties. Agents who treat every association property identically will eventually deliver the wrong packet to a buyer. The Idaho Real Estate Commission (IREC) expects licensees to know which statute applies to the property they are listing or selling.

## What the Condo Resale Certificate Must Contain

Under Idaho Code 55-1516, the seller of a condominium unit must furnish the buyer with a **resale certificate** issued by the association. The certificate must include the monthly common expense assessment, any special assessments currently due or approved, the association's current operating budget, and a statement of any outstanding judgments or pending litigation involving the association.

It must also disclose the amount of reserves, any capital expenditure plans approved by the board, and whether the association holds insurance covering the unit. The certificate should identify any violations charged against the unit and confirm whether the seller is current on assessments. If the association fails to produce the certificate within ten days of a written request, the seller may provide a sworn statement containing the required information.

**Buyers receive a statutory five-day review period after delivery of the resale certificate.** During this window, the buyer may cancel the purchase agreement without forfeiting earnest money. The clock does not start until the certificate is actually in the buyer's hands — email delivery counts only if the purchase agreement authorizes electronic delivery under Idaho's Uniform Electronic Transactions Act.

## Planned Community HOA Disclosures Under Chapter 32

Section 55-3214 mirrors many of the condo requirements but applies to non-condominium planned communities. The resale certificate here must contain the current assessment amount, any pending special assessments, the association's financial statements, and disclosure of pending lawsuits.

One difference that trips agents up: the planned community statute explicitly requires disclosure of any right of first refusal or transfer approval authority held by the association. If a buyer does not receive this information and the association later exercises a right of first refusal, the resulting mess lands squarely on the agent who failed to surface the restriction. The five-day cancellation right applies here as well.

| Requirement | Condominium (55-1516) | Planned Community (55-3214) |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly assessment amount | Required | Required |
| Special assessments | Required | Required |
| Reserve fund balance | Required | Required |
| Pending litigation disclosure | Required | Required |
| Right of first refusal | Not specifically listed | Explicitly required |
| Insurance coverage statement | Required | Not explicitly required |
| Buyer cancellation period | 5 days | 5 days |
| Association response deadline | 10 days | 10 days |

## Forms Idaho Agents Should Use

The Idaho Association of REALTORS (IAR) publishes standardized forms that address HOA and condo transactions. The **RE-25 HOA Addendum** is the form most agents should attach to any purchase agreement involving an association-governed property. This addendum establishes the timeline for document delivery, identifies which party pays for the resale certificate, and memorializes the buyer's cancellation rights.

For condominiums specifically, agents should also reference the **RE-24 Condominium Resale Certificate Request** form when requesting the certificate from the association's management company. Some brokerages have developed internal tracking sheets, but the IAR forms remain the industry standard and carry the language that IREC examiners expect to see in transaction files.

The Idaho [Property Condition Disclosure Form (RE-25 PCDS)](https://irec.idaho.gov/) does not substitute for the resale certificate. Agents occasionally assume the seller disclosure covers association-related information, but the PCDS addresses the physical condition of the property — not the financial health of the HOA. Both documents must be delivered independently.

## Consequences of Non-Compliance: Liability, Rescission, and License Risk

**Failure to deliver the required resale certificate preserves the buyer's right to cancel indefinitely.** Because the five-day cancellation window does not begin until delivery occurs, a closing that proceeds without the certificate leaves the transaction vulnerable to post-closing rescission claims. Idaho courts have recognized that statutory disclosure rights cannot be waived by implication or silence.

From a licensing perspective, IREC can pursue disciplinary action under Idaho Code 54-2059 for incompetence, dishonest dealing, or failure to present documents in a timely manner. Penalties range from a letter of reprimand to license suspension. Civil liability adds another layer — a buyer who discovers undisclosed special assessments after closing may pursue damages against both the seller and the agent who failed to procure the certificate.

Brokerages also face vicarious liability. If an agent's failure to deliver HOA disclosures results in a buyer's loss, the supervising broker can be named in the complaint. This risk alone should make compliance auditing a priority for every Idaho brokerage managing condo or HOA listings.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Idaho HOA Disclosures

**First, agents frequently conflate the seller's property condition disclosure with the HOA resale certificate.** These are separate obligations under separate statutes, and delivering one does not satisfy the other. A seller who checks "yes" to an HOA on the PCDS has not discharged the resale certificate requirement.

Second, agents request the resale certificate too late in the transaction timeline. Associations have ten days to respond, and management companies in high-volume markets like Boise and Meridian often take the full ten days. If you wait until week three of a 30-day escrow, you are gambling with your buyer's cancellation window and your closing date.

Third, listing agents assume the buyer's agent will handle the disclosure request. Under Idaho law, the obligation to furnish the resale certificate rests on the seller. The listing agent should initiate the request on behalf of their client, not rely on the other side to chase it down.

Fourth, agents fail to document the delivery date of the resale certificate. Without proof of when the buyer received the documents, disputes about whether the five-day cancellation period expired become he-said-she-said arguments. Use email with read receipts, signed acknowledgments, or transaction management timestamps.

Fifth, agents skip the HOA addendum on properties where the association is informal or underfunded. Even a three-lot subdivision with a volunteer-run HOA can carry assessment obligations and architectural restrictions. If CC&Rs exist and are recorded, the statutory framework applies regardless of how casual the association appears.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Supervising brokers should build HOA disclosure compliance into their [transaction file review process](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system). At minimum, every file involving association-governed property should contain the signed RE-25 HOA Addendum, a copy of the resale certificate, and documentation of the delivery date to the buyer.

| Audit Checkpoint | What Broker Should Verify | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| HOA Addendum present | RE-25 attached and fully executed | Missing or unsigned addendum |
| Resale certificate in file | Certificate from association, not just seller's summary | Agent-prepared summary substituted for official certificate |
| Delivery date documented | Email timestamp, signed receipt, or platform log | No delivery confirmation of any kind |
| Cancellation period expired | Five days elapsed before closing | Closing scheduled within five days of delivery |
| Payment responsibility assigned | Addendum specifies who pays | Ambiguous or blank payment section |

Brokers who rely on agents to self-audit are asking for trouble. A quarterly file review that specifically flags association-property transactions will catch gaps before they become complaints. ## Staying Current With Idaho HOA & Condo Disclosure Rules 2026

The Idaho legislature periodically amends both Chapter 15 and Chapter 32, and IREC updates its guidance documents accordingly. Agents should monitor IREC bulletins and review IAR form updates each January to catch any changes to disclosure content requirements or procedural timelines. Missing a statutory amendment is not a defense in a disciplinary proceeding.

Agents who [miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) on HOA disclosures are not just inconveniencing their clients — they are creating legal exposure that can follow them for years. The **Idaho hoa & condo disclosure rules 2026** framework is specific enough that compliance should be mechanical, not aspirational. Request the certificate on day one, document every delivery, and never close without confirming the cancellation period has expired.
