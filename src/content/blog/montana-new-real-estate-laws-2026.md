## Montana New Real Estate Laws 2026: The Statutory Changes That Matter Most

The Montana Board of Realty Regulation and the 2025 legislative session produced a slate of changes that take effect between January 1 and July 1, 2026. For agents who handle even a handful of transactions a year, these Montana new real estate laws 2026 carry real financial consequences — ranging from deal rescission to license suspension. This is not a gentle refresh of existing rules.

Three bills signed by the governor directly alter daily agent workflows: Senate Bill 214 (water rights and well disclosures), House Bill 88 (trust account oversight), and House Bill 312 (agency disclosure timing). On top of those, the Board issued Administrative Rule 24.210.641 revising the standard property condition disclosure form. Together, these changes rewrite expectations around transparency, timing, and documentation in Montana residential transactions.

Agents who built muscle memory around the old sequence of disclosures and deadlines need to rebuild that muscle now. The grace period the Board informally extended through Q1 has ended. Enforcement actions have already begun appearing in the Board's June 2026 disciplinary report.

## Revised Seller Disclosure: Form REC-400R Replaces the Legacy Version

**The most immediate change agents will feel is the retirement of the old Montana Seller Property Condition Disclosure Statement.** The Board's revised Form REC-400R adds 14 new line items, including sections on prior flooding events, radon testing history, known wildfire mitigation deficiencies, and broadband infrastructure. These additions reflect both Montana's wildfire reality and buyer complaints documented in the Board's 2024 consumer survey.

Form REC-400R is mandatory for all residential sales where a disclosure is required under MCA 70-9-313. Exempt transactions — foreclosures, court-ordered sales, transfers between co-owners — remain unchanged. But for the 90-plus percent of deals that require disclosure, agents cannot substitute the old form, a brokerage-created alternative, or an out-of-state template.

The form must be delivered to the buyer or buyer's agent within five calendar days of mutual acceptance unless the purchase agreement specifies an earlier deadline. Failure to deliver within that window gives the buyer a statutory right to rescind under MCA 70-9-314 without forfeiting earnest money. That rescission right did not change, but the new form's expanded scope means more sellers will trip over incomplete answers — and agents who fail to review the form before delivery share liability.

## Water Rights and Well Disclosures Under Senate Bill 214

Montana's water law has always been a minefield for out-of-state buyers. Senate Bill 214 creates a standalone disclosure obligation for properties served by a private well or holding any appropriation water right. The new Form REC-412 (Water Rights and Well Disclosure Addendum) must accompany any listing with well water or adjudicated/permitted water rights.

| Requirement | Before SB 214 | After SB 214 (July 1, 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure form | Embedded in general disclosure | Standalone Form REC-412 |
| Delivery deadline | With general disclosure | Within 5 business days of mutual acceptance |
| Well test results | Recommended, not required | Required if test performed within 3 years |
| Water right documentation | Not mandated | Copy of abstract or permit must be provided |
| Responsible party | Seller only | Listing agent must confirm delivery |

Listing agents are now explicitly named as the party responsible for confirming delivery. This is a departure from prior practice where the seller bore sole responsibility. Under SB 214, if the buyer does not receive Form REC-412 within the statutory window, the buyer can void the contract and recover earnest money in full. The listing agent faces potential disciplinary action under MCA 37-51-321 for failing to "reasonably ensure" disclosure delivery.

For agents in the Bitterroot Valley, Gallatin County, and other areas where water rights disputes are common, this law demands a conversation with every seller at listing intake. If you work in markets where [agents already miss deadlines on simpler disclosures](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines), the SB 214 timeline will be unforgiving.

## Trust Account Rules Under House Bill 88

**House Bill 88 imposes quarterly reconciliation reporting for all brokerage trust accounts, effective January 1, 2026.** Prior to this law, the Board could request reconciliation records during an audit, but brokers had no affirmative reporting obligation. Now, every licensed brokerage holding client funds must file Form REC-520 (Trust Account Quarterly Reconciliation) with the Board within 15 days of each quarter's end.

The form requires the broker to attest that the trust account balance matches the sum of all individual client ledgers, that no commingling occurred, and that interest-bearing accounts comply with MCA 37-51-314. Penalties for late or inaccurate filings start at $500 and escalate to $2,500. Three consecutive missed filings trigger a mandatory license review hearing.

This matters for agents because brokers will push compliance downstream. Expect new internal policies requiring agents to submit earnest money receipts and disbursement authorizations within 24 hours of deposit. If your brokerage has not yet issued updated trust account procedures, that conversation needs to happen this week — not at your next quarterly meeting.

## Agency Disclosure Timing: The Shift Under House Bill 312

House Bill 312 amends MCA 37-51-313 to require agency disclosure and signed acknowledgment "before any substantive discussion of terms, price, motivation, or financial qualification." The old standard — disclosure before presentation of an offer — allowed agents to conduct extensive buyer consultations, property tours, and even pricing discussions without a signed agency acknowledgment on file.

The practical effect is that agents must now present the Montana Agency Relationships Disclosure (Form REC-200) at the beginning of first substantive contact. For buyer's agents, that means before discussing what the buyer can afford or what neighborhoods meet their criteria. For listing agents meeting a potential seller, it means before discussing pricing strategy.

| Scenario | Old Timing (pre-HB 312) | New Timing (HB 312) |
|---|---|---|
| First buyer showing | Could disclose at offer | Must disclose before showing if pricing discussed |
| Listing presentation | Before listing agreement | Before discussing price or terms |
| Open house with unrepresented buyer | Before offer | Before substantive conversation at open house |
| Dual agency possibility | Before offer | At first substantive contact with second party |

The penalty for non-compliance is not automatic deal cancellation, but it does give the aggrieved party grounds to file a complaint with the Board. Documented violations can result in mandatory continuing education, probation, or fines up to $1,500 per incident. Repeated violations put your license at risk.

## Common Mistakes Agents Are Already Making

The Board's June 2026 disciplinary summary flagged several patterns. These are not hypothetical risks — they are errors the Board is already documenting.

**First, agents are using the old Form REC-400 instead of the revised REC-400R.** Some MLSs auto-populate disclosure packets from stored templates, and not every brokerage has purged legacy forms from its document library. If a buyer's agent receives the old version, the disclosure is treated as incomplete under MCA 70-9-313, and the buyer's rescission window stays open indefinitely until a compliant form is delivered.

Second, listing agents in rural markets are ignoring the standalone water rights disclosure requirement because the property "has always had a well and everyone knows it." SB 214 does not contain a local-knowledge exception. The disclosure must be documented on Form REC-412 regardless of how obvious the water situation appears to the parties. Oral disclosure does not satisfy the statute.

Third, agents are treating the HB 312 agency disclosure timing as optional when working with past clients. The statute does not exempt repeat clients or referrals. Every new transaction requires a fresh Form REC-200 acknowledgment at first substantive contact, even if the client signed one in a prior deal.

Fourth, some agents operating on teams are delegating trust account receipt documentation to unlicensed assistants without broker authorization. Under HB 88's reporting framework, the [chain of responsibility matters more than ever](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory), and an unlicensed assistant's failure to log a deposit can cascade into a reconciliation discrepancy that triggers Board scrutiny of the entire brokerage.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Brokers carry a heightened obligation under these new laws because two of the three bills — HB 88 and HB 312 — explicitly reference supervisory responsibility. The Board's administrative rules under ARM 24.210.625 already require brokers to maintain "adequate supervisory systems." The 2026 changes define what "adequate" now looks like with more specificity.

**Brokers should audit four areas immediately.** First, confirm that every document management system — whether local or cloud-based — has purged pre-2026 disclosure forms and replaced them with REC-400R and REC-412. Second, implement a tracking mechanism for HB 312 agency disclosure timing that logs the date and context of first substantive contact, not just the date the form was signed. Third, build the quarterly Form REC-520 reconciliation into the brokerage calendar with a 10-day internal deadline (giving five days of buffer before the Board's 15-day filing window closes). Fourth, issue written policy requiring agents to submit earnest money documentation within 24 hours.

Brokers who rely on agents self-reporting compliance are exposed. The Board's [revised supervision standards](https://boards.bsd.dli.mt.gov/realty-regulation) make clear that a broker cannot avoid discipline by claiming ignorance of an agent's non-compliance if the brokerage lacked a reasonable system to detect it. Spot-check disclosure packets monthly. Pull a random sample of closed files quarterly and verify form versions, delivery dates, and acknowledgment signatures.

If your brokerage manages more than 50 transactions a year and still tracks compliance through email threads and shared drives, the operational risk just increased significantly. That unofficial grace period for the January 1 statutes has expired. The July 1 changes — including Form REC-400R and Form REC-412 — will see their grace period close by year-end. After that, agents should expect the Board to enforce Montana new real estate laws 2026 with the same rigor it applies to trust account violations and unauthorized practice.
