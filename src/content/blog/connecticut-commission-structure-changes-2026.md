## Connecticut Commission Structure Rules After the NAR Settlement 2026

The Connecticut commission structure rules after the NAR settlement 2026 have fundamentally changed how agents on both sides of a transaction earn and document their fees. If you hold a license issued by the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection (DCP) and you have not overhauled your compensation paperwork since August 2024, you are already exposed. The settlement's practice changes rippled through SmartMLS policies, state regulatory guidance, and standard-form revisions issued by the Connecticut Association of Realtors (CAR), creating a compliance framework that punishes vagueness.

Connecticut did not pass a standalone statute mirroring the NAR settlement terms. Instead, existing law under Connecticut General Statutes Section 20-325a through 20-325f — which already mandated written agency agreements — became the enforcement backbone. The DCP has signaled through advisory bulletins that any failure to disclose compensation terms in writing triggers the same penalties as an undisclosed dual agency: license suspension, fines up to $25,000 per violation, and mandatory continuing education.

## Written Buyer Representation Is No Longer Optional

**Before you open a lockbox in Connecticut, you need a signed buyer representation agreement in your file.** The CAR Exclusive Buyer Representation Agreement (Form CAR-101, revised January 2026) is the standard vehicle. It specifies the agent's compensation amount or rate, the duration of representation, and the services the buyer will receive.

The form requires agents to fill in a specific dollar amount or percentage — blanks are not acceptable. The DCP's February 2026 advisory bulletin clarified that writing "to be determined" or referencing a future MLS offer of compensation does not satisfy the written-agreement requirement under C.G.S. Section 20-325a(b).

Agents who work with buyers casually — answering questions at an open house, sending a listing link — sometimes argue that no formal representation existed. Connecticut case law from *Revere Real Estate, Inc. v. Cerato* (2019) already established that even informal buyer-agent relationships can trigger fiduciary duties, making written agreements protective for both parties.

## How Compensation Flows Under the New Rules

The old model was simple: the listing broker set a cooperating commission in SmartMLS, and the buyer's agent collected it at closing without the buyer ever writing a check. That pipeline no longer exists in its traditional form.

Under current SmartMLS rules (effective August 13, 2024), no field in the listing input allows an offer of buyer-agent compensation. Sellers can still choose to pay the buyer's agent, but that arrangement must be negotiated outside the MLS and documented in the purchase contract or a separate compensation agreement.

| Scenario | Who Pays Buyer's Agent | Required Documentation |
|----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Buyer pays directly | Buyer | CAR-101 specifying amount; reflected on Closing Disclosure |
| Seller concession covers fee | Seller (indirectly) | Purchase contract addendum; CAR-101 still required |
| Seller agrees in separate agreement | Seller (directly) | Off-MLS compensation agreement + CAR-101 |
| Split arrangement | Both buyer and seller | CAR-101 + purchase contract language + closing instructions |

This means every transaction now requires at least two written instruments addressing compensation: the buyer representation agreement and the purchase contract terms (or separate seller-to-buyer-agent agreement). Missing either one opens the door to post-closing disputes and regulatory complaints.

## Common Mistakes Connecticut Agents Are Making Right Now

After eighteen months of living under the new framework, patterns of non-compliance have emerged. The Connecticut Real Estate Commission's 2025-2026 disciplinary docket shows a spike in compensation-related complaints, and most stem from avoidable errors.

**First, agents are using outdated CAR forms.** The pre-2024 buyer representation agreement (prior CAR-100 series) did not contain the specificity language the settlement requires. If your brokerage's transaction management system still auto-populates the old form, every file you submit is deficient. Second, some buyer's agents are verbally telling clients that "the seller will probably pay" without documenting what happens if the seller refuses. When the seller declines to offer a concession, the buyer learns at the contract stage that they owe their agent a fee — and files a complaint alleging non-disclosure.

Third, listing agents are embedding compensation offers in the MLS "Agent Remarks" field, which SmartMLS explicitly prohibits. Fines from SmartMLS start at $500 and escalate to suspension of MLS privileges. Fourth, agents are failing to update the buyer agreement when terms change mid-transaction — for example, when a buyer initially agrees to pay 2.5% but later negotiates a seller concession that covers half. The original CAR-101 should be amended via the CAR Amendment to Buyer Representation Agreement (Form CAR-101A) to reflect the revised terms.

Fifth, dual agents are not obtaining separate written consent for the compensation structure from both parties, as required under C.G.S. Section 20-325b. The [risks of missing compliance deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) multiply when agents handle both sides without documenting each party's agreement to fee arrangements.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Designated brokers in Connecticut bear personal liability for their agents' compliance failures under C.G.S. Section 20-325f. The DCP does not limit enforcement to the individual licensee — the supervising broker's license is equally at risk when systemic deficiencies exist.

**Brokers should audit every active file for three specific documents:** a signed CAR-101 dated before the first showing, proof that compensation terms appear in the executed purchase contract or a standalone compensation agreement, and confirmation that SmartMLS listing entries contain no prohibited compensation language in any field. A quarterly audit cadence is insufficient given the volume of regulatory changes; monthly reviews of open and recently closed files provide better protection.

| Audit Item | Form/Document | Red Flag If Missing |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Buyer agreement before touring | CAR-101 (2026 revision) | Potential C.G.S. 20-325a violation |
| Compensation amount specified | Dollar amount or percentage in CAR-101 | DCP advisory bulletin non-compliance |
| MLS listing compliance | SmartMLS listing input | No compensation language in any field |
| Purchase contract compensation terms | CAR Purchase Contract Section 12 or addendum | Closing agent cannot disburse without written authority |
| Dual agency compensation consent | CAR Dual Agency Disclosure (Form CAR-201) | C.G.S. 20-325b violation |

Brokers who discover deficiencies should not simply "fix forward." The DCP has indicated in enforcement actions that retroactive corrections without disclosure to affected consumers may constitute an additional violation. Documented remediation — including consumer notification — is the safer path, and [building systematic compliance checks into every file](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) reduces the chance of gaps forming in the first place.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance Are Steep

The Connecticut Real Estate Commission operates under broad disciplinary authority granted by C.G.S. Section 20-320. Violations related to compensation disclosure fall under the "untrustworthiness or incompetence" standard, which does not require proof of intent — negligence is sufficient.

Penalties include license revocation or suspension, civil penalties up to $25,000 per transaction, mandatory additional continuing education hours, and public disciplinary records that appear on the [DCP license lookup portal](https://www.elicense.ct.gov/Lookup/LicenseLookup.aspx). Beyond regulatory consequences, agents face civil exposure: buyers who discover undisclosed compensation arrangements can sue for breach of fiduciary duty and seek rescission of the transaction under Connecticut's Unfair Trade Practices Act (CUTPA), C.G.S. Section 42-110a et seq.

Deal cancellation is a real possibility when compensation paperwork is deficient. Title companies and closing attorneys in Connecticut increasingly require written evidence of all commission disbursement authority before scheduling a closing. If the file lacks a properly executed CAR-101 and corresponding purchase contract language, expect delays — or outright refusal to close.

## Practical Steps to Stay Compliant Through 2026 and Beyond

Build compensation discussions into your first substantive conversation with any buyer or seller prospect. For buyers, present the CAR-101 as non-negotiable prerequisite paperwork — not something to handle "when we find a house." For sellers, explain during the listing appointment that their decision about whether to offer buyer-agent compensation affects contract negotiations and should be documented in the listing agreement (CAR Exclusive Right to Sell, Form CAR-301) even if it cannot appear on SmartMLS.

Track form revisions issued by CAR, which updates its standard forms on a January/July cycle. The January 2026 revision to CAR-101 added a mandatory disclosure paragraph about the source of compensation and a checkbox for the buyer to acknowledge that fees are not set by law or any trade organization. Using an older version after the effective date is a compliance failure regardless of whether the substance of your agreement is correct.
