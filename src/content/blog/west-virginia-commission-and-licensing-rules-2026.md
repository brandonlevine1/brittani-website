## Understanding West Virginia Commission & Licensing Rules 2026

The **West Virginia commission & licensing rules 2026** framework governs how every active licensee earns, discloses, and receives compensation in the state. The West Virginia Real Estate Commission (WVREC), operating under W. Va. Code Chapter 30, Article 40, serves as the regulatory body that enforces these standards. Agents and brokers who misunderstand or ignore these rules face penalties ranging from license suspension to civil liability that can unwind a closed transaction.

This year's regulatory environment reflects both longstanding state law and post-NAR settlement realities. The WVREC has issued updated guidance on buyer representation agreements, commission disclosure timing, and advertising compliance that directly affects how agents structure deals. Failing to adapt is not just risky—it is expensive.

## Licensing Structure and Renewal Requirements

West Virginia operates a two-tier licensing system: salesperson and broker. A salesperson license requires 90 hours of pre-licensing education, passage of both the national and state exam portions, and affiliation with a licensed broker. Broker applicants must hold an active salesperson license for at least two years and complete 90 additional hours of broker-level coursework.

**Renewal deadlines in West Virginia follow a biennial cycle, with all licenses expiring on June 30 of even-numbered years.** The 2026 renewal deadline therefore falls on June 30, 2026. Late renewals within 30 days incur a $50 penalty fee; after 30 days, the license enters inactive status and the agent must petition the WVREC for reinstatement.

Continuing education requires 14 hours per renewal period. The WVREC designates specific mandatory core topics each cycle—for 2025-2026, these include agency law updates, fair housing, and commission disclosure post-settlement changes. The remaining hours may be elective, but must come from WVREC-approved providers listed on the Commission's official roster.

| Requirement | Salesperson | Broker |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-licensing education | 90 hours | 90 additional hours |
| Experience prerequisite | None | 2 years active as salesperson |
| Continuing education (biennial) | 14 hours | 14 hours |
| Mandatory core topics | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| Renewal deadline | June 30, 2026 | June 30, 2026 |
| Late renewal penalty | $50 (within 30 days) | $50 (within 30 days) |

## Commission Agreements and Written Requirements

West Virginia law is explicit: no licensee may claim a commission without a written brokerage agreement. W. Va. Code 30-40-19 requires that the terms of compensation be documented before services are rendered. This applies to both listing agreements and buyer representation contracts.

The WVREC-approved forms include Form REC-LB (Listing Brokerage Agreement) and Form REC-BBA (Buyer Brokerage Agreement). Since 2024, the Commission has required that buyer brokerage agreements specify the exact amount or rate of compensation the buyer's agent will receive, rather than referencing a cooperative offer from the listing side. Agents using outdated forms that lack this specificity risk having their compensation deemed unenforceable.

**Commission cannot flow directly to a salesperson—it must pass through the employing broker's account.** W. Va. Code 30-40-20(a)(10) makes it a violation for any licensee to receive compensation from anyone other than their broker. This means even referral fees from out-of-state sources must be paid to the West Virginia broker first, who then disburses to the agent per their independent contractor agreement.

The timing of commission disclosure matters for [closing procedures in West Virginia](/blog/west-virginia-title-and-closing-procedures-2026). All compensation must appear on the settlement statement, and any changes to commission terms after the original agreement require a written amendment signed by all parties.

## Post-NAR Settlement: How West Virginia Adapted

The nationwide settlement fundamentally changed how buyer-agent compensation is communicated. In West Virginia, the WVREC responded by issuing Advisory Opinion 2024-03, which clarifies that offers of buyer-broker compensation may not appear in MLS fields but can be communicated through listing agreements, direct broker-to-broker communication, or the purchase agreement itself.

Agents must now present Form REC-BBA to buyer clients before showing properties, disclosing how much the buyer's agent expects to earn and identifying who will pay it. If a seller offers compensation that differs from what the buyer agreed to pay their agent, the agent must disclose the discrepancy and obtain written consent before accepting the different amount.

This creates a documentation chain that did not exist before 2024. Every transaction now requires a paper trail showing the buyer agreed to a specific compensation structure, and that any deviation was authorized. Agents who skip this step expose their brokers to complaints filed under W. Va. Code 30-40-27, which allows the WVREC to investigate and sanction without a formal court proceeding.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

The WVREC maintains broad enforcement authority under W. Va. Code 30-40-28. Sanctions range from formal reprimand to license revocation, with monetary penalties up to $5,000 per violation. Repeat offenders face increased scrutiny, and the Commission publishes disciplinary actions in its quarterly bulletin.

**Practicing without a valid license—even inadvertently due to a lapsed renewal—constitutes a misdemeanor under West Virginia law.** Beyond criminal exposure, any commission earned during an unlicensed period is subject to disgorgement. The buyer or seller can also seek rescission of the contract if they can demonstrate the agent's unlicensed status materially affected the transaction.

Civil liability compounds the regulatory risk. If an agent receives an undisclosed commission or kickback, the affected party may sue for damages under both W. Va. Code 30-40-19 and common law fraud. Courts have consistently held that violations of the licensing act create a per se breach of fiduciary duty, shifting the burden of proof to the agent.

| Violation | Potential Penalty |
|---|---|
| Practicing on expired license | Misdemeanor charge, commission disgorgement, up to $5,000 fine |
| Accepting commission not through broker | License suspension, $2,500-$5,000 fine |
| Missing written brokerage agreement | Commission unenforceable, potential civil suit |
| Undisclosed dual compensation | License revocation, civil damages, RESPA violation |
| Failure to complete CE by deadline | License inactivation, $50 late fee, potential lapse |

These penalties intersect with [who pays closing costs in West Virginia](/blog/west-virginia-who-pays-closing-costs-2026), because improperly disclosed commission can trigger disputes about the settlement statement's accuracy. Title companies will flag discrepancies, potentially delaying closing.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make in West Virginia

**First: using outdated buyer representation forms.** Agents who rely on pre-2024 versions of the BBA that reference MLS cooperative compensation fields are using forms the WVREC no longer considers compliant. The current Form REC-BBA (revised January 2025) requires a specific dollar amount or percentage that the buyer agrees to pay, with a separate line indicating whether the buyer authorizes their agent to seek seller-paid compensation.

Second: failing to terminate brokerage agreements in writing. W. Va. Code 30-40-19(c) requires that termination of any brokerage relationship be documented with Form REC-TB (Termination of Brokerage). Agents who simply stop communicating with a client without executing this form remain in a legal agency relationship—and potentially owe fiduciary duties they are no longer fulfilling. This is where [missed deadlines create real liability](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines).

Third: advertising compensation guarantees. The WVREC's advertising rules under Legislative Rule 174 CSR 1 prohibit licensees from guaranteeing a specific commission outcome in marketing materials. Phrases like "guaranteed 3% buyer agent commission" violate the rule because compensation is always negotiable and contingent on a written agreement.

Fourth: depositing earnest money into personal accounts before transferring to the broker. W. Va. Code 30-40-20(a)(8) requires all transaction funds to be deposited in the broker's trust account within one business day of receipt. Agents who hold checks over a weekend without notifying their broker create commingling exposure that can result in immediate license suspension.

Fifth: treating team members as independent brokers. Salespersons operating within a team cannot independently enter into brokerage agreements or accept compensation from outside their sponsoring broker's structure. Every team member's commission must flow through the broker, regardless of internal team splits.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Brokers bear supervisory liability under W. Va. Code 30-40-22. The WVREC holds brokers accountable for the actions of their affiliated licensees, meaning a single agent's violation can trigger sanctions against the entire brokerage.

**Brokers should conduct quarterly audits of three specific areas: trust account reconciliation, active brokerage agreement files, and continuing education compliance tracking.** Trust accounts must be reconciled monthly under 174 CSR 1, Section 8, and brokers must retain bank statements and reconciliation records for five years. The WVREC conducts random trust account audits and has increased their frequency since 2024.

Every active transaction file must contain a signed Form REC-BBA or Form REC-LB before any substantive services begin. Brokers who discover agents working without signed agreements should issue immediate written corrective action and document it in the agent's personnel file. The WVREC considers a pattern of missing agreements evidence of inadequate supervision.

For CE compliance, brokers should pull licensee records from the WVREC's online portal at least 90 days before the June 30, 2026 renewal deadline. Any agent short on hours needs immediate notification. If an agent's license lapses, the broker must remove them from all active transactions that same day—there is no grace period for continued practice.

Brokers should also review all advertising materials quarterly. Social media posts, email signatures, and website bios must include the agent's full licensed name and the brokerage name as registered with the WVREC. Abbreviations or team names used without the brokerage identifier violate 174 CSR 1, Section 6.
