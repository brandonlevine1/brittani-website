## Why Wisconsin HOA & Condo Disclosure Rules 2026 Demand Your Attention

Every condominium and planned-community sale in Wisconsin carries a disclosure obligation that sits entirely outside the standard seller condition report. The Wisconsin hoa & condo disclosure rules 2026 framework is built on Wis. Stat. Chapter 703 (the Wisconsin Condominium Ownership Act) and supplemented by the Wisconsin Real Estate Examining Board's administrative rules under Chapter REEB 24. Agents who treat these disclosures as optional—or who delegate them without tracking delivery—expose their clients and their own licenses to measurable risk.

The stakes are not abstract. A buyer who never receives the required resale package retains an open-ended right to rescind, even after closing funds have been disbursed. The Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services (DSPS) can investigate licensees who fail to ensure compliance, and civil liability for damages flows freely from missing or incomplete documents.

## The Statutory Framework: Wis. Stat. 703.33

**Wisconsin's condominium resale disclosure statute is unusually prescriptive compared to neighboring states.** Section 703.33 requires the seller of a condominium unit to furnish the prospective buyer with a written disclosure package before or at the time the purchase agreement is signed—or within the timeframe stated in the contract itself. The package must include specific financial, governance, and physical-condition information about the association.

Required contents under 703.33(2) include the declaration, bylaws, rules and regulations, the current operating budget, a statement of any pending special assessments, the most recent audited financial statement or annual report, the amount of reserves, and any known pending litigation involving the association. The statute also requires disclosure of any "right of first refusal" or other transfer restrictions that could affect the buyer's ability to close.

For planned communities governed by homeowner associations outside Chapter 703 (sometimes organized under Wis. Stat. Chapter 181 as nonstock corporations), no identical statutory resale-disclosure mandate exists. However, the WB-11 Residential Offer to Purchase, published by the Wisconsin Department of Administration, contains provisions that contractually require HOA document delivery and grant the buyer inspection and objection rights tied to those documents.

## Forms Agents Must Know

Wisconsin agents work with state-approved forms published by the Wisconsin Department of Administration (not the Wisconsin REALTORS Association, though WRA provides addenda). The table below maps the key forms to their function in HOA and condo transactions.

| Form Number | Title | Function in HOA/Condo Sales |
|---|---|---|
| WB-11 | Residential Offer to Purchase | Contains lines 337-362 addressing condominium/HOA document delivery, inspection periods, and buyer objection rights |
| WB-14 | Residential Condominium Offer to Purchase | Specifically designed for condo resales; incorporates 703.33 disclosure obligations directly into the offer |
| WB-40 | Amendment to Offer | Used to extend document-delivery deadlines when association is slow to respond |
| WRA Addendum C | Condominium Addendum | WRA-drafted addendum that expands on statutory requirements; not state-mandated but widely used |

**Agents listing condominiums should default to the WB-14, not the WB-11.** Using the wrong form does not eliminate the statutory obligation, but it does remove built-in contract language that protects both parties and structures the rescission timeline. The Wisconsin Real Estate Examining Board has repeatedly reminded licensees in its [newsletter and enforcement bulletins](https://dsps.wi.gov/pages/BoardsCouncils/RealEstateExaminers/Default.aspx) that form selection is a competency requirement under REEB 24.

## Rescission Rights and Timing

The buyer's rescission window is the single most deal-critical element of Wisconsin's condo disclosure framework. Under Wis. Stat. 703.33(5), the buyer has five business days from receipt of the complete disclosure package to rescind the offer by delivering written notice to the seller or the seller's agent. If the package is delivered before or at the time the offer is accepted, the clock starts at acceptance.

Here is where agents frequently miscalculate: the clock does not start until the package is complete. A partial delivery—say, bylaws and budget but no litigation disclosure—does not trigger the five-day period. The buyer's right to cancel remains open until every required element has been provided.

| Delivery Scenario | Rescission Clock Starts | Practical Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Full package delivered before offer acceptance | At acceptance | Low—timeline is predictable |
| Full package delivered three days after acceptance | Day of actual receipt | Moderate—buyer may already be arranging financing |
| Partial package delivered; remainder follows later | Day the final missing item is received | High—buyer can rescind weeks into the transaction |
| Package never delivered | Never starts | Extreme—buyer can rescind even after closing |

This open-ended exposure is why agents must document delivery with a signed receipt or tracked electronic delivery confirmation. A verbal "I sent it" will not survive a dispute.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make

Five errors appear repeatedly in DSPS enforcement actions and civil litigation involving Wisconsin condo and HOA transactions.

**First, agents assume the association's management company will handle everything.** Management companies respond to the seller or their agent's request, but they have no independent duty to the buyer. If the management company sends an incomplete package, the listing agent who forwards it without verifying completeness has not satisfied the statute. The agent should cross-reference the 703.33(2) checklist before marking the item complete.

Second, buyer's agents fail to calendar the rescission deadline. The five-business-day window is short, and if a buyer wants to object to something in the financials—say, underfunded reserves or upcoming special assessments—the agent needs to advise the buyer of the deadline in writing. Missing it means the buyer waives rescission rights.

Third, agents use the WB-11 for a condominium sale instead of the WB-14. The WB-11 addresses HOA documents in general terms, but it does not mirror the statutory requirements of Chapter 703. Agents who write condo offers on the WB-11 create ambiguity about which rescission rights apply and may inadvertently shorten or eliminate their buyer's statutory protections.

Fourth, listing agents fail to request the resale package early enough. Most Wisconsin management companies quote 7 to 14 business days for preparation. If the agent waits until after an accepted offer, the delay compresses the buyer's inspection period and can push the rescission window past the financing contingency deadline, creating conflicting timelines that [lead to deal breakdowns](/blog/where-deals-break-down).

Fifth, agents confuse the 703.33 rescission right with the general inspection contingency in the offer. These are separate legal mechanisms. A buyer can exercise the statutory rescission even if they have already waived their inspection contingency, because the condo disclosure right exists independently of the contract's inspection provisions.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Supervising brokers carry liability under Wis. Stat. 452.14 for the acts of their licensees. When a condo or HOA transaction goes sideways because of disclosure failures, DSPS routinely names both the agent and the broker in its complaint. Brokers should build three specific audit points into their transaction-review process.

**First, verify form selection at offer submission.** A quick check that condo units are written on the WB-14 (or that the WB-11 includes the WRA Condominium Addendum C) eliminates the most common structural error. This takes seconds and prevents weeks of downstream confusion.

Second, require a delivery receipt in every condo file before the transaction moves past attorney review. The receipt should confirm the date the buyer received the complete package and should list each document included. If your [team supervision structure](/blog/wisconsin-teams-supervision-rules-2026) delegates file review to a transaction coordinator, make sure the TC knows that a missing delivery receipt is a hard stop, not a soft reminder.

Third, audit rescission-deadline calculations quarterly. Pull a sample of closed condo files and confirm that the five-business-day window was correctly calculated from actual delivery—not from offer acceptance—and that no file shows a rescission deadline that predates complete delivery. This audit takes an hour and catches systemic training gaps before they produce a DSPS complaint.

## Liability When Compliance Fails

The consequences of non-compliance operate on three levels. Contractually, the buyer retains rescission rights indefinitely if the package was never delivered. Courts have enforced post-closing rescission in Wisconsin when buyers proved they never received the 703.33 disclosures, resulting in unwinding of the sale and return of the purchase price.

At the regulatory level, DSPS can impose disciplinary action under Wis. Stat. 452.14, including license suspension, formal reprimand, mandatory education, and fines. The Real Estate Examining Board treats failure to ensure proper disclosure delivery as incompetent practice under REEB 24.075.

At the civil level, both buyers and sellers can pursue damages against agents who caused harm through negligent handling of disclosure obligations. Listing agents who failed to order or verify documents, and buyer's agents who failed to advise clients of rescission rights, have both been named in Wisconsin civil actions. Errors and omissions insurance may cover defense costs, but it rarely covers regulatory fines—and it never covers reputational damage.

## Practical Workflow for 2026 Compliance

Building compliance into your listing-side workflow starts on the day you take the listing. Order the resale package from the management company or association secretary immediately—before you go to market. The [Wisconsin agency disclosure timeline](/blog/wisconsin-agency-disclosure-requirements-2026) already requires early paperwork delivery, so folding the HOA request into your first-week tasks creates no additional burden.

On the buy side, confirm receipt in writing and calendar the five-business-day rescission window with a one-day advance reminder. Review the financials yourself or ensure your buyer has access to someone who can interpret reserve studies and special-assessment histories. If you spot issues—underfunded reserves, pending litigation, transfer restrictions—raise them before the rescission window closes, not during the [contingency deadline negotiation](/blog/wisconsin-buyer-contingency-deadlines-2026).
