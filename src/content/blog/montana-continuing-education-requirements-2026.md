## Understanding Montana Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Montana real estate agents face a straightforward but unforgiving continuing education structure. The Montana continuing education requirements 2026 cycle demands that every active licensee complete 12 hours of approved coursework before their license renewal date. Miss the deadline by even one day and your license drops to inactive status, halting every transaction you touch.

The Montana Board of Realty Regulation, which operates under the Department of Labor and Industry, administers these requirements through the authority granted in Montana Code Annotated (MCA) Title 37, Chapter 51. The Board has made no significant changes to the hour totals for the 2026 cycle, but enforcement actions have increased over the past two years, particularly around documentation and course approval verification.

**Every Montana licensee renews on a staggered two-year cycle tied to their birth year.** If your birth year is even, you renewed in 2024 and will renew again in 2026. Odd birth years renewed in 2025 and will next renew in 2027. This means roughly half the state's licensed agents face a renewal deadline in 2026, making this the year to get your house in order.

## The 12-Hour Breakdown: Core vs. Elective Requirements

Montana's 12-hour CE requirement splits into two categories. Two hours must come from a Board-designated core course that changes each cycle, covering legislative updates, rule changes, and enforcement trends. The remaining 10 hours are elective, drawn from any course approved by the Board.

The core course for the 2026 renewal cycle focuses on Montana's updated agency disclosure rules and property condition reporting standards. Agents must complete this specific course; no substitutions are accepted regardless of how many elective hours you stack up. The Board publishes the approved core course provider list on the [Montana Board of Realty Regulation website](https://boards.bsd.dli.mt.gov/realty-regulation).

Elective hours can cover topics ranging from fair housing updates to contract law, ethics, risk management, and property management. Montana does not mandate specific elective topics, but agents should note that courses must be pre-approved by the Board. Taking a course from an unapproved provider yields zero credit, even if the content is relevant.

| Requirement | Hours | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Core Course | 2 | Board-designated topic; no substitutions |
| Electives | 10 | Any Board-approved course or provider |
| Total Required | 12 | Must be completed before renewal date |
| Carryover Hours | 0 | Montana does not allow hour banking |
| Online Courses | Permitted | Provider must hold Board approval |

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

The consequences of non-compliance in Montana escalate quickly. Under MCA 37-51-321, the Board can impose fines up to $500 per violation, and practicing on an inactive license carries separate disciplinary action. An agent who closes a deal while their license is inactive exposes themselves, their brokerage, and potentially their clients to legal liability.

**A lapsed license does not just affect the agent; it can unwind a transaction.** If a buyer or seller discovers post-closing that their agent was unlicensed at the time of the deal, they may have grounds to challenge the commission payment or, in extreme cases, seek rescission. Montana courts have addressed this issue under general contract law principles, holding that unlicensed practice voids the agent's right to compensation.

Reinstatement requires completing all outstanding CE, paying a reinstatement fee (currently $150 in addition to the standard renewal fee), and submitting Form RER-2 to the Board. The Board processes reinstatements within 10 to 15 business days, meaning an agent could be sidelined for nearly a month during their busiest season. This timeline should concern anyone managing active listings or pending contracts, and it underscores [why agents miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) more often than they'd like to admit.

## Common Mistakes Montana Agents Make With CE

Five errors surface repeatedly in Board enforcement records and brokerage audits. Avoiding them requires attention to detail, not additional coursework.

**First, agents complete courses from unapproved providers.** This happens most often with national online platforms that hold approvals in other states but not Montana. The Board maintains a searchable provider database, and checking it before enrollment takes under a minute. Agents who skip this step discover the problem only when their renewal application is rejected.

Second, agents confuse the core course with an elective on a similar topic. Taking a general "legislative update" elective does not satisfy the core requirement. The core course has a specific Board-assigned course number, and only providers authorized to deliver that exact course can issue credit for it.

Third, agents fail to retain completion certificates. Montana requires licensees to keep certificates for at least four years beyond the renewal cycle in which they were earned, per Administrative Rules of Montana (ARM) 24.210.641. The Board can audit any licensee at any time, and "I completed it but lost the certificate" is not a defense.

Fourth, agents wait until the final month before their renewal deadline to begin coursework. Providers occasionally lose Board approval mid-cycle, course availability fluctuates, and technical problems with online platforms happen at the worst possible times. Starting CE at least 90 days before your renewal date eliminates most of these risks.

Fifth, agents assume their broker is tracking compliance on their behalf. While brokers have supervisory obligations, the individual licensee bears ultimate responsibility for their own CE completion and license status under MCA 37-51-303.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Designated brokers in Montana carry explicit supervisory duties under ARM 24.210.641 and MCA 37-51-313. The Board holds brokers accountable for maintaining a roster of actively licensed agents and verifying that no one under their supervision practices on an expired or inactive license.

| Broker Obligation | Regulatory Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Verify agent license status | MCA 37-51-313 | Before each transaction |
| Maintain CE completion records | ARM 24.210.641 | Ongoing; retain 4 years |
| Report inactive agents to Board | ARM 24.210.625 | Within 10 days of discovery |
| Supervise advertising of licensed status | MCA 37-51-321 | Ongoing |
| Submit Form RER-5 for agent changes | Board requirement | Within 5 business days |

**Brokers who fail to audit their agents' CE status face their own disciplinary exposure.** The Board can fine a brokerage up to $500 per instance and, in severe cases, suspend the broker's license entirely. This creates a cascade effect: if a designated broker loses their license, every agent under that broker's supervision must immediately transfer to another brokerage or cease practice.

Smart brokerages build CE tracking into their quarterly compliance reviews. They request copies of completion certificates from every agent by a set internal deadline, typically 60 days before the earliest renewal date in their office. ## Forms and Filing: The Paperwork Side of Renewal

Montana's renewal process requires agents to submit Form RER-1 (License Renewal Application) along with proof of CE completion and the renewal fee. The fee for salesperson renewal is currently $200, while broker renewal costs $250. These forms are available through the Board's online portal, and electronic filing is the default method.

If an agent's CE is incomplete at the time of filing, the Board will not process the renewal. There is no grace period and no conditional approval. The agent's license expires on the renewal date and immediately moves to inactive status.

For agents seeking reinstatement after a lapse, Form RER-2 (Reinstatement Application) requires a detailed accounting of all completed CE, including course titles, provider names, approval numbers, and dates of completion. The Board cross-references this information against its provider database before issuing reinstatement. Errors or inconsistencies on Form RER-2 result in additional delays that can stretch the process beyond 30 days.

Brokers processing agent transfers or terminations use Form RER-5 (Change of Status Notification), which must be filed within five business days of the change. This form does not directly relate to CE compliance, but brokers frequently discover CE deficiencies during the transfer process when the receiving brokerage runs its own verification.

## How Montana Compares to Neighboring States

Montana's 12-hour requirement sits on the lower end compared to its neighbors, which may create a false sense of simplicity for agents licensed in multiple states. The structure differences matter more than the raw numbers.

| Idaho | 12 | 2 years | Yes | No |

Agents who hold [Wisconsin commission and licensing credentials](/blog/wisconsin-commission-and-licensing-rules-2026) alongside their Montana license need to track both states' deadlines independently. No reciprocity agreement between Montana and any neighboring state allows CE hours completed in one jurisdiction to automatically count in another. Each state's Board must independently approve any course before it generates credit.

## Planning Your 2026 CE Strategy

The most effective approach is completing all 12 hours in a concentrated window rather than spreading coursework across the full two-year cycle. Montana does not award partial credit for incomplete courses, so a half-finished six-hour class on the day before your deadline counts for nothing.

Build your CE schedule around three anchors: complete the core course first (it has the fewest provider options and fills up fastest), knock out elective hours during your slowest transaction month, and submit your renewal application at least 30 days before your deadline. This buffer accounts for Board processing times, certificate delivery delays, and any discrepancies that require correction.
