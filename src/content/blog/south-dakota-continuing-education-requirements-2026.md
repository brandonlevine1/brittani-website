## Understanding South Dakota Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Missing a continuing education deadline in South Dakota does not just mean a lapsed license — it means every deal you touch becomes a potential liability bomb. The South Dakota continuing education requirements 2026 carry real consequences for agents and brokers who fall behind, and the state's regulatory framework leaves little room for excuses. The South Dakota Real Estate Commission (SDREC), operating under SDCL Title 36, Chapter 36-21A, enforces these requirements with the kind of precision that punishes procrastinators.

South Dakota runs on a biennial renewal cycle. Every two years, licensed salespersons and brokers must complete 24 hours of approved continuing education to maintain active status. The current cycle that matters most to agents reading this runs through November 30, 2026, and the clock is already ticking.

## The 24-Hour Breakdown: Mandatory vs. Elective Hours

**Not all 24 hours are created equal, and confusing the two categories is one of the fastest ways to end up short at renewal time.** The SDREC requires a specific split between mandatory and elective coursework that agents must follow precisely.

The mandatory portion consists of a 2-hour Commission Update course developed and approved by the SDREC each cycle. This course covers recent changes to South Dakota real estate law, administrative rules under ARSD 20:69, and any new forms or disclosure requirements issued by the Commission. You cannot substitute this with a generic ethics or legal course from another state.

The remaining 22 hours come from elective courses approved by the SDREC. These can cover topics like contract law, property management, fair housing, agency relationships, or real estate finance. Agents may complete electives through classroom instruction or [approved online providers listed on the Commission's website](https://dlr.sd.gov/real_estate/default.aspx). The table below shows how the hours stack up.

| Requirement | Hours | Format | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commission Update (mandatory) | 2 | Classroom or approved online | November 30, 2026 |
| Elective coursework | 22 | Classroom or approved online | November 30, 2026 |
| Total CE required | 24 | Mixed | November 30, 2026 |
| Late renewal grace period | N/A | None — license lapses | N/A |

South Dakota does not offer a grace period for late CE completion. When the deadline passes, your license lapses immediately.

## What Happens When an Agent Fails to Comply

The consequences of noncompliance extend well beyond a stern letter from the SDREC. Under SDCL 36-21A-22, the Commission has authority to deny renewal, suspend, or revoke a license for failure to meet CE requirements. An agent whose license lapses cannot legally conduct real estate transactions — period.

**Any transaction initiated or closed by an agent operating on a lapsed license creates enormous liability for the agent, the broker, and potentially the parties to the deal.** Buyers and sellers could challenge the enforceability of contracts facilitated by an unlicensed individual. Courts have found that contracts brokered by unlicensed agents may be voidable, meaning a disgruntled party could seek to unwind a deal entirely.

Financial penalties compound the problem. Reinstatement after a lapse requires completing all outstanding CE, submitting Form 101 (Application for License Renewal), and paying reinstatement fees that exceed standard renewal costs. If the lapse exceeds a certain period, the Commission may require the agent to retake the state licensing examination — essentially starting over. For brokers, allowing a lapsed agent to operate under their license can trigger disciplinary action under SDCL 36-21A-39, which governs broker supervisory responsibilities.

The reputational damage is harder to quantify but equally real. A single deal that unravels because of a licensing lapse can generate complaints to the SDREC, E&O insurance claims, and the kind of attention no agent wants from the state attorney general's office.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With South Dakota CE

Patterns of noncompliance tend to repeat themselves cycle after cycle. The SDREC sees the same errors from agents who genuinely believed they were in good standing. Here are five specific mistakes that trip up South Dakota agents.

**First, agents confuse approved courses with non-approved courses.** Just because a course covers real estate topics does not mean the SDREC has approved it for CE credit. Agents who complete courses through non-approved providers — or take courses approved in another state but not in South Dakota — discover at renewal time that those hours count for nothing. Always verify the course approval number with the SDREC before enrolling.

Second, agents complete the 22 elective hours but skip the mandatory 2-hour Commission Update. The SDREC will not accept 24 hours of electives in place of 22 electives plus the 2-hour mandatory course. This distinction catches agents who batch their CE in a single weekend without reading the requirements carefully.

Third, agents fail to retain certificates of completion. The SDREC requires that agents keep proof of course completion for at least one full renewal cycle after the CE was earned. When audit letters arrive, agents who tossed their certificates or relied on a provider that went out of business find themselves unable to prove compliance. Track your certificates the same way you track [why checklists beat memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) in transaction management — systematically and without relying on recall.

Fourth, agents assume their broker is tracking CE for them. While brokers have supervisory obligations, the legal responsibility for completing CE rests with each individual licensee. Agents who delegate this responsibility entirely to their office manager are playing a dangerous game.

Fifth, agents wait until the final weeks before the November 30 deadline and find that popular courses are full, online platforms experience high traffic slowdowns, or personal emergencies leave them unable to finish. The SDREC does not grant deadline extensions for procrastination or scheduling conflicts.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers in South Dakota carry a supervisory obligation that goes beyond collecting commission splits. Under SDCL 36-21A-39 and ARSD 20:69:04, designated brokers must exercise reasonable supervision over every licensee affiliated with their office. That includes monitoring CE compliance.

| Broker Responsibility | How to Verify | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Confirm active license status | Check SDREC online license lookup | Quarterly, minimum |
| Verify CE completion before renewal | Request certificates or SDREC transcript | Annually |
| Ensure mandatory Commission Update is included | Review CE breakdown by course type | Before renewal submission |
| Audit new affiliates' CE status | Request SDREC transcript at onboarding | At affiliation |
| Document all verification efforts | Maintain internal compliance log | Ongoing |

**Brokers who fail to audit their agents' CE status are not just exposing themselves to SDREC discipline — they are creating liability on every transaction their office touches.** If an agent closes a deal on a lapsed license, the broker's E&O carrier may deny coverage for claims arising from that transaction. The reasoning is straightforward: the broker had a duty to verify and failed.

A practical approach involves building CE verification into existing compliance workflows. Many brokerages already run [deadline tracking systems](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) for transaction milestones. Adding a CE expiration check to that same system requires minimal effort but prevents catastrophic oversights. Some offices assign a compliance coordinator to pull SDREC transcripts for every affiliated agent at least twice per cycle — once at the midpoint and once 90 days before the deadline.

The SDREC's online license verification tool allows brokers to confirm an agent's current status in real time. Brokers should use this tool before allowing any agent to write offers, list properties, or attend closings on the firm's behalf. The cost of checking is zero; the cost of not checking can be career-ending.

## Course Providers and Delivery Methods for 2026

South Dakota permits CE through both classroom and distance education formats. The SDREC maintains a list of approved providers and courses, updated each cycle. Agents should verify approval status directly through the Commission rather than relying on provider marketing materials.

Classroom courses are offered through local associations of REALTORS, community colleges, and private education companies across the state. Distance education courses must meet ARSD 20:69:03:06 standards, which include identity verification, timed progression requirements, and proctored or monitored final assessments. A course that lets you click through slides in 20 minutes and print a certificate does not meet South Dakota's standards — and the SDREC has rejected CE claims from agents who completed courses through noncompliant platforms.

The 2-hour mandatory Commission Update course is typically offered both in-person at SDREC-sponsored events and through designated online providers. Because this course changes every cycle to reflect current legislative and regulatory updates, prior-cycle versions do not carry over. You must take the 2026-cycle version of the Commission Update to satisfy the mandate.

## Planning Your CE Strategy Before the Deadline

Treating CE like a last-minute errand is how agents end up on the wrong side of the SDREC's enforcement authority. A smarter approach spreads the 24 hours across the full two-year cycle. Completing one 3-hour elective course per quarter starting in early 2025 means you finish well before the November 2026 deadline with minimal disruption to your transaction schedule.

Agents juggling heavy transaction volumes should consider how compliance tasks compound during busy seasons. The same principle that makes [agents hate paperwork](/blog/why-agents-hate-paperwork) applies to CE — when it piles up, it gets ignored, and ignored obligations eventually explode. Building CE into your quarterly business review prevents the November panic.

Document everything. Save digital copies of course completion certificates in a dedicated folder. Screenshot your SDREC transcript periodically. If a provider goes dark or a system error wipes your records, you need independent proof that you completed the work.
