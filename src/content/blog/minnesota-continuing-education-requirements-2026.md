## Understanding Minnesota Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Every licensed real estate agent in Minnesota faces the same recurring obligation: complete your continuing education hours before your renewal deadline or lose the right to practice. The Minnesota continuing education requirements 2026 cycle carries specific mandates that differ in meaningful ways from neighboring states, and misunderstanding even one rule can put your license — and your active deals — at risk.

The Minnesota Department of Commerce, which regulates real estate licensees under Minnesota Statutes Chapter 82, administers all CE requirements. Unlike states where the real estate commission operates independently, Minnesota's commerce department handles licensing, enforcement, and provider approvals under one roof. This centralized structure means that disciplinary actions for CE non-compliance show up on the same record as other regulatory violations.

Your renewal cycle runs on a two-year schedule tied to your birth month, not a calendar year. If your license expires on June 30, 2026, every required hour must be completed and reported before that date — no grace period, no extensions.

## Credit Hour Breakdown and Mandatory Modules

**Minnesota requires 30 hours of continuing education for both salespersons and brokers during each two-year renewal cycle.** Those 30 hours are not all electives. The state mandates specific topic coverage that you cannot skip or substitute.

Here is how the 30 hours break down for 2026 renewals:

| Requirement | Salesperson | Broker |
|---|---|---|
| Total CE Hours | 30 | 30 |
| Agency Law (mandatory) | 1 hour minimum | 1 hour minimum |
| Fair Housing (mandatory) | 1 hour minimum | 1 hour minimum |
| Elective Approved Topics | 28 hours | 28 hours |
| Renewal Cycle Length | 2 years | 2 years |
| Renewal Fee | $80 | $120 |

The mandatory agency law hour must specifically address Minnesota Statutes Section 82.67, which governs disclosure of agency relationships. The fair housing module must cover federal Fair Housing Act provisions as well as the Minnesota Human Rights Act (Chapter 363A), which extends protected class coverage beyond federal minimums.

All courses must be approved by the Department of Commerce before they count. Course providers receive an approval number, and agents should verify that number before enrolling. Taking an unapproved course is the equivalent of taking no course at all — there is no retroactive credit.

## How to Verify Your CE Status and File for Renewal

The Department of Commerce maintains an online licensing portal where agents can check their CE transcript, verify completed hours, and submit renewal applications. The renewal form itself is submitted electronically through the Licensing and Renewal System. There is no separate paper form number for CE completion — it integrates directly into your renewal application.

**Agents should pull their CE transcript at least 60 days before their renewal deadline.** Providers have up to 10 business days to report course completions to the state, so last-minute courses risk not appearing on your record in time. If a discrepancy exists, you will need to contact the provider directly and request they re-submit your completion certificate.

For agents who also hold licenses in bordering states, Minnesota does not offer blanket CE reciprocity. Wisconsin, for example, has its own [continuing education structure](/blog/wisconsin-continuing-education-requirements-2026) with different hour counts and topic mandates. Completing CE in another state does not automatically satisfy Minnesota's requirements unless the specific course carries Minnesota Department of Commerce approval.

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

Non-compliance with CE requirements triggers an automatic license status change. Your license moves from active to inactive on the expiration date if renewal is not completed. There is no warning letter, no courtesy call, no 30-day buffer.

Once inactive, you cannot represent buyers or sellers, cannot collect commissions on pending transactions, and cannot hold yourself out as a licensed agent. Any transactions you attempt to conduct on an inactive license expose you to personal liability and potential disciplinary action under Minnesota Statutes Section 82.81, which covers unauthorized practice.

The financial consequences extend beyond the $80-$120 renewal fee. Reactivating a lapsed license requires completing all outstanding CE hours plus paying a late fee. If your license has been inactive for more than two years, you may be required to retake the state licensing examination entirely — effectively starting from scratch. For brokers, a lapsed license also jeopardizes the status of every agent supervised under that brokerage, creating a cascading compliance failure.

> Minnesota Statutes Section 82.55, Subdivision 2 states: "A licensee whose license has expired may not engage in any activity for which a license is required until the license is renewed or reinstated."

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Minnesota CE

Five errors appear repeatedly in enforcement actions and license audit findings. Knowing them in advance saves you from becoming a cautionary tale.

**First, agents confuse "registered" with "completed."** Signing up for a course does not generate CE credit. You must finish the course, pass any required assessment, and receive a completion certificate before the provider reports hours to the state. Agents who register for courses in the final week of their cycle and do not finish them end up with lapsed licenses.

Second, agents assume that attending a brokerage training session counts toward their 30 hours. Internal brokerage education — no matter how valuable — does not satisfy state CE requirements unless the course and instructor have been independently approved by the Department of Commerce. Your broker's weekly sales meeting is not CE.

Third, some agents complete all 30 hours in elective topics without satisfying the mandatory agency law and fair housing modules. The state's system will reject your renewal even if your total hour count exceeds 30. Those two mandatory modules are non-negotiable.

Fourth, agents who hold multiple license types (for example, a real estate license and an insurance license) sometimes conflate the CE requirements. Real estate CE and insurance CE are governed by different statutes and cannot cross-apply unless a course carries dual approval — which is rare.

Fifth, agents relocating from other states assume their prior state's CE transfers automatically. It does not. You must hold Minnesota-approved course credit regardless of what you completed elsewhere.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Designated brokers carry direct responsibility for the compliance status of every agent affiliated with their office. Minnesota Statutes Section 82.63 places supervision obligations on the broker that include — but are not limited to — verifying that affiliated agents maintain active licensure.

| Broker Obligation | Frequency | Consequence of Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Verify agent license status | Before each renewal cycle | Broker disciplinary action |
| Confirm CE transcript accuracy | Annually recommended | Potential vicarious liability |
| Remove inactive agents from roster | Immediately upon lapse | Fines, license suspension |
| Maintain training records | Ongoing | Audit findings |
| Report agent terminations | Within 3 business days | Administrative penalties |

**Brokers who allow an agent with an inactive license to continue practicing face their own disciplinary proceedings.** The Department of Commerce views this as a supervision failure, not merely the individual agent's problem. Fines can reach $10,000 per violation under Minnesota's administrative penalty authority.

A practical approach: run a license verification report on every affiliated agent at least quarterly. The Department of Commerce's online lookup tool makes this a five-minute task. Cross-reference your agent roster against active license records and flag anyone whose renewal date falls within the next 90 days. This is the kind of recurring compliance task that [agents frequently miss](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) when relying on memory alone.

Building a [systematic checklist approach](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) to broker-level compliance audits prevents the kind of oversight that triggers state investigations. Document every verification in writing, even if it is just a spreadsheet with dates and confirmation numbers.

## Approved Providers and Course Selection Strategy

The Department of Commerce publishes a searchable list of approved CE providers on its website at [mn.gov/commerce](https://mn.gov/commerce/licensees/real-estate/education/). Agents should check this list before paying for any course. Approved providers include universities, professional associations like Minnesota Realtors, private education companies, and some national online platforms.

Not all approved courses are equal in practical value. Agents approaching their 2026 renewal should consider allocating elective hours toward topics that reflect current regulatory changes — particularly around buyer agency agreement requirements, wire fraud prevention, and data privacy obligations that have evolved significantly since 2024.

Course costs vary widely. University-affiliated programs tend to charge $15-$25 per credit hour, while private online providers range from $8-$20 per hour. The cheapest option is not always the fastest, and the fastest is not always the most substantive. Pick courses that actually teach you something relevant to your practice rather than optimizing purely for speed or cost.

## Planning Your 2026 Renewal Timeline

The agents who never face compliance issues are the ones who build CE completion into their annual calendar rather than treating it as a last-minute scramble. Splitting your 30 hours across the two-year cycle — roughly 15 hours per year, or about 1.25 hours per month — eliminates the panic that leads to mistakes.

Mark your renewal deadline on every calendar you use. Set a 90-day advance reminder, a 60-day reminder, and a 30-day final check. At the 30-day mark, pull your transcript from the Department of Commerce portal and confirm that every completed course appears with the correct hour count. If something is missing, you still have time to resolve it.
