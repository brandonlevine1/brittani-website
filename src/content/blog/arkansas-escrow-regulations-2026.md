## Understanding Arkansas Escrow Regulations 2026

Getting escrow wrong in Arkansas does not just stall a deal—it can end a career. Arkansas escrow regulations 2026 remain governed primarily by the Arkansas Real Estate License Law (Ark. Code Ann. § 17-42-101 et seq.) and the rules promulgated by the [Arkansas Real Estate Commission (AREC)](https://www.arec.arkansas.gov/). Every licensee who touches earnest money, option fees, or any funds held on behalf of a transaction party should understand exactly where the guardrails sit.

The state treats escrow violations as one of the most serious categories of agent misconduct. AREC Rule 10.10 specifically addresses trust account requirements, while Ark. Code Ann. § 17-42-311 outlines the disciplinary actions the commission can take against non-compliant agents and brokers. These provisions did not soften in 2026; if anything, enforcement patterns show AREC auditing trust accounts with greater frequency.

This article breaks down the statutory framework, the forms you must use, the mistakes that trigger enforcement, and what brokers need to do to keep their office clean.

## Statutory Framework for Escrow in Arkansas

**Ark. Code Ann. § 17-42-311 grants AREC broad authority to investigate, suspend, revoke, or fine licensees for "failure to account for or remit any money coming into their possession that belongs to others."** That language is intentionally wide. It covers earnest money, security deposits, repair credits held pending completion, and any other funds entrusted to an agent during the course of a transaction.

AREC Rule 10.10 spells out the operational requirements. Brokers must maintain a trust account at an FDIC-insured financial institution located in Arkansas. Funds must be deposited within three business days of the broker receiving the executed contract (or the check itself, whichever is later). Commingling personal or business operating funds with trust account funds is a per-se violation that AREC does not treat as accidental.

Rule 10.11 requires that trust account records include a journal of all receipts and disbursements, individual ledger cards for each transaction or depositor, and monthly reconciliation statements. These records must be maintained for a minimum of five years and made available to AREC upon request.

## Forms and Documentation Agents Must Use

Arkansas does not mandate a single statewide earnest money contract in the way some states do, but the Arkansas REALTORS Association (ARA) publishes standard forms that the vast majority of transactions use. The most relevant forms for escrow purposes in 2026 include:

| Form Number | Form Name | Escrow Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| ARA Form 1 | Residential Sales Contract | Paragraph 3 designates escrow agent and deposit amount |
| ARA Form 2 | Counter Offer | May modify escrow terms from the original contract |
| ARA Form 7 | Release of Earnest Money | Required for any disbursement outside closing |
| ARA Form 9 | Amendment to Contract | Used to change escrow agent, deposit timeline, or amount |

**Paragraph 3 of ARA Form 1 is where agents most often create liability for themselves.** It identifies the escrow agent, the dollar amount of the deposit, and the deadline for delivery. Any discrepancy between what paragraph 3 says and what actually happens—wrong amount deposited, wrong entity holding funds, late delivery—becomes evidence in a potential enforcement action.

ARA Form 7 (Release of Earnest Money) must be signed by all parties before the escrow agent disburses funds outside of a normal closing. Agents who pressure an escrow holder to release funds without this form signed by both buyer and seller are inviting a complaint. If the parties cannot agree on disbursement, the escrow holder must interplead the funds with the circuit court under Ark. Code Ann. § 16-111-101, and should not simply sit on the money indefinitely.

## Penalties and Liability for Non-Compliance

The consequences of escrow violations in Arkansas cascade across three domains: regulatory, civil, and criminal. Understanding each layer helps agents appreciate why [missing deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) in this area is not a trivial issue.

**Regulatory penalties** under Ark. Code Ann. § 17-42-311 include license suspension, revocation, and monetary fines of up to $2,500 per violation. AREC can also require restitution and mandate additional continuing education hours. The commission publishes disciplinary actions in its quarterly newsletter, which means reputational damage accompanies any sanction.

**Civil liability** arises when a party to the transaction suffers financial harm because of escrow mismanagement. An agent who delays depositing earnest money could face a breach-of-fiduciary-duty claim from the seller if the buyer later defaults and the funds are not secured. Arkansas courts have upheld damages awards in such scenarios when agents failed to follow the contract's escrow instructions.

**Criminal exposure** exists in extreme cases. Conversion of trust funds—using escrow money for personal expenses—constitutes theft under Ark. Code Ann. § 5-36-103. Depending on the amount, the charge can be a Class B or Class C felony.

| Violation Type | Potential Consequence | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Late deposit (3+ business days) | Fine up to $2,500; license reprimand | AREC Rule 10.10 |
| Commingling funds | License suspension or revocation | Ark. Code Ann. § 17-42-311 |
| Disbursement without Form 7 | Civil liability; AREC complaint | ARA Form 7 protocol |
| Conversion of trust funds | Felony criminal charge | Ark. Code Ann. § 5-36-103 |
| Failure to maintain records 5 years | Fine; audit finding | AREC Rule 10.11 |

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Escrow in Arkansas

Thousands of Arkansas transactions close without incident every month. The ones that generate complaints share a handful of recurring patterns.

**Mistake 1: Holding the check "until inspection."** Some buyer's agents hold the earnest money check in their desk drawer, intending to deposit it only after the inspection period passes. AREC does not recognize this practice. The three-business-day clock starts upon contract execution, not upon contingency removal. The contract may specify a different arrangement, but absent explicit language, the default rule applies.

**Mistake 2: Depositing into the wrong account.** Agents who work across multiple brokerages, or who recently changed firms, occasionally deposit earnest money into a prior broker's trust account or—worse—into their personal business account. Even an honest mistake triggers a commingling finding if AREC audits.

**Mistake 3: Verbal release of funds.** A listing agent calls the title company and says "the seller agreed to release earnest money back to the buyer." Without ARA Form 7 signed by both parties, the escrow holder who disburses on a verbal instruction has violated protocol. The agent who made the call also shares liability. Verbal agreements regarding escrow disbursement do not satisfy Arkansas documentation requirements.

**Mistake 4: Failing to identify the escrow agent clearly.** Paragraph 3 of ARA Form 1 requires the specific name of the entity holding funds. Writing "TBD" or leaving it blank creates ambiguity that can delay closing and expose the agent to claims of negligence. If the escrow agent is not yet confirmed at the time of offer, agents should use ARA Form 9 to amend the contract once it is.

**Mistake 5: Ignoring interpleader timelines.** When buyer and seller dispute who gets the earnest money, the escrow holder cannot indefinitely hold the funds. Arkansas courts expect a timely interpleader action. Brokers who let disputed funds sit for months without filing risk both a commission complaint and judicial sanctions.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Brokers bear ultimate responsibility for their office's escrow compliance. Ark. Code Ann. § 17-42-301(b) makes the principal broker accountable for all trust account activity conducted under the brokerage's license, regardless of which agent received the deposit.

Monthly reconciliation is not optional—it is a regulatory requirement under Rule 10.11. Brokers should reconcile the trust account bank statement against the transaction ledger by the 10th of each month. Any discrepancy must be investigated and resolved immediately, not carried forward to the next month.

**Brokers should also audit their agents' use of ARA Form 7 in every terminated transaction.** A deal that falls apart without a properly executed release form is a ticking clock. The broker needs a system—whether manual or automated—that flags terminated contracts and confirms disbursement documentation exists.

Training is another enforcement point. AREC requires that brokers provide supervision and oversight, and a broker who cannot demonstrate that agents received instruction on escrow procedures may face vicarious liability. Consider a quarterly review of trust account procedures with all agents, documented with sign-in sheets. For brokerages managing high transaction volume, automating [compliance checklists](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) removes the reliance on human memory.

Record retention is the final audit pillar. Five years of trust account records must be accessible. Digital storage is permissible so long as the records can be reproduced in hard copy upon AREC request. Brokers who lose records due to failed hardware, closed cloud accounts, or office moves face the same penalties as those who never kept records at all.

## How Arkansas Compares to Neighboring States

Arkansas's three-business-day deposit requirement is relatively standard, but its enforcement posture differs from neighboring jurisdictions. Understanding the comparison helps agents who operate near state lines or hold licenses in multiple states.

| Requirement | Arkansas | Missouri | Tennessee | Oklahoma |

| Deposit deadline | 3 business days | "Timely" (no fixed days) | 3 business days | 5 business days |
| Trust account location | Must be in-state | In-state preferred | In-state required | In-state required |
| Interpleader trigger | Dispute with no resolution | Dispute with no resolution | 90-day hold max | Dispute with no resolution |

This table makes clear that Arkansas falls in the middle of the pack on fine amounts but is strict on deposit timing and geographic requirements for the trust account.

## Protecting Yourself Under Arkansas Escrow Regulations 2026

The agents who stay out of trouble tend to follow a predictable set of habits. They deliver earnest money to the designated escrow agent the same day the contract is executed—not on day three, but day one. They confirm receipt in writing, attach that confirmation to the transaction file, and note the date and amount.

They also resist shortcuts on disbursement. When a deal falls apart, they immediately prepare ARA Form 7 and send it to both parties for signature. If one party refuses to sign, they alert their broker within 24 hours so interpleader can be considered before the dispute ages.
