## Louisiana Buyer Agency Agreement Requirements 2026: The Rules Have Teeth Now

The era of casually showing homes without paperwork is over in Louisiana. **Louisiana buyer agency agreement requirements 2026 demand that every agent secure a signed, written agreement before delivering substantive brokerage services to a buyer.** This is not optional guidance from your broker—it is an enforceable mandate backed by the Louisiana Real Estate Commission (LREC), federal settlement terms, and MLS participation rules that went into effect after August 17, 2024.

Louisiana's regulatory framework for agency relationships has always been more prescriptive than many agents realize. La. R.S. 9:3891 et seq., the Louisiana Real Estate License Law, and LREC Rule Chapter 29 together establish the legal scaffolding for buyer representation. The 2024 NAR settlement added a new structural requirement on top of that existing law: agents participating in any MLS must have a written agreement with buyers before touring listed properties.

This post breaks down exactly what Louisiana agents must do, what forms to use, and what happens when compliance fails.

## What Louisiana Law Actually Requires

Louisiana codified agency disclosure obligations decades ago under the Licensee Disclosure and Duties provisions of La. R.S. 9:3891. The statute requires licensees to disclose their agency relationship to all parties before performing brokerage services. Prior to 2024, many buyer agents relied on oral understandings or delayed paperwork until an offer was imminent.

That approach is now a violation. The LREC issued Bulletin 2024-04, which clarified that written buyer representation agreements must be executed before or at the time substantive services begin. "Substantive services" includes property tours, sending listings tailored to the buyer's criteria, and preparing CMAs for purchase decisions.

The written agreement must contain specific elements: the scope of services, the compensation terms (including a definite dollar amount or percentage that cannot be open-ended), a definite termination date, and a clear statement that compensation is negotiable. Omitting any of these elements exposes the agent to disciplinary action.

## The Forms Louisiana Agents Must Use

**The Louisiana REALTORS association publishes the standard forms that satisfy both LREC rules and MLS compliance requirements.** The two primary documents are the LR-BRA (Buyer Representation Agreement) and the LR-EBRA (Exclusive Buyer Representation Agreement). Both were revised in late 2024 and again in early 2025 to align with settlement mandates.

| Form | Use Case | Exclusivity | Key Feature |
|------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| LR-BRA | Non-exclusive buyer rep | No | Buyer may work with multiple agents simultaneously |
| LR-EBRA | Exclusive buyer rep | Yes | Single agent represents buyer for defined period |
| LR-ABRA | Agency amendment/addendum | N/A | Modifies existing agreement terms mid-relationship |
| LR-TBA | Termination of buyer agreement | N/A | Formal release signed by both parties |

The LR-EBRA is by far the most common form used in practice. It binds the buyer to one agent for a specified term and obligates the buyer to compensate the agent if they purchase a property during the agreement period using another licensee—or no licensee at all. The compensation section now requires agents to fill in exact amounts, not blanks referencing "whatever the listing broker offers."

Agents who are not REALTOR members still must comply with LREC rules. Non-member brokerages typically draft proprietary agreements, but those documents must still contain every element outlined in LREC Rule 2901 and the MLS participation requirements tied to their board affiliation.

## How Compensation Terms Must Be Disclosed

One of the most significant operational changes under the 2026 framework is how buyer agent compensation appears in the agreement. The old model—where listing brokers posted cooperative compensation on the MLS and buyer agents simply collected it—is gone. Louisiana agents must now specify their fee in the buyer agreement itself.

The agreement can state compensation as a flat dollar amount, a percentage of the purchase price, or a combination. It cannot state "to be determined" or reference MLS offers of compensation as the sole source of payment. If a seller separately offers to pay the buyer's agent (through a seller concession or listing broker cooperation), that payment may satisfy the buyer's obligation, but the buyer agreement must still state the fee the buyer is responsible for if no such offer exists.

**This is where many agents stumble: they sign agreements with vague compensation language assuming the seller will always cover the fee.** When sellers decline to contribute, the buyer owes the fee, and if the agreement language is ambiguous, collection becomes a legal nightmare. LREC has flagged this as a growing source of consumer complaints since early 2025.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failing to secure a proper buyer agency agreement before providing services triggers multiple layers of liability in Louisiana. The consequences are not theoretical—LREC has been actively auditing files and responding to complaints related to missing or deficient agreements since Q1 2025.

| Violation | Potential Consequence | Authority |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|
| No written agreement before touring | Fine up to $2,000; license suspension | LREC Rule 2901; La. R.S. 37:1455 |
| Missing termination date in agreement | LREC reprimand; mandatory corrective education | LREC Bulletin 2024-04 |
| Open-ended compensation clause | Agreement deemed unenforceable; agent forfeits fee | La. R.S. 9:3893; NAR settlement terms |
| Failure to provide agency disclosure | Civil liability to buyer; possible deal rescission | La. R.S. 9:3891(A) |
| MLS violation (touring without agreement) | MLS fines; possible MLS suspension | Local board MLS rules |

Beyond regulatory penalties, agents face civil exposure. A buyer who later claims they did not understand the agency relationship—or that they were never told the agent expected compensation—can sue for breach of fiduciary duty. Louisiana courts have historically been unsympathetic to agents who cannot produce signed documentation. The [consequences of missing deadlines and paperwork failures](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) are not limited to transaction delays; they can end careers.

## Common Mistakes Louisiana Agents Make

After reviewing dozens of LREC disciplinary actions and speaking with managing brokers across the state, five recurring errors stand out.

First, agents present the agreement at the wrong time. Signing at the offer stage—rather than before the first showing—violates the rule entirely. The agreement must be in place before substantive services begin, full stop. Agents who wait until a buyer is emotionally committed to a property are creating an unenforceable document and a regulatory violation simultaneously.

Second, agents fail to explain the compensation section verbally. Louisiana law requires that the agreement itself disclose compensation terms clearly, but LREC guidance also expects agents to explain—not just hand over—the document. When buyers later claim they did not understand what they signed, the agent bears the burden of proving adequate disclosure.

Third, agents let agreements lapse without renewal. A buyer agency agreement with a 90-day term that expires while the buyer is still actively searching creates a gap in representation. Any services provided during that gap are uncompensated and unprotected. Agents must calendar expiration dates and secure renewals before they lapse—a workflow issue that [proper pipeline tracking](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) can prevent.

Fourth, agents use outdated forms. The LR-EBRA was revised twice between late 2024 and mid-2025. Agents still using the pre-settlement version are submitting agreements that do not contain required language about compensation negotiability. LREC auditors are specifically flagging form version numbers.

Fifth, agents do not provide a signed copy to the buyer at the time of execution. La. R.S. 9:3891 and LREC Rule 2901 both require immediate delivery of a copy. Agents who say they will "send it later" are already in violation the moment the buyer walks out without their copy.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Managing brokers in Louisiana carry supervisory liability under La. R.S. 37:1455.1. When a sponsored agent fails to comply with buyer agency agreement requirements, the broker faces potential discipline up to and including license revocation. This is not a passive responsibility.

**Brokers should implement a file review trigger that checks for a signed buyer agreement before any showing confirmation or offer submission occurs.** The review should confirm the form version is current, the compensation field is completed with a definite amount, the termination date is present, and the buyer received a copy.

Monthly or quarterly compliance audits are insufficient given the pace of enforcement. Brokerages handling volume should consider real-time transaction management systems that flag missing documents the moment an agent creates a buyer file. Brokers must also maintain records showing they provided training on the new requirements. LREC has shown leniency toward brokers who can demonstrate documented training efforts and systematic audit processes, even when individual agents commit violations. Without that paper trail, the broker absorbs full liability alongside the agent.
