# South Dakota New Real Estate Laws 2026: What Every Agent and Broker Must Know Before October

The 2026 legislative session in Pierre produced more real estate-related bills than any session in the past decade. South Dakota new real estate laws 2026 affect buyer agency agreements, seller disclosures, wire fraud prevention, and continuing education requirements — and the compliance deadlines are already landing. If you hold a license through the South Dakota Real Estate Commission (SDREC), the changes below apply to you starting as early as July 1, 2026, with additional provisions phasing in through January 2027.

This is not a surface-level overview. Below, you will find the specific statutes, form numbers, liability exposures, and enforcement timelines that matter to working agents and their designated brokers across the state.

## Buyer Representation Agreements Are Now Mandatory Under SDCL 36-21A-72

**House Bill 1147, signed into law on March 12, 2026, codifies a written buyer representation requirement that goes beyond the post-NAR settlement guidance many agents were already following.** Under the amended SDCL 36-21A-72, a licensee may not conduct substantive showing activity on behalf of a buyer without first obtaining a signed buyer representation agreement that includes four mandatory elements: the scope of services, the duration of the agreement, the method and amount of compensation, and a clear statement of the buyer's right to terminate.

The SDREC published Form 1052 — Buyer Representation Agreement (2026 Revision) — in June 2026 specifically to satisfy these requirements. Agents who previously used brokerage-created forms should cross-check them against Form 1052's mandatory fields. Any omission of the four required elements renders the agreement non-compliant, which means the agent has no enforceable right to compensation under the new statute.

This shift mirrors what has happened in other states, but South Dakota's version is notably strict on the "substantive showing activity" trigger. A preliminary phone consultation does not trigger the requirement, but the moment you schedule a showing, pull a lockbox code on the buyer's behalf, or submit an offer, you must have that signed form in hand.

## South Dakota New Real Estate Laws 2026: Updated Seller Disclosure Requirements

The Seller's Property Condition Disclosure Statement — Form 1029 — received its most significant overhaul since 2014. Senate Bill 88, amending SDCL 43-4-44, adds three new disclosure categories that sellers must address: known flood zone status based on the most recent FEMA maps, the presence of any private well or septic system inspection deficiencies within the past five years, and whether the property has been the subject of an insurance claim for mold or water intrusion.

**Agents representing sellers are now required to deliver the updated Form 1029 before the buyer's offer is accepted, not merely before closing.** The old timing provision, which allowed delivery at any point before the transaction closed, created a loophole that the legislature explicitly closed. If the updated disclosure is not in the buyer's hands before acceptance, the buyer gains a five-business-day rescission window starting from the date of actual receipt.

This timing change is where most deal disruptions will occur in the second half of 2026. Listing agents who wait until the inspection period to hand over disclosures are now operating outside the law, and the liability consequences are real. The SDREC can assess penalties of up to $2,000 per violation, and the buyer's rescission right is independent of any contractual waiver.

| Flood zone status | Not required | Mandatory on Form 1029 |

## Wire Fraud Prevention: The New Explicit Agent Obligation

Wire fraud continues to plague real estate closings nationwide, and South Dakota's response in 2026 is to put skin in the game for licensees. The SDREC amended Administrative Rule 20:69:06:03 to require that both the listing agent and the buyer's agent provide written notice to their respective clients about wire fraud risks and instruct them to verify all wiring instructions by calling the title company at a known, independently obtained phone number.

The required notice language is now embedded in the SDREC's revised Transaction Notification Insert (Form 1061). Agents must include Form 1061 as part of the initial client packet, and the client must sign or electronically acknowledge receipt. Failure to deliver this form creates an affirmative defense for the client in any subsequent dispute over misdirected funds, meaning the brokerage could absorb liability for losses that would otherwise fall on the defrauded party.

This is not theoretical risk. South Dakota saw a 34% increase in reported real estate wire fraud attempts between 2024 and 2025 according to SDREC enforcement data. Agents who treat this as a checkbox exercise rather than a genuine client conversation are exposing their brokerages to six-figure liability. ## Continuing Education Changes: New Modules, New Deadlines

**The SDREC's CE requirements did not change in total hours — agents still need 24 hours per two-year renewal cycle — but the content mandate shifted significantly.** Effective January 1, 2026, at least 2 of those 24 hours must be completed in a dedicated module covering the 2026 legislative changes to agency law and fair housing protections. The SDREC approved three providers to offer this module as of August 2026, and the list is posted on the [SDREC website](https://dlr.sd.gov/realestate/).

Additionally, the first renewal cycle after July 1, 2026, requires that agents complete at least 1 hour of wire fraud and cybersecurity training from an SDREC-approved provider. This hour counts toward the overall 24 but cannot substitute for the agency/fair housing module. Agents whose renewal falls between July and December 2026 have until their renewal date to complete both new modules; agents renewing in the first half of 2027 have no extension.

The SDREC has signaled it will audit CE compliance more aggressively this cycle. During the 2024-2025 cycle, the commission issued 47 license suspensions for CE non-compliance. Expect that number to climb as the commission enforces the new subject-matter requirements alongside total-hour mandates.

## Common Mistakes Agents Will Make Under the New Laws

Every regulatory shift produces a wave of avoidable errors. Based on the statutory language and early SDREC guidance bulletins, five mistakes will trip up South Dakota agents most frequently in 2026 and 2027.

**First, agents will use outdated disclosure forms.** Form 1029 and Form 1052 both received 2026 revisions, and the old versions are no longer compliant. If your transaction management system auto-populates forms, verify that the template library has been updated. Using a 2024 Form 1029 after July 1, 2026, does not satisfy SDCL 43-4-44 as amended, period.

Second, agents will deliver seller disclosures too late. The shift from "before closing" to "before offer acceptance" is the single most consequential timing change in South Dakota real estate this year. Agents accustomed to the old workflow will hand disclosures to buyers during the inspection period and then face a rescission demand they cannot contest.

Third, buyer's agents will begin showing homes without a signed Form 1052 in the file. The "substantive showing activity" trigger is easy to trip accidentally — pulling a lockbox code counts. Agents who plan to sign the agreement at the first showing are already too late if they accessed the property through MLS-linked lockbox systems that log activity before the door opens.

Fourth, agents will treat Form 1061 (wire fraud notice) as optional or bury it in a stack of documents without explanation. The form requires a separate acknowledgment signature, and the SDREC has indicated that a general "I received all documents" signature page does not satisfy the rule.

Fifth, agents will assume their existing CE hours cover the new mandatory modules. General ethics or legal update courses do not satisfy the 2-hour agency/fair housing module requirement unless the SDREC has specifically approved the course for that purpose. Check the course approval number before counting it toward compliance.
