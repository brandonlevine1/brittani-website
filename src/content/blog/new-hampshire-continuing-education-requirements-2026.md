## Understanding New Hampshire Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Failing to meet New Hampshire continuing education requirements 2026 is one of the fastest ways to lose your license and expose your brokerage to liability. The New Hampshire Real Estate Commission (NHREC), operating under RSA 331-A, sets clear expectations for every active licensee in the state. Yet every renewal cycle, agents scramble at the last minute or miss the deadline entirely.

The state requires 15 hours of approved continuing education during each two-year renewal period. Three of those hours must come from a Commission-designated core course that changes each cycle. The remaining 12 hours are elective, drawn from NHREC-approved providers covering topics like agency law, fair housing, contracts, and environmental disclosures.

**New Hampshire does not grant extensions for incomplete CE hours.** If your renewal date arrives and you have not submitted proof of completion, your license lapses. There is no grace period built into the statute, and the Commission has shown no appetite for creating one.

## The Regulatory Framework: RSA 331-A and Commission Rules

The legal backbone of New Hampshire's licensing and CE requirements sits in [RSA 331-A](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xxx/331-a/331-a-mrg.htm), the state's real estate practice act. Section 331-A:15 specifically addresses continuing education obligations, while the NHREC's administrative rules (Chapter Ree 300) provide granular details on approved course formats, instructor qualifications, and record-keeping.

Agents renew using Form NHREC-2, the biennial renewal application. This form requires attestation that all CE hours have been completed and asks for course provider names, dates, and certificate numbers. Submitting a false attestation on Form NHREC-2 constitutes a violation of RSA 331-A:26, which authorizes the Commission to impose fines, suspend licenses, or revoke them outright.

The Commission also maintains a list of approved education providers, updated quarterly. Taking a course from a non-approved provider — even one approved in another state — will not count toward your New Hampshire hours unless the Commission has specifically granted reciprocal approval.

## Consequences of Non-Compliance: Beyond a Lapsed License

The immediate consequence of missing CE deadlines is license lapse. But the downstream effects reach much further, particularly for agents mid-transaction. Under RSA 331-A:3, performing real estate services without a valid license is a misdemeanor, punishable by fines up to $2,000 per occurrence.

**A lapsed license can unravel active deals.** If a buyer or seller discovers their agent was unlicensed during the transaction, they may have grounds to cancel the contract. Commission agreements executed by an unlicensed agent are generally unenforceable, meaning the agent forfeits their fee even if the deal closes. Brokers can also face disciplinary action under RSA 331-A:26-a for failing to verify that their agents held active licenses.

If your license has been lapsed for more than 90 days, the Commission requires you to retake the state portion of the licensing exam before reactivation. Lapse beyond two years forces you to start the entire pre-licensing education process from scratch — 40 hours of coursework plus both exam sections.

| Lapse Duration | Reinstatement Requirement | Exam Required | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-90 days | Complete CE + late fee | No | $100 late fee |
| 91 days - 2 years | Complete CE + state exam | State portion only | $150 + exam fee |
| Over 2 years | Full pre-license education (40 hrs) | Full exam (both portions) | Full application fee |

## The 2026 Core Course: What to Expect

Each renewal cycle, the NHREC designates a mandatory 3-hour core course that addresses current legislative changes, emerging compliance issues, or areas where the Commission has observed frequent violations. For the 2025-2026 cycle, the core course focuses on updated agency disclosure practices and the revised property condition disclosure form (NHREC Form PCD-1).

The core course cannot be substituted with elective hours. Agents who complete 15 hours of electives but skip the core course will have their renewal denied. The Commission publishes the core course syllabus on its website, and multiple approved providers offer it in both live classroom and online formats.

Timing matters here. Popular providers fill their final sessions weeks before the renewal deadline. Agents who wait until the last month often find themselves unable to secure a seat, particularly for live offerings in smaller markets like the Upper Valley or North Country regions.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With New Hampshire CE

**Mistake 1: Assuming out-of-state CE transfers automatically.** New Hampshire does not have blanket reciprocity for continuing education credits. An agent holding licenses in both New Hampshire and Massachusetts cannot apply Mass CE hours to their NH requirement unless the specific course has been approved by the NHREC. This catches dual-state agents every cycle, particularly those working the border markets around Nashua and Salem.

**Mistake 2: Relying on broker notifications instead of tracking your own deadline.** The Commission mails renewal notices approximately 60 days before expiration, but the statute places responsibility squarely on the individual licensee. If the notice gets lost in the mail or your address on file is outdated, that is not a valid defense for a lapsed license.

Mistake 3: Completing courses from providers whose approval has lapsed. The NHREC reviews and re-approves education providers on a regular cycle. A provider approved in 2024 may not retain approval in 2026. Always verify current provider status on the Commission's website before enrolling.

Mistake 4: Failing to retain certificates of completion. The Commission conducts random audits and may request proof of CE completion up to four years after a renewal cycle. If you cannot produce certificates, the Commission treats those hours as incomplete. Store digital copies in a system that will survive a laptop crash or email migration.

Mistake 5: Counting pre-licensing instructor hours as CE. Agents who teach pre-licensing courses sometimes assume those teaching hours satisfy their own CE requirement. They do not, unless the Commission has specifically approved the course for CE credit and the instructor separately enrolls as a student participant.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Brokers in New Hampshire carry affirmative supervisory obligations under RSA 331-A:16. This is not a passive role. The statute holds brokers responsible for ensuring that every agent affiliated with their office maintains an active, valid license at all times.

| Broker Responsibility | Statutory Basis | Consequence of Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Verify agent license status before affiliation | RSA 331-A:16 | Fine up to $2,000; license suspension |
| Monitor renewal deadlines for all affiliated agents | NHREC Admin Rule Ree 302.04 | Written warning; repeat violations lead to hearing |
| Remove lapsed agents from active transaction work | RSA 331-A:3 | Broker license revocation possible |
| Maintain records of agent CE compliance | Ree 302.06 | Audit failure; mandatory corrective action plan |

**Brokers should build a tracking calendar that flags agent renewal dates at 90, 60, and 30 days before expiration.** A single lapsed agent working on even one transaction creates liability exposure for the entire brokerage. The Commission has publicly disciplined brokerages for this exact failure in recent enforcement actions.

If you manage a team, consider requiring agents to submit CE certificates to the office 30 days before their renewal date. This creates an internal verification checkpoint that catches problems before they become regulatory violations. Keeping [checklists and systems](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) in place for compliance tracking is not optional overhead — it is risk management.

## How CE Compliance Intersects With Transaction Work

An agent whose license lapses mid-transaction creates an immediate crisis. Any listing agreement, buyer agency agreement, or purchase and sale contract signed during the lapse period is potentially voidable. The [points where deals typically break down](/blog/where-deals-break-down) already include financing, inspections, and appraisals — adding a licensing deficiency to the list is entirely preventable.

For transaction coordinators managing files in New Hampshire, verifying agent license status at file opening is a basic quality control step. The NHREC's online license verification tool allows instant lookup by name or license number. Building this check into your intake process prevents downstream chaos.

Brokers should also be aware that errors and omissions insurance policies frequently exclude coverage for acts performed while unlicensed. This means a malpractice claim arising from a lapsed-license transaction may leave the agent and brokerage without insurance protection — exposing personal assets to judgment.

## Elective Course Selection: Strategy, Not Afterthought

The 12 elective hours offer more flexibility than many agents realize, but strategic selection matters. Agents working in specific niches — commercial, property management, or new construction — should seek courses that directly enhance their practice area expertise. Generic courses satisfy the hour requirement but provide no competitive differentiation.

The Commission approves courses in categories including contract law, environmental hazards, fair housing, technology in practice, and brokerage management. Agents planning to [move into supervisory roles](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) or open their own firms should prioritize courses in brokerage operations and trust accounting, as these topics appear heavily on the broker upgrade exam.

Distance learning courses must include an assessment component to qualify for NHREC approval. Courses that allow passive viewing without a proctored quiz or final exam will not receive Commission credit, even if offered by an otherwise approved provider.

## Planning Ahead: Building CE Into Your Annual Calendar

The two-year renewal cycle creates a dangerous illusion of abundant time. Agents who complete all 15 hours in the first year of their cycle never face renewal stress. Those who defer until month 23 frequently encounter scheduling conflicts, provider capacity issues, and the inevitable emergency that consumes the final available weekend.

**Splitting CE into roughly 8 hours in year one and 7 hours in year two is the simplest insurance against deadline failure.** Mark your renewal date in every calendar system you use. Set recurring reminders. Treat it with the same urgency as a closing date, because the consequence of missing it is equally severe.
