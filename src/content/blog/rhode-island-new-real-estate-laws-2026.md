## Rhode Island New Real Estate Laws 2026: The Statutory Changes That Hit This Summer

The Rhode Island Department of Business Regulation published its final rulemaking package in April, and the bulk of changes take effect on July 1, 2026. If you are still operating under last year's procedures, the Rhode Island new real estate laws 2026 create real liability exposure for your license and your brokerage. This is not a minor update to a single form—it touches buyer agency, seller disclosures, earnest money handling, wire fraud prevention, and continuing education requirements simultaneously.

The regulatory authority driving these changes is the Rhode Island Real Estate Commission, operating under R.I. Gen. Laws Title 5, Chapter 20.5. Several amendments also stem from the state legislature's passage of H.B. 6142 and S.B. 0987, both signed by the governor in March 2026. Together, they represent the most aggressive single-year overhaul Rhode Island has made to its real estate practice act since the 2009 recession-era reforms.

## Buyer Agency Agreements Are Now Mandatory Before Touring

**Rhode Island joins the growing list of states requiring written buyer representation agreements prior to any property showing.** The amendment to R.I. Gen. Laws Section 5-20.5-18 demands that agents obtain a signed Buyer Agency Agreement (Form RE-28B) before engaging in substantive real estate activity on behalf of a buyer, which the statute defines as touring properties, submitting offers, or negotiating terms.

The form itself has been revised. Form RE-28B now requires explicit disclosure of the compensation structure, including whether the buyer agent's fee will come from the seller's proceeds, the buyer directly, or a combination. This mirrors post-NAR settlement requirements but goes further by mandating a not-to-exceed dollar figure or percentage on the face of the agreement.

Failure to secure this agreement creates two problems. First, the Commission can impose administrative fines of up to $1,500 per violation and require completion of a six-hour remedial ethics course. Second, any commission dispute that arises without a signed RE-28B will be presumed unenforceable under the new statute, meaning you may not recover your fee regardless of the work performed.

## Seller Disclosure Form Overhaul: Form RE-22R Replaces RE-22

The existing Seller's Real Estate Condition Report, known for decades as Form RE-22, has been retired. Its replacement—Form RE-22R—adds fourteen new disclosure categories that reflect modern construction issues and environmental risks specific to Rhode Island's coastal geography. New categories include flood zone reclassification history, presence of PFAS-contaminated well water, and post-2020 insurance claim history.

Agents representing sellers must ensure the revised form is completed and delivered to prospective buyers before or at the time of the first offer. The delivery timing requirement under R.I. Gen. Laws Section 5-20.6-5 has not changed, but the penalty structure has. Under the amended Section 5-20.6-8, a buyer who never receives Form RE-22R gains an extended rescission window of five calendar days from actual receipt—even post-contract.

This change matters because deals that otherwise would have survived inspection can now unravel on disclosure grounds alone. If a listing agent distributes the old Form RE-22 after July 1, the Commission treats it as non-delivery. The form number matters.

## Earnest Money and Escrow Timelines Get Shorter

| Requirement | Pre-July 2026 Rule | Post-July 2026 Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit to escrow | 5 business days after execution | 2 business days after execution |
| Escrow account reconciliation | Monthly | Bi-weekly (every 14 days) |
| Interest disbursement notice | At closing | Within 3 days of deposit |
| Disputed funds hold period | Until mutual release | 30-day max, then interpleader required |

**The compressed two-business-day deposit window is the change most likely to catch agents off guard.** Under amended Regulation 250-RICR-100-00-1.7, the clock starts at mutual execution—not at delivery of fully signed copies to both parties. If your transaction management workflow relies on waiting for countersigned documents to trickle back before triggering deposit reminders, you are already behind.

Brokerages holding escrow accounts face an additional burden: the reconciliation frequency has doubled. The biweekly reconciliation requirement means that a single missed cycle can result in an audit flag from the Commission. Fines for escrow account deficiencies now start at $2,500 per incident under the revised penalty schedule, up from $1,000.

For agents working with [transaction coordinators who track deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines), this shift requires an immediate update to checklist templates and automated reminder systems.

## Wire Fraud Protections: Form RE-31W and New Liability Standards

Rhode Island's new Wire Verification Disclosure (Form RE-31W) must be provided to all parties within 24 hours of a fully executed purchase agreement. The form requires listing brokerages to include verified wire instructions from the title company or closing attorney, a statement confirming the verification method used, and a warning about common wire fraud tactics.

The liability framework here is what makes this law unusual. Under H.B. 6142, the listing agent's brokerage is deemed the "first responsible party" for verifying and distributing accurate wire instructions. If a buyer wires funds to a fraudulent account and the brokerage failed to provide Form RE-31W, the brokerage faces strict liability for the misdirected funds up to $50,000—regardless of whether the fraud originated from a third-party email compromise.

> "The brokerage's obligation under Section 5-20.5-22.1 is not merely to inform, but to verify through independent confirmation with the receiving institution prior to distributing wire instructions to any party." — H.B. 6142, Section 3(b)(2)

This is a dramatic departure from the prior framework, where agents faced only negligence-based exposure. If you want to understand how other states handle similar [wire fraud prevention protocols](/blog/wisconsin-wire-fraud-prevention-2026), the comparison is instructive—Rhode Island's strict liability standard is among the most aggressive in the country.

## Common Mistakes Agents Will Make Under These New Laws

**The first and most predictable error is using outdated forms.** Agents who print or auto-populate documents from their existing templates will inadvertently distribute Form RE-22 instead of RE-22R, or use a buyer agency agreement that lacks the required compensation cap language. The Commission has indicated it will not grant a grace period—enforcement begins July 1.

The second mistake involves miscounting the earnest money deposit window. Agents accustomed to the five-business-day timeline will assume they have breathing room. They do not. Two business days from mutual execution means that a contract signed on a Wednesday afternoon requires deposit by end of business Friday. A Thursday execution means the following Monday. Missing this deadline gives the seller grounds to void the contract under the amended standard purchase agreement language in Form RE-15R.

Third, agents will forget to distribute Form RE-31W within the 24-hour window because it is a new workflow step with no existing muscle memory. Unlike disclosure forms that agents have delivered for years, this wire verification form has no precedent in Rhode Island practice. It requires coordination with the title company or closing attorney before delivery—and that coordination takes time agents will not anticipate.

Fourth, buyer agents will show property before securing the signed RE-28B, either out of habit or because a client pressures them to "just take a quick look." The statute makes no exception for informal or preliminary showings. An open house visit where the agent provides specific guidance or negotiation advice qualifies as substantive activity.

Fifth, agents will fail to update their continuing education records. The new laws added a mandatory two-hour module on wire fraud prevention and a one-hour module on the revised disclosure forms to the 2026-2027 renewal cycle. These modules must be completed through a Commission-approved provider before the December 31, 2026 renewal deadline.

Brokers carry vicarious liability for agent non-compliance under R.I. Gen. Laws Section 5-20.5-14. That liability is not theoretical—the Commission issued 23 brokerage-level sanctions in 2025, and the expanded penalty schedule makes 2026 violations significantly more expensive. A broker who cannot demonstrate active supervision and compliance monitoring faces license suspension, not just fines.

**Every brokerage should conduct a full file audit of active transactions before July 1.** Any deal currently under contract that will close after the effective date must include the updated forms where applicable. The Commission's guidance letter from May 2026 explicitly states that transactions closing on or after July 1 are subject to the new standards, regardless of when the contract was originally executed.

## How the Rhode Island Real Estate Commission Will Enforce These Changes

The Commission announced a dedicated compliance review unit funded through a temporary assessment increase of $35 per licensee during the 2026-2027 renewal period. This unit will conduct randomized file audits of 10% of active brokerages during the first six months after implementation. The [Rhode Island Department of Business Regulation](https://dbr.ri.gov/divisions/real-estate) has published its enforcement priority list, with wire fraud disclosure and buyer agency agreement compliance at the top.

Complaints from consumers will also trigger targeted investigations. Given that Form RE-31W is a consumer-facing document, buyers who experience wire fraud or near-miss incidents will have a clear paper trail to reference when filing complaints. The Commission's investigators will look for the 24-hour delivery timestamp, the verification method documented on the form, and the brokerage's written SOP for wire instruction confirmation.

Disciplinary outcomes range from mandatory continuing education and probation for first-time technical violations to license revocation for repeat offenders or agents involved in escrow fund mishandling. The amended penalty matrix published in Regulation 250-RICR-100-00-1.12 provides specific fine amounts tied to each violation category—removing much of the discretion that previously allowed informal warnings.
