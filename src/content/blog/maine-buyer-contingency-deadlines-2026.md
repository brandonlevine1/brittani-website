## Why Maine Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026 Demand Precision

Missing a single contingency deadline can kill a deal, expose your brokerage to liability, or cost your client thousands in forfeited earnest money. Maine buyer contingency deadlines 2026 are governed by a combination of the Maine Association of Realtors (MAR) standard forms and state statutory requirements, and the margin for error is thinner than most agents realize. The Maine Real Estate Commission (MREC) has made supervisory compliance a priority in recent enforcement actions, putting brokers on notice that sloppy deadline management is no longer a minor infraction.

Maine operates under a modified "time is of the essence" framework. When dates are inserted into the MAR Form 102 (Purchase and Sale Agreement), those dates are contractual obligations with real legal teeth. Failing to perform by a stated deadline creates exposure that ranges from contingency waiver to breach-of-contract claims.

## The Legal Foundation: Maine Statutes and MAR Forms

**The Maine Revised Statutes Title 32, Chapter 114 governs real estate brokerage practice, while Title 33, Chapter 7 (Statute of Frauds) requires all real property contract modifications to be in writing.** These two bodies of law form the backbone of contingency enforcement in Maine transactions.

The primary forms agents work with for contingency management include:

| Form | Purpose | Contingency Role |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| MAR Form 102 | Purchase and Sale Agreement | Contains all contingency deadlines |
| MAR Form 103 | Addendum to Purchase Agreement | Extends or modifies deadlines |
| MAR Form 108 | Inspection Addendum | Specifies inspection response windows |
| MAR Form 120 | Financing Contingency Addendum | Sets loan commitment deadlines |
| MAR Form 104 | Release of Purchase Agreement | Formally terminates if deadline failures occur |

The [Maine Real Estate Commission](https://www.maine.gov/pfr/professionallicensing/professions/real-estate/real-estate-commission) has enforcement authority under 32 MRSA § 13197 to discipline licensees who fail to exercise reasonable skill and care in transaction management. Deadline mismanagement falls squarely within this statutory language.

Every deadline in Form 102 is calculated from the "Effective Date," which is defined as the date the last party signs the fully executed agreement. Agents who miscalculate this starting point set the entire timeline on the wrong track from day one.

## Key Contingency Deadlines and How They Interact

Maine purchase agreements typically contain three to five contingencies, each with its own deadline. The interaction between these deadlines creates a cascading sequence that agents must manage simultaneously.

The inspection contingency—governed by MAR Form 108—is usually the first to expire. Buyers must complete inspections and deliver their response (accept, request repairs, or terminate) within the negotiated window. If the buyer fails to deliver a written response by the deadline, most Form 108 language treats the property condition as accepted.

**The financing contingency under MAR Form 120 requires the buyer to provide evidence of loan commitment by a date specified in the contract.** This is not the same as a pre-approval letter. Agents who confuse these two documents put their buyers at risk of losing contingency protection. The financing deadline often falls 21 to 30 days after the effective date, though this is fully negotiable.

The appraisal contingency, when included as a separate provision, runs concurrently with financing but has its own notification requirement. If the property appraises below the purchase price, the buyer must notify the seller in writing within the timeframe stated in the agreement—not simply wait for the lender to issue a denial.

Title review contingencies give the buyer a specified number of days to object to title defects after receiving the title commitment. Under Maine practice, attorneys typically handle closings, and the buyer's attorney reviews title. But the agent remains responsible for ensuring the title commitment is ordered promptly enough to allow review within the contingency window.

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

The consequences of missed contingency deadlines in Maine fall into three categories: contractual, regulatory, and financial.

From a contractual standpoint, a missed deadline typically waives the buyer's protection. The buyer loses the right to terminate under that contingency and must proceed to closing or face breach-of-contract liability. If the buyer walks away after a waived contingency, the seller can retain the earnest money deposit under the liquidated damages provisions of MAR Form 102.

Regulatory consequences come from MREC. Under 32 MRSA § 13197, the Commission can impose sanctions including license suspension, mandatory continuing education, fines up to $1,500 per violation, and consent agreements that become part of the agent's public record. Recent MREC enforcement bulletins have specifically referenced failure to track contractual deadlines as evidence of incompetent practice.

**Financial liability extends beyond lost earnest money.** If a buyer suffers damages because their agent failed to properly track or communicate a contingency deadline, the agent and brokerage face potential civil liability. Maine courts have recognized that agents owe fiduciary duties including competent management of transaction timelines. A [missed deadline](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) that costs a buyer their inspection remedy or financing escape hatch can result in claims that exceed the commission earned on the transaction.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Maine Contingency Deadlines

Three to five errors appear repeatedly in MREC disciplinary files and brokerage E&O claims. Understanding these patterns is the fastest way to audit your own practice.

**Mistake 1: Miscalculating the Effective Date.** The effective date is when the last party signs—not when the offer was submitted, not when it was verbally accepted, and not when the agent received the signed copy. If the seller signs at 11 p.m. on a Tuesday but the buyer's agent doesn't receive the executed contract until Wednesday morning, the effective date is still Tuesday. Agents who start counting from receipt rather than execution lose a full day on every deadline.

**Mistake 2: Relying on verbal communications for contingency actions.** Maine's Statute of Frauds (33 MRSA § 51) requires written modifications to real property contracts. An agent who calls the listing agent and says "my buyer wants to extend the inspection deadline" has accomplished nothing legally binding. Without a signed MAR Form 103 addendum, the original deadline stands. This mistake appears in MREC disciplinary actions year after year.

**Mistake 3: Confusing calendar days with business days.** MAR Form 102 calculates deadlines in calendar days unless otherwise specified. Agents accustomed to other states' forms sometimes assume weekends and holidays are excluded. In Maine, a 10-day inspection window that starts on a Friday includes the following weekend days in the count. The only exception under MAR forms is when a deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or Maine state holiday—in which case it extends to the next business day.

**Mistake 4: Failing to calendar the response window after an inspection request.** When a buyer submits repair requests under Form 108, the seller has a specified number of days to respond. Many buyer's agents track the initial inspection deadline but forget to calendar the seller's response deadline. If the seller fails to respond in time, the default provision in the form controls—and agents who don't know what that default says cannot advise their clients properly.

**Mistake 5: Not confirming receipt of contingency notices.** Delivering a contingency removal or termination notice by email is permissible under MAR Form 102's notice provisions, but the sending agent must confirm actual receipt. An email that bounces or lands in spam has not been "delivered" under Maine contract law. Agents should request read receipts or follow up with confirmation from the receiving agent.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Maine brokers carry supervisory responsibility under MREC Rule Chapter 10, which requires designated brokers to maintain systems for reviewing and managing transactions conducted by their agents. This is not optional oversight—it is a licensing obligation.

| Broker Audit Item | Frequency | Risk If Missed |
|-------------------|-----------|----------------|
| Effective date accuracy on all contracts | Every transaction | Cascading deadline errors |
| Written contingency modifications on file | Every modification | Statute of Frauds violations |
| Deadline tracking system in use | Quarterly review | Systemic compliance failure |
| Agent competency on MAR form updates | Annual training | Outdated form usage |
| Earnest money handling tied to contingency outcomes | Every deposit | Trust account violations |

Brokers should require agents to submit a deadline summary sheet within 24 hours of full execution. This sheet should list every contingency, its deadline date, and the form number governing it. Without this basic documentation, brokers cannot fulfill their supervisory duty.

MREC has signaled through recent consent agreements that "I didn't know my agent missed the deadline" is not an acceptable defense for designated brokers. The Commission expects brokers to have affirmative systems in place—not reactive responses after a complaint is filed. Reviewing [why checklists beat memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) is relevant here because systematic tracking replaces the individual agent's recall with a repeatable process.

Brokers should also audit whether agents are using current-year MAR forms. Form revisions happen regularly, and outdated forms may contain contingency language that has been superseded. Using a 2024 Form 108 in a 2026 transaction creates ambiguity that neither party benefits from.

The failures happen in execution—specifically, in the gap between knowing a deadline exists and actually acting on it before it passes.

This buffer accounts for the time needed to obtain client signatures on extension addenda or termination notices.
