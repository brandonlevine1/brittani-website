## Idaho Continuing Education Requirements 2026: The Rules That Will Keep Your License Active

Understanding the Idaho continuing education requirements 2026 is not optional homework—it is the difference between an active license and an involuntary career pause. The Idaho Real Estate Commission (IREC), operating under Idaho Code Title 54, Chapter 20, sets the standards every active and inactive licensee must meet. Miss a deadline or take the wrong course, and you could face penalties ranging from forced inactivity to a $5,000 fine per transaction conducted on a lapsed license.

Idaho's renewal cycle runs on a two-year schedule tied to your original licensure date—not a universal calendar deadline. That individual timeline catches agents off guard every cycle, especially those who transferred in from states with a single annual renewal date. The stakes are real: IREC reported over 400 late renewals in the 2023-2024 cycle, many of which triggered broker-level compliance headaches.

## How Many Hours and What Courses Are Required

**Active Idaho licensees must complete 18 hours of approved continuing education within their two-year renewal cycle.** Of those 18 hours, a minimum of 2 hours must come from the IREC-designated core course, which changes topic each cycle. The remaining 16 hours can come from any course approved by the Commission, covering subjects like contracts, fair housing, risk management, or ethics.

For the 2025-2026 cycle (which determines your 2026 renewal eligibility), the mandatory core course addresses updated agency disclosure protocols and advertising compliance under IREC Rule 101. Agents renewing with an active status must also certify that they have completed at least one hour of Idaho-specific content beyond the core course. That additional hour must address state statutes—not generic national content repackaged with an Idaho label.

Inactive licensees have a different bar: they need only 12 hours per cycle, but the 2-hour core course remains mandatory. If you plan to reactivate, you must complete the full 18 hours plus submit Form IRE-2 (Application for License Reactivation) along with a $75 reactivation fee.

| Status | Total Hours Required | Core Course | Elective Hours | Renewal Fee |
|--------|---------------------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Active Salesperson | 18 | 2 (mandatory) | 16 | $150 |
| Active Broker | 18 | 2 (mandatory) | 16 | $150 |
| Inactive Salesperson | 12 | 2 (mandatory) | 10 | $75 |
| Inactive Broker | 12 | 2 (mandatory) | 10 | $75 |
| Reactivation | 18 + Form IRE-2 | 2 (mandatory) | 16 | $75 + $150 |

The IREC maintains an [approved provider and course list on its website](https://irec.idaho.gov/) that is updated quarterly. Do not assume a course you took through a national platform counts unless you verify its Idaho approval number.

## Deadlines, Grace Periods, and What Expiration Really Means

Your license expires at midnight on the last day of your birth month in even or odd years, depending on your license number. There is no blanket "everyone renews on December 31" rule. IREC sends a reminder notice roughly 60 days before expiration, but that notice is a courtesy—not a legal requirement. You are responsible for tracking your own date.

**Idaho does not offer a grace period for CE completion after your expiration date.** If your license expires without all 18 hours on file, it immediately moves to inactive status. You cannot list, show, negotiate, or close transactions during inactivity. Any commission earned while inactive is subject to disgorgement under Idaho Code 54-2053, meaning you may be ordered to return fees already paid to you.

If your license stays inactive for more than two consecutive years, you must retake the Idaho state exam—not just the CE hours. Agents who have been licensed for decades sometimes assume longevity grants them leniency. It does not.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance: Fines, Liability, and Deal Risk

Idaho Code 54-2060 authorizes civil penalties of up to $5,000 per violation for practicing real estate without a current active license. The IREC can also issue formal complaints leading to license revocation proceedings, which become part of your permanent disciplinary record on the national ARELLO database. That record follows you across state lines if you ever seek reciprocity.

From a transaction standpoint, a deal negotiated by an agent with an expired license creates grounds for contract rescission by the other party. Courts in Idaho have held that licensure is a condition precedent to earning commission. If opposing counsel discovers your lapse, your client can walk, your commission disappears, and your E&O insurance may deny coverage because you were operating outside the scope of your license.

Brokers face vicarious liability here too. Under IREC Rule 400, the designated broker is responsible for ensuring every affiliated agent holds a current active license. If you are a broker running a team or managing a brokerage, every unlicensed day an agent spends in production is your regulatory exposure. This connects directly to [why agents miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines)—the operational chaos of a busy pipeline makes administrative compliance feel like background noise until it becomes a five-figure problem.

## Common Mistakes Idaho Agents Make With CE Compliance

Mistake number one: completing 18 hours of elective coursework but forgetting the mandatory core course. IREC will not credit your renewal unless the core course appears on your transcript. Every cycle, agents call the Commission in a panic during their final week because they assumed any 18 hours would satisfy the requirement.

Mistake number two: taking courses from unapproved providers. Online platforms that serve multiple states sometimes offer courses labeled "applicable in Idaho" that have not actually received IREC approval. The Commission assigns a specific Idaho course approval number to every qualifying offering. If your certificate does not list that number, the hours will not post to your record.

**Mistake number three: relying on the provider to report your completion to IREC.** Approved providers are required to report within 10 business days, but delays and data errors happen. Agents should confirm their transcript through the IREC online portal within two weeks of finishing any course. Do not wait until renewal month to discover that 6 hours never posted.

Mistake number four: double-dipping hours across renewal cycles. Idaho does not allow you to carry excess hours from one cycle into the next. If you completed 24 hours in your last cycle, those extra 6 hours vanish—they do not reduce your next cycle's obligation.

Mistake number five: confusing Idaho's requirements with those of a neighboring state. Agents licensed in both Idaho and Washington or Utah sometimes assume reciprocity extends to CE acceptance. It does not. Each state evaluates courses independently, and Idaho-specific content cannot be substituted with another state's core material. ## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

**Designated brokers in Idaho carry a statutory obligation under Idaho Code 54-2038 to supervise affiliated licensees, which includes verifying current license status.** This is not a suggestion—it is an affirmative duty. IREC can discipline a broker who allows an unlicensed or lapsed agent to conduct business under the brokerage's name, even if the broker was unaware of the lapse.

Brokers should implement a quarterly license audit. Pull each agent's status from the IREC online verification system and cross-reference it against your internal roster. Set calendar alerts 90 days before each agent's renewal date. Document these checks in writing—if IREC ever audits your brokerage, a paper trail of proactive compliance reviews is your best defense.

| Broker Responsibility | Frequency | Documentation |
|----------------------|-----------|---------------|
| Verify agent license status | Quarterly | Screenshot or PDF of IREC portal |
| Confirm CE transcript current | 90 days before agent renewal | Email confirmation from agent |
| Remove lapsed agents from MLS | Immediately upon expiration | MLS removal receipt |
| File Form IRE-6 for terminated agents | Within 10 days of separation | Signed copy retained |
| Report unlicensed activity | Upon discovery | Written incident report to IREC |

When an agent's license lapses, the broker must immediately remove that agent's listings from the MLS and reassign any pending transactions to a licensed agent. Failure to do so exposes the brokerage to claims of unauthorized practice facilitation. Form IRE-6 (Notification of Transfer or Termination) must be filed within 10 business days anytime an agent leaves or is removed, regardless of the reason.

Brokers managing large teams know that [checklists beat memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) when it comes to compliance tracking. A single spreadsheet with renewal dates, CE completion status, and IREC verification screenshots can save thousands in potential penalties.

## Planning Ahead: How to Stay Compliant Without Scrambling

The most effective approach is front-loading your CE hours in the first year of your cycle. Complete at least 12 hours—including the core course—within the first 12 months after your last renewal. That leaves only 6 elective hours for year two, which is manageable even during peak selling season.

Keep digital copies of every completion certificate in a dedicated folder, labeled with the IREC approval number and date. If a provider fails to report your hours, you will need that certificate as proof when filing a manual credit request with IREC via Form IRE-9 (Continuing Education Credit Request).

Set two calendar reminders: one at 180 days before your renewal and one at 90 days. The 180-day alert is your signal to check your transcript and identify any gaps. The 90-day alert is your hard deadline—if anything is missing, you still have time to complete a course and have it reported before expiration.
