## Understanding Oregon Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Every licensed real estate professional in Oregon operates under the authority of the Oregon Real Estate Agency (OREA), the regulatory body that administers licensing, enforcement, and education standards under Oregon Revised Statutes Chapter 696. If you hold an active license, the Oregon continuing education requirements 2026 cycle demands your attention now—not three weeks before your renewal date.

Oregon mandates 30 hours of approved continuing education for every two-year renewal period. That number has not changed from prior cycles, but the content requirements and enforcement mechanisms have grown sharper. The OREA has signaled increased auditing of CE compliance, particularly around mandatory topic areas that some agents treat as optional.

Your renewal date depends on when you were first licensed, not on a universal calendar deadline. This staggered system means some agents renew in early 2026, others in late 2026, and some won't face their next renewal until 2027. Check your specific expiration date through the [OREA eLicense portal](https://orea.elicense.irondata.com/).

## Mandatory Course Breakdown and Hour Allocation

**Not all 30 hours are created equal—Oregon prescribes specific mandatory topics that must be completed before elective hours count.** The structure is defined under OAR 863-020-0035 and leaves less flexibility than many agents assume.

Here is the breakdown of required hours:

| Category | Hours Required | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-------|
| Oregon Agency Law | 3 | Must be OREA-approved course specifically on agency relationships |
| Oregon Law and Rule Required Course (OLRRC) | 3 | Updated each biennium by the OREA with current rule changes |
| Fair Housing / Civil Rights | 3 | Federal and state protected classes |
| Elective CE (approved topics) | 21 | Must be from OREA-certified providers |
| **Total** | **30** | All must be completed before renewal date |

The OLRRC is a course the OREA redesigns each renewal cycle. For the 2025-2026 biennium, it covers recent legislative changes, updated administrative rules, and enforcement actions. You cannot substitute a generic law update course from another state. The course must carry the OREA's specific approval number for the current cycle.

Elective hours can cover topics like contracts, property management, appraisal, risk management, or technology—so long as the provider holds active OREA certification under OAR 863-020-0020.

## The Renewal Process: Forms and Filing

Oregon uses electronic renewal through the eLicense system. The primary form you will interact with is the **License Renewal Application**, submitted online. There is no paper form number in the traditional sense—Oregon moved fully digital for renewals several years ago—but you must ensure your CE completion records are uploaded or reported by your course provider before you submit.

Course providers are required to report completions to the OREA within 10 days under OAR 863-020-0055. However, agents should verify their transcript shows all 30 hours before clicking submit. If the system shows incomplete hours, your renewal will be denied and your license will lapse on its expiration date.

The renewal fee for 2026 remains $300 for brokers (what Oregon calls its standard licensee class) and $300 for principal brokers. Late renewals within 30 days of expiration incur a $50 late fee. Beyond that window, reactivation becomes significantly more complex and costly.

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

**Practicing on an expired or inactive license is not a gray area—it is a violation of ORS 696.020 and carries both regulatory and civil consequences.** The OREA treats this as unlicensed activity, regardless of whether the lapse was intentional.

Under ORS 696.990, civil penalties can reach $1,000 per violation. Each transaction conducted while unlicensed may constitute a separate violation. Beyond fines, any commission earned during a period of non-licensure is potentially unenforceable. A seller or buyer who discovers their agent was unlicensed during a transaction has grounds to void the agreement or pursue damages.

If your license has been inactive for more than two years due to CE non-completion, Oregon requires you to retake the state portion of the licensing exam. More than three years inactive, and you must retake both the national and state exams. This is codified in OAR 863-014-0025.

Insurance implications matter too. Errors and omissions coverage typically excludes acts performed without a valid license. If a client files a complaint with the OREA during a period you were technically lapsed, your E&O carrier may deny the claim entirely.

## Common Mistakes Oregon Agents Make with CE Compliance

Five errors surface repeatedly in OREA enforcement actions and broker audits. Each is avoidable with minimal planning, yet they account for the majority of lapsed licenses and disciplinary actions in the state.

**First, agents confuse "enrolled" with "completed."** Registering for a course is not the same as finishing it. The OREA transcript only reflects completed courses where you passed any required assessment. Agents who sign up for classes in November and assume they are covered for a December renewal routinely discover their transcript is blank.

Second, agents take courses from non-approved providers. A CE course approved in Washington or California does not automatically count in Oregon. Every course must carry an active OREA approval number. The Agency publishes a searchable list of [approved providers and courses](https://www.oregon.gov/rea/licensing/pages/continuing-education.aspx) on its website.

Third, agents double-count hours. Oregon does not allow the same course to be taken twice in one renewal cycle for credit. If you took the fair housing course in year one of your biennium and retook it in year two, only one instance counts. This leaves agents unexpectedly short when they check their transcripts.

Fourth, agents ignore the mandatory topic requirements and fill all 30 hours with electives. The system will not flag this during enrollment—it only becomes apparent at renewal when the application is denied for missing the required 3-hour Agency course or the OLRRC.

Fifth, agents who [miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) on other transaction tasks tend to miss CE deadlines too. It is a pattern problem, not an isolated one.

## What Principal Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Oregon places direct supervisory responsibility on principal brokers under ORS 696.022. This is not a suggestion—it is a statutory duty. If an agent under your supervision practices on a lapsed license, you share exposure.

| Broker Responsibility | Statutory Basis | Practical Action |
|----------------------|-----------------|------------------|
| Verify active license status of all affiliated agents | ORS 696.022 | Check eLicense portal quarterly |
| Ensure agents meet CE requirements before renewal | OAR 863-014-0010 | Maintain internal tracking calendar |
| Report known violations to OREA | ORS 696.800 | Document and submit within 10 days |
| Supervise transaction activity of all licensees | ORS 696.022(1) | Audit closed files for license-active dates |
| Maintain records of agent credentials | OAR 863-015-0005 | Digital file per agent, updated annually |

**Principal brokers who rely on agents to self-report their CE status are accepting unnecessary risk.** A single agent practicing on a lapsed license can expose the brokerage to regulatory action, client complaints, and potential commission disgorgement across every transaction that agent touched.

Build a system that flags renewal dates 90 days out. Require agents to share their OREA transcript at least 60 days before expiration. If you manage a team of more than five agents, manual tracking becomes unreliable—this is exactly the kind of compliance tracking where [checklists and automated systems](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) outperform human memory.

Some brokerages now include CE compliance as a condition of their independent contractor agreement. If the agent fails to maintain an active license, the agreement automatically terminates. This protects the brokerage from vicarious liability.
