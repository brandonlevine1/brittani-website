## How the NAR Settlement Reshaped Commission Rules in South Dakota

The South Dakota commission structure rules after the NAR settlement 2026 have fundamentally changed how agents on both sides of a transaction discuss, document, and collect compensation. The August 2024 practice changes mandated by the National Association of Realtors settlement didn't just affect coastal markets with million-dollar listings. They landed squarely in Sioux Falls split-levels and Rapid City ranches alike, and two years in, enforcement has only tightened.

South Dakota's real estate market operates under the authority of the [South Dakota Real Estate Commission (SDREC)](https://dlr.sd.gov/realestate/), which regulates licensees under SDCL Title 36, Chapter 36-21A. The commission has incorporated the settlement's practice changes into its supervisory expectations, meaning that a failure to comply is no longer just an MLS policy issue. It is a licensing issue.

**Every agent and broker in the state should treat these rules as non-optional regulatory obligations, not industry suggestions.** The consequences for noncompliance range from MLS fines to license suspension, and in worst-case scenarios, deal rescission and civil liability.

## Written Buyer Agreements: The Non-Negotiable Starting Point

Before the settlement, many South Dakota buyer agents operated on a handshake understanding that compensation would come from the listing side. That era is over. A written buyer representation agreement must be executed before an agent provides any substantive services to a buyer, including touring properties.

The South Dakota Association of REALTORS (SDAR) publishes standardized forms for this purpose. The key document is the **SDAR Buyer Representation Agreement**, which requires agents to disclose the specific amount or rate of compensation the buyer will owe. Vague language like "customary commission" or "to be determined" fails to satisfy the settlement's requirements and exposes the agent to enforcement action.

**The compensation field in the buyer agreement must be specific and objective.** This means a dollar amount, a percentage, or a formula that produces a definitive number. Open-ended terms are treated as non-compliant by MLS auditors and, increasingly, by the SDREC during complaint investigations.

## MLS Compensation Fields Are Gone — Here Is What Replaced Them

The most visible change under the settlement was the elimination of blanket offers of buyer agent compensation in every MLS. In South Dakota, the Realtor Association of the Black Hills MLS, the SDAR MLS, and other regional systems all stripped their compensation fields in compliance with the August 2024 deadline.

This does not mean sellers cannot contribute to buyer agent compensation. It means the mechanism for communicating that offer has moved off-platform. Sellers and listing agents can still offer concessions or agree to pay the buyer's agent, but those arrangements must be documented through direct negotiation, typically reflected in the purchase agreement or a separate compensation addendum.

| Pre-Settlement Practice | Post-Settlement Practice |
|---|---|
| Listing agent enters buyer agent commission in MLS | No commission offers permitted in any MLS field |
| Buyer agents assume compensation from listing side | Buyer agents must have written agreement specifying their fee |
| Commission split disclosed only in MLS | Compensation negotiated directly between parties |
| Buyer rarely signs compensation agreement | Written buyer representation agreement required before touring |
| Seller implicitly pays both sides | Seller contribution negotiated separately, off-MLS |

## Specific South Dakota Forms and Documents Agents Must Know

South Dakota agents rely on a combination of SDAR-published forms and brokerage-specific documents. While brokerage agreements can be customized, they must still satisfy both the NAR settlement requirements and SDREC rules under SDCL 36-21A and ARSD 20:69.

Here are the forms every South Dakota agent should have in their transaction file:

The **SDAR Buyer Representation Agreement** is the primary document establishing the buyer-agent relationship and disclosing compensation. The **SDAR Exclusive Right to Sell Listing Agreement** remains the standard listing contract, but agents must ensure it no longer references MLS-distributed buyer agent compensation. Any reference to a cooperating broker commission offered through the MLS should be struck or removed.

For transactions where the seller agrees to contribute to the buyer agent's fee, agents should use a **Seller Concession Addendum** or document the agreement within the purchase contract itself. SDAR has issued guidance recommending that seller-paid buyer agent compensation be addressed in a dedicated section of the purchase agreement to avoid ambiguity during closing.

The **SDAR Agency Disclosure Form** also remains required under SDCL 36-21A-77 and ARSD 20:69:04:01. This form must be presented at the first substantive contact with a consumer, and it now works in tandem with the buyer representation agreement to establish both the nature of the relationship and its financial terms. Treating these as separate compliance obligations is a mistake many agents make, and it is addressed further below.

## What Happens When an Agent Fails to Comply

The penalties for noncompliance operate on multiple tracks, and they can stack.

**At the MLS level**, violations of the compensation-field prohibition or failure to verify buyer representation agreements can trigger fines typically ranging from $500 to $5,000 per incident, depending on the MLS. Repeated violations may result in MLS access suspension, which effectively prevents an agent from listing or searching properties.

At the state licensing level, the SDREC has authority under SDCL 36-21A-72 to investigate complaints and impose sanctions including reprimand, license suspension, and license revocation. The commission has signaled through its 2025 and 2026 bulletins that settlement compliance falls within the scope of "competent practice," meaning violations could be prosecuted as a failure to meet professional standards.

At the transaction level, a missing or defective buyer representation agreement could give a buyer grounds to dispute the agent's compensation claim entirely. If a buyer closes on a home and later discovers their agent never had a compliant written agreement, the agent may face a civil claim for disgorgement of fees. South Dakota courts have historically looked unfavorably on agents who cannot produce proper documentation, and the post-settlement environment has only raised the bar.

## Common Mistakes South Dakota Agents Are Making Right Now

Two years into the new rules, certain errors keep appearing in transaction audits and SDREC complaints. Here are the five most frequent.

**First, agents are using outdated listing agreement forms that still reference MLS-distributed buyer agent compensation.** Some brokerages have not updated their templates since 2024, and agents pulling old PDFs from shared drives are unknowingly creating non-compliant listings. Every listing agreement executed in 2026 should be reviewed against current SDAR templates.

Second, buyer agents are obtaining signed buyer representation agreements but leaving the compensation field incomplete or using conditional language. Phrases like "up to 3%" or "as negotiated at closing" do not satisfy the specificity requirement. The number must be fixed or calculated by a clear formula at the time of signing.

Third, some agents are verbally communicating seller-offered buyer agent compensation during showings or open houses. Under the settlement rules, this information can be shared, but only in response to a direct inquiry and only after confirming the buyer has a written representation agreement. Proactively advertising compensation amounts at open houses or in listing remarks is a violation.

Fourth, dual agency transactions in South Dakota are creating confusion about which agreements must be in place. Even when an agent represents both parties with proper disclosure under SDCL 36-21A-77, a written buyer representation agreement with compensation terms is still required for the buyer side. Dual agency does not waive the buyer agreement obligation.

Fifth, agents are neglecting to keep copies of signed buyer representation agreements in the transaction file. The SDREC requires that licensees maintain transaction records for at least three years under ARSD 20:69:03:02. A verbal claim that an agreement existed is not a defense during an audit.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers carry supervisory liability under SDCL 36-21A-56 for the actions of their affiliated licensees. The settlement-era changes have expanded the practical scope of that responsibility.

**Every managing broker in South Dakota should be conducting quarterly audits of transaction files to verify three things.** First, that every buyer-side transaction includes a signed buyer representation agreement with specific, compliant compensation terms. Second, that no active listing includes language or MLS entries referencing buyer agent compensation offers. Third, that agency disclosure forms are present and dated prior to the buyer representation agreement.

Brokers should also review their office policies and training materials. The SDREC has increasingly emphasized that brokers who fail to train agents on post-settlement requirements are themselves at risk of disciplinary action. A written office policy memo documenting the new procedures, with agent acknowledgment signatures, creates a defensible record if a complaint is filed.

For brokerages handling high transaction volumes, manual auditing becomes unsustainable quickly. Automating the review of required documents — checking that buyer agreements, agency disclosures, and compensation addenda are present and properly executed — reduces the risk of a violation slipping through.
