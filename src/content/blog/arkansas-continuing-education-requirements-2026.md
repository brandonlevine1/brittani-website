## Understanding Arkansas Continuing Education Requirements 2026

The Arkansas Real Estate Commission (AREC) does not give grace periods, does not send reminder postcards, and does not care that your closing schedule was insane in August. If you hold an active license in this state and fail to meet the Arkansas continuing education requirements 2026 cycle mandates, your ability to practice stops cold on October 1. That is not a metaphor — it is the statutory outcome defined under Arkansas Code Annotated Section 17-42-307.

Every active salesperson must complete seven hours of approved continuing education each license year, which runs from October 1 through September 30. Principal brokers owe the state ten hours: the same seven required of all licensees plus three additional hours of broker-management coursework. These requirements have remained structurally consistent since AREC's last major rule revision, but the designated core course content changes annually, which means last year's completion does not satisfy this year's obligation.

AREC publishes its list of approved providers and the annual core course topic each spring through its official [course approval page](https://www.arec.arkansas.gov/education/continuing-education/). Agents who assume any generic online CE course counts often discover their error only when their license fails to renew — a preventable disaster that costs time, money, and sometimes active transactions.

## The Exact Hour Breakdown and Course Structure

**The distinction between the mandatory core course and elective hours trips up more agents than any other CE detail in Arkansas.** Of your seven required hours, six must come from the AREC-designated annual core course. That course covers a topic chosen by the Commission each year — often reflecting new legislation, recent disciplinary trends, or federal regulatory updates that affect Arkansas practitioners.

The remaining one elective hour can come from any AREC-approved continuing education offering. Some agents stack this with the core course if their provider offers a seven-hour bundle, while others pick a standalone topic like fair housing updates or contract law refreshers.

| License Type | Total CE Hours | Core Course Hours | Elective Hours | Broker-Management Hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salesperson (Active) | 7 | 6 | 1 | 0 |
| Principal Broker | 10 | 6 | 1 | 3 |
| Associate Broker | 7 | 6 | 1 | 0 |
| Inactive (reactivating) | All missed years | Per year missed | Per year missed | If broker, per year missed |

Associate brokers follow the same schedule as salespersons unless they serve as the principal broker of a firm, in which case the ten-hour requirement applies. There is no partial credit for courses abandoned midway, and AREC does not accept CE completed before the start of the current license year on October 1.

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

The consequences of missing your CE deadline extend well beyond an administrative inconvenience. Under AREC Rule 7.4, a license that is not renewed by September 30 automatically becomes inactive. An inactive licensee cannot list property, write offers, negotiate contracts, collect commissions, or hold themselves out as a licensed agent in any capacity.

**If you close a transaction while your license is inactive, the Commission can pursue disciplinary action including fines up to $5,000 per violation under ACA Section 17-42-311.** Your broker also faces exposure — AREC holds principal brokers responsible for ensuring that every agent operating under their supervision maintains active licensure. A broker who allows an unlicensed or inactive agent to transact business risks their own license and can be held jointly liable for consumer harm.

Beyond fines, transactions closed by an improperly licensed agent may be voidable. Buyers or sellers who discover the irregularity can use it as grounds to unwind a deal or pursue civil damages. Title companies increasingly run license verification checks before disbursing funds, meaning your deal might not even reach the closing table if your status shows inactive in the AREC database.

Reactivation requires completing all CE hours for each missed annual cycle, passing any applicable examination if the license has been inactive for more than two years, and paying accumulated late fees. The financial and reputational cost of letting CE lapse dwarfs the seven hours of coursework required to stay current.

## Common Mistakes Arkansas Agents Make With CE

Five errors appear repeatedly in AREC disciplinary records and license-renewal rejections. Recognizing them now saves you from becoming a cautionary tale your broker emails to the rest of the office.

**First, agents complete courses from non-approved providers.** Arkansas does not honor CE from providers who lack specific AREC approval, regardless of whether those courses carry approval from another state's commission. The AREC-approved provider list is the only authority. Taking a course approved by the Texas Real Estate Commission, for example, earns you zero credit in Arkansas unless that provider also holds separate AREC approval.

Second, agents confuse the license renewal date with the CE completion deadline. Your CE must be completed and reported by September 30. The renewal application and fee are a separate administrative step. Completing CE on September 29 but failing to submit your renewal form still results in an inactive license.

Third, agents fail to retain certificates of completion. AREC Rule 7.5 requires licensees to maintain documentation of CE completion. While providers report electronically, system errors occur. Agents without backup documentation have no recourse when the Commission's records do not reflect their completed hours.

Fourth, brokers who changed firms mid-year assume their new principal broker handled transfer paperwork that includes CE verification. It does not. Your CE obligation follows your individual license, not your brokerage affiliation. Firm transitions do not pause, reset, or transfer your compliance timeline.

Fifth, agents take the prior year's core course content thinking it satisfies the current year. AREC designates a new core course topic annually. Completing 2025's core course after October 1, 2025 does not satisfy the 2026 license year requirement unless AREC explicitly carries forward that topic designation — which it rarely does.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Principal brokers in Arkansas carry a supervisory duty that extends explicitly to CE compliance monitoring. AREC Rule 10.1 requires principal brokers to maintain current records of every licensee affiliated with their firm, including verification that each agent has completed annual CE requirements.

| Broker Responsibility | Regulatory Basis | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Verify agent CE completion | AREC Rule 10.1 | Annually before Sept 30 |
| Maintain agent license documentation | AREC Rule 10.2 | Ongoing |
| Report agent termination or transfer | Form AREC-004 | Within 10 days of event |
| Ensure advertising compliance for active licensees only | ACA 17-42-313 | Ongoing |
| Respond to AREC audit requests | AREC Rule 10.5 | As requested |

Brokers should not rely on agents' verbal assurances that their CE is done. AREC's online license lookup tool allows brokers to verify each agent's status in real time. A thirty-minute audit each August — checking every affiliated licensee's status — prevents the catastrophic scenario of discovering an agent closed deals while technically inactive.

When an agent's license lapses, the broker must immediately remove that agent from all active listings, pending transactions, and firm advertising. Failure to do so exposes the brokerage to Commission discipline and civil liability. The form to report an agent's disassociation is AREC-004, which must be filed within ten calendar days of the triggering event.

## Approved Course Delivery Methods and Provider Standards

AREC approves continuing education delivered through classroom instruction, live webinar (synchronous online), and asynchronous online self-study formats. Each delivery method must meet specific AREC standards for interactivity, assessment, and time verification. Online self-study courses, for instance, must include timed segments that prevent students from advancing without spending the minimum required time on each module.

Providers must apply to AREC for course approval each year using the provider application process outlined in AREC Rule 8. Approval from a prior year does not automatically carry forward. This is why agents occasionally discover that a provider they used successfully last year no longer appears on the approved list — the provider may have failed to renew their own AREC approval.

Agents should confirm provider approval status before enrolling by checking the AREC website directly. A certificate from a non-approved provider is worthless to the Commission regardless of the course quality or the agent's time investment.

## How CE Connects to Transaction Compliance

Continuing education is not an isolated administrative task — it feeds directly into transaction competency and [avoiding the breakdowns that kill deals](/blog/where-deals-break-down). The annual core course frequently addresses changes to Arkansas-specific contract forms, disclosure obligations, and agency law interpretations that affect how agents handle live transactions.

For the 2026 cycle, agents should pay attention to any core course content addressing updates to the AREC-promulgated contract forms, including the standard Offer and Acceptance form and the Property Condition Disclosure Statement (Form AREC-PCD). Agents who complete CE passively — clicking through slides without absorbing content — miss material that directly affects their legal obligations in active deals.
