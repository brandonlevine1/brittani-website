## Why Arkansas Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026 Demand Your Attention

Missing a single deadline in an Arkansas residential transaction can cost your buyer their earnest money, expose your brokerage to an Arkansas Real Estate Commission complaint, or kill a deal that took weeks to negotiate. Understanding Arkansas buyer contingency deadlines 2026 is not optional knowledge — it is the baseline competency that separates agents who close from agents who face disciplinary action. The Arkansas Real Estate Commission (AREC) has made clear through enforcement actions that ignorance of contract timelines is not a defense.

The stakes are higher this year because market conditions have tightened seller patience. Sellers and their agents are more willing to declare a buyer in default when a deadline lapses, particularly in competitive price bands below $350,000. Every day you allow a contingency clock to tick without action is a day closer to a formal dispute or commission complaint.

## The Forms That Govern Buyer Contingencies in Arkansas

The Arkansas REALTORS Association (ARA) publishes the standard contract forms used in the vast majority of residential transactions statewide. The primary document is the **ARA Sales Contract (Form 1)**, which contains the contingency framework agents must follow. Supplemental forms add layers of deadline obligation that compound quickly.

**Form 2 (Addendum to Sales Contract)** is where agents add custom contingency language, including sale-of-property contingencies and extended financing conditions. Form 5 (Property Inspection Response) governs the inspection negotiation timeline. Form 6 (Amendment to Contract) is required to memorialize any deadline extension agreed to by both parties.

| Form Number | Purpose | Key Deadline Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Form 1 | Sales Contract | Inspection, financing, appraisal, closing date |
| Form 2 | Addendum | Sale-of-property contingency, custom deadlines |
| Form 5 | Inspection Response | Buyer's repair request submission |
| Form 6 | Amendment | Deadline extensions, modified terms |
| Form 9 | Release of Contract | Formal termination when deadline allows |

Each form references specific calendar-day counts that begin running from the "binding agreement date" — defined in Form 1 as the date the last party signs or initials acceptance. Agents who miscalculate binding agreement date miscalculate every downstream deadline.

## Statutory and Regulatory Framework

Arkansas Code Annotated Section 17-42-311 grants AREC authority to discipline licensees for "failure to exercise reasonable skill and care" in representing clients. A pattern of missed deadlines or poor deadline communication falls squarely within this provision. AREC Rule 10.6 further requires agents to present all offers and counteroffers promptly, which includes time-sensitive contingency responses.

> "A licensee shall not be guilty of negligent or incompetent performance of any act for which a license is required." — Ark. Code Ann. Section 17-42-311(a)(6)

The [Arkansas Real Estate Commission](https://www.arec.arkansas.gov/) publishes disciplinary orders quarterly, and multiple cases in 2024 and 2025 involved agents who failed to communicate inspection deadlines to buyers or who did not submit financing contingency removals on time. Penalties ranged from formal reprimands to $1,000 fines and mandatory continuing education hours. License suspension remains on the table for repeat offenders.

AREC Rule 10.10 places supervisory responsibility on designated brokers. If an agent in your office habitually misses deadlines, the broker's license is at risk alongside the agent's. This is not theoretical — AREC has pursued brokers in cases where supervision systems were found to be absent or inadequate.

## The Anatomy of Arkansas Contingency Timelines

**The inspection contingency is where most deals blow up.** Form 1 requires the buyer to complete inspections and deliver written notice of deficiencies (using Form 5) within the number of days specified in the blank. Once Form 5 is delivered, the seller typically has a set response period — commonly 3 to 5 days — to accept, reject, or counter the repair requests.

The financing contingency operates differently. The buyer must apply for financing within the days specified in Form 1 and must provide written notice if financing is denied. Failure to notify within the deadline means the contingency is deemed satisfied. The buyer cannot later claim financing fell through and demand earnest money return if they missed the notification window.

Appraisal contingencies, when included, require the buyer to deliver notice of a low appraisal within a specified period after receiving the appraisal report. Many agents confuse the appraisal contingency deadline with the financing contingency deadline — they are distinct clocks running on different triggers.

| Contingency Type | Clock Starts | Typical Duration | Consequence of Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inspection | Binding agreement date | 10-14 calendar days | Contingency waived; buyer proceeds as-is |
| Financing denial notice | Loan denial received | 3-5 calendar days | Contingency deemed satisfied |
| Appraisal objection | Appraisal report received | 3-5 calendar days | Buyer accepts appraised value |
| Sale of property | Seller receives competing offer | 48-72 hours (kick-out) | Buyer's contract terminated |
| Closing | Binding agreement date | As specified | Possible default and earnest money forfeiture |

The sale-of-property contingency (Form 2) introduces a "kick-out" clause that gives the buyer a short window — typically 48 to 72 hours — to waive the contingency or release the contract after the seller receives another offer. This is the fastest-moving deadline in any Arkansas transaction and the one most frequently botched.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Contingency Deadlines

**First: miscounting the start date.** Agents frequently start counting from the date the buyer signed rather than the binding agreement date. If the seller signed two days later, every deadline is two days later than the buyer's agent assumed. This single error accounts for a disproportionate share of AREC complaints.

Second: failing to deliver notice in the form required. Form 1 specifies that notices must be written and delivered according to the contract's notice provisions. A text message to the other agent does not constitute valid notice under most Arkansas contracts unless the parties specifically agreed to electronic delivery in the contract. Agents who rely on informal communication find themselves unable to prove timely performance when disputes arise.

Third: assuming a deadline extension is automatic. When a buyer needs more time for inspections or financing, both parties must execute Form 6 (Amendment to Contract) before the original deadline expires. An oral agreement to extend is unenforceable under the Arkansas Statute of Frauds (Ark. Code Ann. Section 4-59-101), which requires real estate contract modifications to be in writing. Agents who tell their clients "the other side agreed to give us more time" without getting signatures on Form 6 are creating liability.

Fourth: ignoring holiday and weekend computation rules. The ARA Sales Contract contains a time computation clause specifying that if a deadline falls on a weekend or Arkansas state holiday, performance is due the next business day. Agents who forget this rule either panic unnecessarily or, worse, miss the actual adjusted deadline because they assumed they had the weekend.

Fifth: failing to calendar the kick-out deadline on sale-of-property contingencies. This 48-to-72-hour window requires immediate buyer action. Agents who learn about it from a Monday morning email about a Friday competing offer have already burned through most of the response window. The failure to [track deadlines systematically](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) is the root cause of kick-out failures.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Designated brokers in Arkansas carry personal regulatory exposure for their agents' deadline failures. AREC Rule 10.10 requires brokers to maintain "adequate supervision" of all licensees operating under their license. A broker who cannot demonstrate a system for tracking contingency deadlines is exposed to disciplinary action even if no individual transaction results in a complaint.

**Brokers should audit three things quarterly.** First, verify that every active contract file contains a deadline summary sheet or equivalent tracking document showing all contingency dates, the binding agreement date, and confirmation of timely notice delivery. Second, review a random sample of closed files to confirm that Form 6 amendments were executed before — not after — original deadlines expired. Third, confirm that agents are using the current-year ARA forms rather than outdated versions with different deadline structures.

The brokerage's policy manual should specify who is responsible for deadline tracking on each transaction. In offices without a dedicated transaction coordinator, this responsibility falls on the agent by default. But [understanding what a transaction coordinator does](/blog/what-is-a-transaction-coordinator) makes clear why many top-producing Arkansas offices have shifted deadline management to a dedicated compliance role. The broker's duty is to ensure the system exists and functions regardless of who performs the work.

Training is not optional. AREC's continuing education requirements include mandatory courses on contract law and agency duties. Brokers who supplement these requirements with internal deadline-management training — documented in writing — create an evidentiary record that protects the brokerage in the event of a complaint. Quarterly file audits paired with training records demonstrate the "adequate supervision" AREC requires.

## Liability When Deadlines Are Missed

The consequences cascade depending on which party the agent represents. A buyer's agent who allows the inspection contingency to lapse has effectively locked their client into purchasing a property with known defects and no recourse. The buyer's only options at that point are to close or to breach the contract and lose earnest money — potentially facing a seller's suit for specific performance.

A listing agent who fails to notify their seller of a buyer's untimely contingency removal attempt may forfeit the seller's right to declare the buyer in default. In both scenarios, the damaged party's first call is to an attorney, and the second call is to AREC. Licensees found to have caused harm through deadline negligence face potential malpractice claims in addition to regulatory penalties.

**Errors and omissions insurance does not cover intentional acts or gross negligence.** An agent who simply forgot a deadline may be covered. An agent who knew the deadline, failed to calendar it, and had no system in place to track it may find their E&O carrier denying coverage on the basis that the failure was systemic rather than accidental. This distinction matters enormously when a buyer loses a $10,000 earnest money deposit due to agent negligence.

The financial exposure is real. Beyond AREC fines up to $1,000 per violation, civil liability for earnest money losses, repair costs the buyer would have negotiated, or the delta between contract price and appraised value can reach five figures easily. ## Building a Deadline-Proof System for Arkansas Transactions

The most reliable agents build their deadline tracking backward from closing. Start with the closing date, then map every contingency deadline as a countdown milestone. Each milestone gets two alerts: one at 72 hours out (action window) and one at 24 hours out (emergency flag). This two-tier system catches the deadlines that slip past the first alert.

Paper calendars fail because they do not travel with the agent and they do not send alerts. Generic CRM reminders fail because they are not calibrated to the specific day counts in each contract. Purpose-built transaction management tools that parse contract dates and auto-generate deadline calendars based on the binding agreement date eliminate the human error in day-counting.
