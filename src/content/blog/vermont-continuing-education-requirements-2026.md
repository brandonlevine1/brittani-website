## Understanding Vermont Continuing Education Requirements 2026

Every two years, Vermont real estate licensees face the same question: have I done enough to stay compliant? The Vermont continuing education requirements 2026 cycle brings familiar obligations alongside updated curriculum topics that reflect shifting market regulations and consumer protection priorities. Missing even one requirement can put your license — and your active deals — at risk.

The Vermont Real Estate Commission (VREC), operating under the Office of Professional Regulation (OPR) within the Vermont Secretary of State's Office, governs all continuing education mandates. The statutory authority flows from 26 V.S.A. Chapter 41, which establishes licensing standards for real estate brokers and salespersons. VREC sets both the total hour count and the specific mandatory curriculum topics that every licensee must complete before submitting a renewal application.

Vermont's renewal cycle runs on a two-year schedule, with the current cycle requiring completion by March 31, 2026 for most licensees. The exact expiration date appears on your individual license, so verify yours rather than relying on general guidance. Filing late is not the same as filing on time with a grace period — Vermont does not offer a formal grace period for CE completion.

## Hour Breakdown and Mandatory Topics

**Vermont mandates 24 total continuing education hours per renewal cycle, split between 8 mandatory hours and 16 elective hours.** The mandatory hours are prescribed by VREC and typically cover topics the commission deems most relevant to public protection. Elective hours can come from any VREC-approved course or provider.

The 8 mandatory hours for the 2026 renewal cycle include fair housing law updates, Vermont license law and commission rules, agency disclosure practices, and ethics. VREC publishes the exact mandatory curriculum annually, and the 2026 cycle reflects post-NAR settlement practice changes that affect buyer representation agreements statewide. Agents should confirm they are enrolling in the correct cycle's mandatory offering, as taking a previous cycle's mandatory course will not count.

The 16 elective hours offer more flexibility, covering subjects like commercial real estate, environmental regulations, technology in real estate practice, or property management. However, every elective course must be delivered by a VREC-approved education provider. Courses approved in other states do not automatically transfer — you must verify Vermont approval before counting those hours.

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

Failing to complete CE before your renewal deadline triggers automatic license lapse. Vermont does not place your license in a suspended or inactive holding status — it simply expires. Once expired, you are not authorized to practice real estate, earn commissions, or represent yourself as a licensed agent in any capacity.

**The consequences extend beyond the individual agent to the brokerage itself.** Under 26 V.S.A. Section 2296, a broker who permits an unlicensed person to engage in real estate activity faces disciplinary action from VREC, including fines and potential license revocation. Any transaction you touch while lapsed could be challenged by a buyer, seller, or opposing party, creating grounds for deal cancellation or civil liability claims.

Reinstatement after lapse requires completing all outstanding CE, paying a late renewal fee, and potentially appearing before the commission. If your license has been lapsed for more than two years, Vermont may require you to retake the licensing exam entirely. The financial and professional cost of letting CE slide far outweighs the investment of keeping up with 24 hours over a two-year window.

VREC also has the authority to audit CE records at any time — not just at renewal. The commission can request proof of completion, and agents must retain certificates of completion for at least four years. Failing to produce documentation upon request is treated as noncompliance, even if you actually attended the course.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make With Vermont CE

The most frequent errors are not dramatic — they are mundane oversights that create real problems. Here are five specific mistakes Vermont agents keep making cycle after cycle.

**First, agents enroll in courses from non-approved providers and assume the hours will count.** VREC maintains an approved provider list, and courses from out-of-state providers or national platforms may not carry Vermont approval. Always verify directly through the [Vermont Office of Professional Regulation](https://sos.vermont.gov/opr/real-estate/) before paying for a course.

Second, agents complete the correct total hours but miss one or more mandatory topic areas. Finishing 24 hours of elective-style courses does not satisfy the 8-hour mandatory requirement. The system treats incomplete mandatory hours the same as incomplete total hours — your renewal will be denied.

Third, agents lose or fail to download their certificates of completion. VREC requires that you retain proof of CE completion, and some online providers only make certificates available for a limited time after course completion. Download and store every certificate immediately in a dedicated file, both digital and physical.

Fourth, agents wait until the final weeks before the deadline to begin their coursework. Popular VREC-approved courses fill up, and technical issues with online platforms are more common under deadline pressure. Starting early in the cycle eliminates this risk entirely.

Fifth, agents confuse the Vermont renewal cycle with neighboring states' cycles if they hold licenses in multiple jurisdictions. Vermont's March 31 deadline and two-year cycle do not align with every state, and [meeting one state's requirements does not guarantee compliance elsewhere](/blog/wisconsin-continuing-education-requirements-2026). Track each state's obligations separately.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers carry a distinct legal burden under Vermont law. Section 2296 of Title 26 makes clear that the principal broker is responsible for supervising all licensees affiliated with the brokerage, and that includes verifying CE compliance.

**At minimum, brokers should audit every affiliated agent's CE status at least 90 days before the renewal deadline.** This gives agents enough time to complete outstanding hours without last-minute panic. Waiting until renewal day to check creates a scenario where a lapsed agent may have already participated in active transactions without a valid license.

Brokers should maintain an internal tracking spreadsheet or system that logs each agent's CE completion dates, mandatory topic checkboxes, and certificate file locations. This is not optional diligence — it is a direct defense against VREC enforcement action. If a complaint is filed against the brokerage, demonstrating a proactive audit process can be the difference between a warning and a fine.

| Broker Audit Task | Recommended Frequency | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Verify each agent's CE hours completed | Quarterly | Catches shortfalls early |
| Confirm mandatory topics are covered | 90 days before deadline | Prevents partial compliance |
| Collect and file CE certificates | Upon each course completion | Satisfies VREC documentation requests |
| Check approved provider status | Before agents enroll | Avoids wasted hours and fees |
| Review multi-state license holders | Annually | Prevents cross-state confusion |

Brokers who manage teams with [agents prone to missing deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) should consider setting up automated reminders tied to each individual's renewal date. Vermont does not send courtesy reminders with enough lead time to be useful, so the brokerage-level system becomes the real safety net.

## Forms and Filings Agents Should Know

Vermont's renewal process is handled through the OPR's online licensing portal. The primary form is the Real Estate License Renewal Application, submitted electronically through the Secretary of State's online services system. Agents must self-certify CE completion on this application — VREC does not pre-verify your hours before accepting the form.

The self-certification is where risk lives. By submitting the renewal application, you are attesting under penalty of disciplinary action that you have completed all 24 hours, including the 8 mandatory hours. If an audit later reveals that your self-certification was false, VREC can pursue sanctions beyond simple noncompliance — this becomes a matter of misrepresentation to the commission.

For agents who let their license lapse and seek reinstatement, the process involves the Reinstatement Application and payment of both the renewal fee and a late penalty fee. If the lapse extends beyond two years, you will be directed back to the examination process, which means retaking the Vermont-specific portion of the real estate exam. The current renewal fee for salespersons is $100, and brokers pay $150 — reinstatement fees add to those totals.

## How CE Connects to Transaction Compliance

Continuing education is not just a box-checking exercise — the topics covered in mandatory CE directly affect how you handle transactions. The 2026 mandatory curriculum's emphasis on buyer agency agreement practices reflects the nationwide shift in how agents document their representation relationships. Agents who skip or skim through these modules risk making [costly paperwork errors](/blog/why-agents-hate-paperwork) in their actual deals.

Vermont's agency disclosure requirements, covered in the mandatory curriculum, tie directly to the Agency Disclosure Form that agents must present to all parties. Getting this wrong does not just mean a VREC complaint — it can give a party grounds to void a contract. The CE requirement exists precisely because these rules change, and agents who practiced correctly two years ago may not be practicing correctly today.

**The connection between CE compliance and transaction risk is direct and measurable.** A lapsed license means every listing agreement, buyer agency agreement, and purchase contract you signed during the lapse period is potentially voidable. Opposing counsel in a disputed transaction will check your license status as a matter of course, and a gap in licensure is an easy target.
