# South Dakota Buyer Agency Agreement Requirements 2026: What Every Agent and Broker Must Get Right

The rules around **South Dakota buyer agency agreement requirements 2026** are no longer something agents can afford to treat casually. Following the NAR settlement implementation and the South Dakota Real Estate Commission's subsequent guidance updates, every buyer-side agent in the state faces a clear mandate: get the agreement signed before you start working, or risk your license and your paycheck. The days of handshake understandings and informal touring arrangements are over.

South Dakota's regulatory framework sits at the intersection of state statute, Commission rules, and industry form standards that changed significantly after August 2024. This article breaks down exactly what agents must do, what forms to use, where agents are getting tripped up, and what brokers need to enforce at the office level.

## How South Dakota Law Defines Buyer Agency

South Dakota codifies real estate agency relationships under [SDCL Chapter 36-21A](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/Codified_Laws/DisplayStatute.aspx?Type=Statute&Statute=36-21A), the state's Real Estate Licensing Act. Section 36-21A-17 specifically addresses agency disclosure obligations, requiring licensees to disclose their agency relationship in writing before performing any act that could be considered representation.

The South Dakota Real Estate Commission, housed under the Department of Labor and Regulation, enforces these statutes. Commission Administrative Rule 20:69:05 further mandates that agents maintain written agreements documenting the scope and terms of their buyer representation. A verbal agreement does not satisfy the statute.

**This means the written buyer agency agreement is not just a best practice in South Dakota — it is a legal prerequisite to acting as a buyer's agent.** Agents who show property, write offers, or provide pricing guidance without a signed agreement are operating outside the law.

## Required Forms and What Must Be in Them

The South Dakota Association of REALTORS (SDAR) publishes the standard forms that most brokerages across the state adopt. For buyer agency, agents should be working with two primary documents.

Form 851, the Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement, establishes the agent as the buyer's sole representative for a defined period. Form 852, the Non-Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement, allows the buyer to work with multiple agents simultaneously. Both forms were revised in late 2024 and again in early 2026 to reflect post-settlement transparency requirements around compensation.

Every buyer agency agreement used in South Dakota — whether SDAR forms or brokerage-proprietary versions — must include several non-negotiable elements. The following table outlines the required components:

| Required Element | Where It Appears on Form 851/852 | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Agent and buyer names | Header section | Identifies the parties to the contract |
| Definite expiration date | Section 2 (Term) | Open-ended agreements violate Commission guidance |
| Compensation amount or rate | Section 4 (Compensation) | Must be specific, not "to be determined later" |
| Services to be provided | Section 3 (Scope) | Defines what the agent will and will not do |
| Signature of buyer and agent | Signature block | Unsigned forms are unenforceable and non-compliant |

Agents working with proprietary brokerage forms need to verify that every element above is present. The Commission has flagged incomplete forms during audits, and missing a single field — especially the compensation disclosure — can trigger a formal complaint.

## South Dakota Buyer Agency Agreement Requirements 2026: Timing and Execution Rules

**The single most important compliance rule is timing.** Under current Commission interpretation and the MLS policies adopted by Realtor Association of the Sioux Empire and Black Hills Association of REALTORS MLSs, the buyer agency agreement must be executed before the agent conducts a property tour with the buyer.

This is not limited to private showings. Open house attendance where the agent accompanies the buyer, virtual tours conducted as part of a representation relationship, and any property access arranged by the agent all trigger the requirement. If you are acting in the capacity of a buyer's agent, the agreement must already be signed.

South Dakota does not currently mandate a specific "cooling off" period for buyer agency agreements, unlike some states that allow rescission within a set number of days. Once the buyer signs, the agreement is binding for its stated term. Agents should discuss the term length openly with buyers before signing and document that conversation in their files.

The practical reality is that most agents in South Dakota set initial terms of 90 to 180 days. Shorter terms can work well for buyers who want flexibility, and they reduce the risk of disputes if the relationship sours. Brokers who [track deadlines carefully](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) will want to calendar agreement expiration dates just like they do inspection and financing contingencies.

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

Non-compliance carries real consequences in South Dakota. The Real Estate Commission has enforcement authority under SDCL 36-21A-38, which allows for disciplinary action including formal reprimand, mandatory education, fines of up to $2,000 per violation, license suspension, and license revocation.

Beyond Commission action, an agent without a signed buyer agency agreement faces a practical problem: they may have no enforceable right to compensation. If a dispute arises over who procured the buyer, the absence of a written agreement leaves the agent exposed. South Dakota courts have historically looked to written contracts as the foundation for commission claims, and an oral arrangement is difficult to enforce under the state's statute of frauds principles.

**There is also the risk of deal cancellation.** If a listing agent or seller discovers mid-transaction that the buyer's agent never executed a proper agency agreement, it raises questions about the buyer's representation and can create title and liability concerns that slow or kill the deal. Brokers on the listing side increasingly audit the buyer agent's documentation before accepting offers.

Errors and omissions insurance carriers are paying attention too. A claim arising from a transaction where the agent lacked proper agency documentation is harder to defend, and some carriers have added specific policy language requiring compliance with post-settlement buyer agency rules as a condition of coverage.

## Five Common Mistakes South Dakota Agents Make

Knowing the rules is one thing. Applying them consistently across every transaction is where agents stumble. These are the five errors that show up most frequently in Commission complaints and brokerage audits.

**First, agents present the buyer agency agreement at the offer stage rather than before touring.** This is the most common violation. The agent shows properties for days or weeks, builds rapport, and only pulls out the form when it is time to write an offer. By that point, the agent has already provided substantive services without authorization. The Commission considers this a violation regardless of whether the buyer eventually signs.

Second, agents leave the compensation field vague. Writing "per MLS" or "standard commission" in the compensation section does not satisfy the requirement for a specific, disclosed amount. The form must state a dollar amount, a percentage, or a clear formula. Ambiguity here is the fastest way to generate a buyer complaint post-closing.

Third, agents fail to provide a copy of the signed agreement to the buyer. SDCL 36-21A-21 requires that all parties receive copies of documents they sign. Handing the buyer a pen and then filing the form without giving them their copy is a violation that compounds other issues if a dispute later arises.

Fourth, agents use outdated forms. SDAR revises its forms regularly, and the 2026 versions of Forms 851 and 852 contain updated language around compensation transparency that was not present in earlier editions. Using a 2023 or 2024 version of the form means the agreement may lack required disclosures, which can be flagged during an MLS compliance check or Commission audit.

Fifth, agents treat the buyer agency agreement as a formality rather than a conversation. Rushing through the form without explaining terms, exclusions, and the buyer's rights creates a dynamic where the buyer later claims they did not understand what they signed. This is a [recurring pattern in deals that break down](/blog/where-deals-break-down) and one that a five-minute explanation at signing can prevent entirely.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers in South Dakota carry supervisory liability under SDCL 36-21A-34. If an agent under your license fails to obtain proper buyer agency agreements, the Commission can hold you responsible. This is not theoretical — the Commission has sanctioned brokers for systemic failures in agent supervision.

A broker's compliance program should start with a transaction file checklist that flags the buyer agency agreement as a threshold document. No agreement, no file opened. [Checklists outperform memory](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) in every study on compliance, and real estate is no exception. The buyer agency agreement should be the first document uploaded or filed in every buyer-side transaction.

Brokers should also conduct quarterly audits of active and closed files specifically checking for three things: that the agreement was signed before the first showing date, that the compensation field is filled in with a specific amount, and that the buyer received a copy. These three checks catch the vast majority of compliance gaps.

| Audit Check | What to Look For | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Timing of execution | Signed date vs. first showing date in MLS | Agreement signed same day as or after offer submission |
| Compensation clarity | Section 4 of Form 851/852 | Blank field, "TBD," or "per MLS" language |
| Buyer copy provided | Confirmation in file or email trail | No record of delivery to buyer |
| Form version | Footer date on SDAR form | Pre-2025 form versions still in use |
| Expiration date | Section 2 term field | Blank or "indefinite" notation |

Brokers with teams face an additional layer of complexity. If a team leader signs the buyer agency agreement but a team member conducts the showings, the file must clearly reflect the supervisory relationship and the team member's authority to act. The Commission has questioned arrangements where the signing agent and the servicing agent are different people without proper documentation of the team structure.

Training is the other half of enforcement. Holding a 30-minute compliance session at the start of each quarter — focused specifically on agency agreement execution, not general CE content — gives brokers documentation that they took affirmative steps to educate their agents. This documentation matters if the Commission ever investigates a complaint against the brokerage.
