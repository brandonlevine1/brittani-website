## Maryland Continuing Education Requirements 2026: The Full Breakdown

If you hold an active real estate license in Maryland, the Maryland continuing education requirements 2026 renewal cycle is not something you can afford to treat as an afterthought. The Maryland Real Estate Commission (MREC), operating under the Maryland Department of Labor's Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing, enforces strict hour and topic mandates that trip up even experienced agents. Missing a single requirement does not just delay your renewal—it can kill active deals and expose your brokerage to regulatory liability.

Maryland's continuing education framework is governed by the Maryland Real Estate Brokers Act, codified in Title 17 of the Business Occupations and Professions Article, Annotated Code of Maryland. The implementing regulations live in the Code of Maryland Regulations (COMAR) Title 09, Subtitle 11. These are the documents that dictate what counts, what does not, and what happens when you fall short.

## Hour Requirements and Mandatory Topics

Every licensee in Maryland must complete 15 hours of approved continuing education during each two-year renewal period. That number has not changed for 2026, but the composition of those hours matters more than the total. MREC mandates specific topic minimums that you cannot satisfy with generic electives.

| Requirement | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Total CE per cycle | 15 | Must be completed before license expiration date |
| Fair Housing | 1.5 | Must include federal and Maryland fair housing law updates |
| Ethics | 3.0 | Can include NAR Code of Ethics if MREC-approved |
| Agency/Disclosure | 1.5 | Must cover Maryland-specific agency relationships |
| Legislative Updates | 1.5 | Covers changes to Maryland real estate statutes |
| Electives | 7.5 | Any MREC-approved topic |

The 3 hours of ethics content is a common point of confusion. Maryland does not automatically accept NAR's Code of Ethics course as satisfying this requirement unless the specific offering has been approved by MREC. Agents who assume their NAR ethics class counts without verifying MREC approval sometimes discover at renewal that they are 3 hours short.

## Key Deadlines and the Renewal Process

**Maryland real estate licenses expire on a staggered two-year cycle, and your specific expiration date is printed on your license.** Unlike states that use a single annual deadline for all licensees, Maryland staggers renewals by birth month. Your CE must be completed before your expiration date—not submitted, not in progress, but completed with a certificate on file.

Renewal applications are filed through the Maryland Department of Labor's online licensing portal. The relevant form is the [Real Estate License Renewal Application](https://www.dllr.state.md.us/license/mrec/), which requires you to attest under penalty of perjury that you have met all CE requirements. MREC conducts random audits, and false attestation carries its own penalties under Maryland law separate from CE non-compliance.

Course providers are required to electronically report completion to MREC, but delays happen. Keep your own certificates. If the system does not reflect your completed hours at renewal time, the burden is on you to produce proof.

## What Happens If You Fail to Comply

The consequences of non-compliance escalate quickly and affect more than just your ability to print a new license card. Under Business Occupations and Professions Article Section 17-322, MREC has broad authority to deny renewal, suspend, or revoke a license for failure to meet CE requirements.

**If your license lapses, you cannot legally represent clients, collect commissions, or hold yourself out as a licensee.** Any transaction you participate in during a lapse period is conducted without a valid license, which creates grounds for commission disgorgement, contract voidability, and civil liability. Maryland courts have held that unlicensed activity—even due to an inadvertent lapse—does not entitle an agent to compensation for services rendered during the gap.

Reinstatement within three years requires completing all outstanding CE hours, paying back fees, and submitting the Reinstatement Application to MREC. After three years, the license is considered permanently expired, and you must re-qualify as a new applicant—including retaking the state licensing exam. The fee structure breaks down like this:

| Scenario | Action Required | Approximate Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Renewal on time | Complete 15 CE hours + renewal fee | $100 renewal fee |
| Lapse (0-3 years) | Complete CE + reinstatement application | $150 reinstatement fee + CE costs |
| Lapse (3+ years) | Retake exam, new application | $300+ including exam, application, pre-license education |
| Practicing on lapsed license | Commission disgorgement + potential fine | Up to $5,000 fine per MREC disciplinary action |

Brokers face their own exposure. Under COMAR 09.11.02.04, a broker who allows an affiliated licensee to practice with a lapsed license can face disciplinary action against the broker's own license, including fines and suspension.

## Common Mistakes Maryland Agents Make

Five errors appear with predictable regularity in MREC disciplinary files and renewal rejection notices. Knowing them is the difference between a smooth renewal and a frantic scramble.

**First, agents confuse approval jurisdictions.** A course approved by another state's real estate commission or by NAR does not automatically satisfy Maryland's requirements. MREC maintains its own approved provider and course list. Taking a Virginia-approved course while living near the DC border does not count unless that same course also carries MREC approval. Always check the MREC approved education provider list before enrolling.

Second, agents complete their hours but in the wrong topic categories. Finishing 15 hours of electives and zero hours of fair housing does not satisfy the mandate. MREC's audit process checks topic categories individually, not just total hours. You can be 15 hours complete and still non-compliant.

Third, agents wait until the final weeks before expiration to begin their CE. Course providers occasionally lose their MREC approval mid-cycle, or technical issues prevent timely electronic reporting. Starting early creates a buffer against problems you cannot control. Agents who handle their CE in the final 30 days have no margin for error if something goes wrong with [tracking deadlines and compliance requirements](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines).

Fourth, agents fail to retain completion certificates. When MREC's electronic reporting system does not reflect a completed course, the agent must produce independent proof. Without a saved certificate or transcript, you are effectively starting over.

Fifth, agents holding licenses in multiple states assume reciprocity extends to CE. Maryland does not accept continuing education from other jurisdictions unless the specific course is MREC-approved. Dual-licensed agents near DC, Virginia, or Pennsylvania get caught by this annually. Understanding [how license portability works across states](/blog/washington-license-reciprocity-portability-2026) helps frame this issue, but Maryland's rules stand on their own.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Maryland places affirmative supervisory duties on brokers that go beyond simply reminding agents about deadlines. COMAR 09.11.02 requires associate brokers and brokers of record to maintain a system for verifying licensee compliance. Failing to do so is a standalone violation—separate from the individual agent's failure.

**Brokers should build a documented audit process that verifies CE completion at least 60 days before each affiliated agent's renewal date.** This is not optional best practice; it is a regulatory expectation. MREC's investigators have cited brokerages for inadequate supervision when agents were found practicing on lapsed licenses, even when the broker personally had no knowledge of the lapse.

The audit should check three things for each agent: total hours completed, topic category compliance, and MREC approval status of the course providers used. A spreadsheet tracking agent names, license expiration dates, and CE completion dates—with copies of certificates on file—satisfies the documentation standard MREC expects during a brokerage inspection.

Brokers who manage teams larger than a handful of agents may also want to review their errors and omissions coverage. Some E&O policies contain exclusions for claims arising from unlicensed activity. If a deal closes during an agent's lapsed period and later becomes disputed, the brokerage may find itself uninsured for that transaction. Catching CE lapses before they happen is not just compliance—it is risk management that protects the entire brokerage's financial position.

For brokerages already struggling to keep [transaction paperwork organized and compliant](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory), adding CE tracking to the pile can feel overwhelming. The solution is systematizing it the same way you systematize transaction files: with clear checklists, assigned responsibility, and automated reminders.

## Approved Providers and Course Selection Strategy

MREC publishes a list of approved continuing education providers on the Maryland Department of Labor website. Agents should verify approval status before paying for any course, as unapproved courses—regardless of quality—generate zero credit toward renewal.

Both classroom and online distance education courses are accepted, provided the provider holds current MREC approval. Distance education courses must include interactive elements and a proctored final assessment to qualify. Courses that are entirely self-paced with no assessment component do not meet Maryland's distance education standards under COMAR 09.11.01.10.

When selecting elective courses, prioritize topics that serve double duty—content that satisfies your CE obligation and also makes you more effective in practice. Maryland-specific contract law updates, environmental hazard disclosures, and settlement procedure changes tend to deliver more practical value than generic sales training courses.
