## Understanding West Virginia FIRPTA Withholding Rules for Foreign Sellers 2026

Agents working transactions involving foreign nationals in the Mountain State face a dual-layer withholding obligation that catches many licensees off guard. The **West Virginia firpta withholding rules for foreign sellers 2026** require compliance with both federal FIRPTA requirements under IRC Section 1445 and West Virginia's own nonresident withholding statute. Missing either one exposes the buyer—and potentially the agent—to direct financial liability.

FIRPTA (the Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act) mandates that buyers withhold 15% of the gross sale price when purchasing U.S. real property from a "foreign person" as defined by the IRS. West Virginia adds its own 2.5% nonresident withholding under W. Va. Code 11-21-71a, collected at the settlement table and remitted to the West Virginia State Tax Department on Form WV/NRW-2. The combined withholding can reach 17.5% of the sale price before the seller sees a dime.

This is not a topic agents can delegate entirely to the closing attorney and forget. The IRS holds the buyer liable if withholding fails, and the listing agent's failure to disclose the seller's foreign status can torpedo a deal weeks after closing.

## The Federal Framework: IRC Section 1445 and Forms You Must Know

**The 15% federal withholding applies to the gross amount realized—not the gain—making it a substantial hit on foreign sellers.** The buyer (or the buyer's agent acting in a withholding capacity) must remit the funds to the IRS using Form 8288 (U.S. Withholding Tax Return for Certain Dispositions by Foreign Persons) and Form 8288-A (Statement of Withholding on Dispositions by Foreign Persons). Both forms must be filed within 20 days of the closing date.

The seller receives a copy of Form 8288-A stamped by the IRS, which they then attach to their U.S. income tax return to claim credit for the withholding. If the foreign seller believes the actual tax liability is less than 15%, they can apply for a withholding certificate using IRS Form 8288-B before closing—but this application can take 90 days or longer to process.

Reduced rates apply in specific circumstances. For properties priced at $300,000 or less where the buyer signs an affidavit of intended residence, withholding drops to 0%. Between $300,001 and $1,000,000 with the same buyer residence affidavit, withholding reduces to 10%.

| Sale Price | Buyer Residence Affidavit | Withholding Rate |
|---|---|---|
| $300,000 or less | Yes | 0% |
| $300,001–$1,000,000 | Yes | 10% |
| Any amount | No | 15% |
| Any amount (no affidavit, non-residence) | No | 15% |

## West Virginia's Additional Nonresident Withholding: W. Va. Code 11-21-71a

West Virginia does not simply rely on federal FIRPTA compliance. The state imposes a separate 2.5% withholding on gains from the sale of real property by nonresidents, including foreign nationals. This requirement is codified in [W. Va. Code 11-21-71a](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/11-21-71a/) and administered by the West Virginia State Tax Department.

The closing agent—typically a West Virginia attorney handling settlement—must collect the 2.5% withholding and remit it using Form WV/NRW-2 (Statement of West Virginia Income Tax Withheld for Nonresident Individual or Organization). The form is due within 30 days of closing. If multiple sellers are involved, a separate WV/NRW-2 must be filed for each nonresident seller.

**Foreign sellers can apply for a reduced or waived state withholding by filing Form WV/NRW-4 (Nonresident Income Tax Agreement) with the Tax Department before closing.** Approval is not automatic. If the seller can demonstrate the transaction will result in no West Virginia taxable gain—due to a loss on the sale or an applicable exclusion—the department may issue a waiver letter.

## Liability Exposure: What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

The consequences of FIRPTA noncompliance are not abstract. The IRS can assess the full 15% withholding amount directly against the buyer if the required tax was not remitted. This means if a foreign seller walks away with the full sale price and no one withheld, the buyer owes 15% of the gross sale amount to the IRS—plus interest and potential penalties.

For agents, the liability picture is different but still serious. A listing agent who knows (or should have known) the seller is a foreign person and fails to disclose this to the buyer's side can face claims for negligent misrepresentation. The West Virginia Real Estate Commission considers such omissions a potential violation of W. Va. Code 30-40-19, which governs licensee duties of disclosure and fair dealing.

On the state withholding side, W. Va. Code 11-21-71a places the withholding obligation squarely on the "transferee" or their designated agent at closing. If the closing agent fails to withhold and remit the 2.5%, the State Tax Department can assess the full amount against the transferee (buyer), along with a penalty of up to 25% of the unpaid withholding and interest accruing from the closing date.

| Failure Type | Who Bears Liability | Potential Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Federal 15% not withheld | Buyer | Full 15% plus interest and IRS penalties |
| State 2.5% not withheld | Buyer/closing agent | Full 2.5% plus up to 25% penalty plus interest |
| Listing agent conceals foreign status | Agent/broker | Commission complaints, civil liability, deal rescission |
| Late filing of Form 8288 | Buyer/buyer's agent | IRS penalty per IRC 6651 |

Deals have been unwound over FIRPTA failures. A buyer discovering post-closing that they owe 15% of the purchase price to the IRS—when those funds should have been held from the seller's proceeds—will pursue every party who touched the transaction.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make in West Virginia FIRPTA Transactions

**First, agents frequently assume that "foreign" means only overseas residents.** Under FIRPTA, a "foreign person" includes U.S. permanent residents who have lost their green card status, certain dual-status aliens, and entities organized under foreign law. A seller who lives in Morgantown but holds citizenship in Canada and no green card is a foreign person for FIRPTA purposes.

Second, agents conflate the buyer's residence exemption with a blanket waiver. The reduced withholding rates (0% under $300,000, 10% between $300,001 and $1,000,000) only apply if the buyer personally signs an affidavit confirming intent to use the property as a primary residence for at least 50% of the time during each of the first two 12-month periods. An investor buyer purchasing a rental property gets no reduction regardless of price.

Third, listing agents fail to identify the issue early enough. The IRS withholding certificate process (Form 8288-B) takes 90 days or more. If a foreign seller wants to reduce the withholding below 15%, that application must be submitted months before closing—not during the contract period. Agents who wait until title work begins lose this option entirely.

Fourth, agents confuse FIRPTA withholding with West Virginia's nonresident withholding and assume only one applies. Both the federal 15% and the state 2.5% must be addressed independently. Each has separate forms, separate deadlines, and separate remittance procedures.

Fifth, buyer's agents forget to advise their clients about FIRPTA liability at the offer stage. The buyer—not the seller—bears the legal risk if withholding is missed. This should be disclosed in writing before an offer is submitted on a property with a foreign seller. ## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers supervising agents involved in foreign seller transactions should implement specific file review checkpoints. The first audit point is the listing agreement stage: does the file contain documentation of whether the seller is a U.S. person or a foreign person? A FIRPTA affidavit—either confirming domestic status or flagging foreign status—should appear in every listing file.

**The second audit point is the contract stage.** Brokers should verify that the purchase agreement addresses FIRPTA withholding explicitly—who is responsible, how funds will be held, and whether the seller intends to apply for a withholding certificate. The West Virginia Association of Realtors standard contract forms include FIRPTA language, but agents using custom contracts often omit it.

Third, brokers should confirm that the closing agent received written instructions regarding both federal and state withholding amounts. A simple email confirmation from the closing attorney acknowledging the withholding obligations—before the closing date—protects the brokerage from claims that the issue was never raised.

Fourth, brokers should maintain a policy requiring agents to document the buyer's affidavit of intended residence (if claiming a reduced rate) or confirm that full 15% withholding was applied. This documentation should be in the file at the time of compliance review, not reconstructed after a problem surfaces.

The West Virginia Real Estate Commission's licensing division has authority to investigate brokerages where repeated FIRPTA failures suggest inadequate supervision under W. Va. Code 30-40-20. A pattern of noncompliance across multiple transactions can result in license sanctions for the supervising broker.

## Practical Timeline for FIRPTA-Affected Closings in West Virginia

Foreign seller transactions require earlier preparation than domestic deals. The listing agent should confirm the seller's status during the listing intake—ideally with a written questionnaire. If the seller intends to apply for a reduced withholding certificate via Form 8288-B, that application should go to the IRS at least 90 days before the anticipated closing date.

At contract execution, the buyer's side should be informed in writing of the withholding obligation and the buyer's personal liability. The closing attorney should receive a FIRPTA memo from the listing or buyer's agent identifying the seller as a foreign person and specifying whether a withholding certificate application is pending.

At settlement, the closing attorney deducts both the federal withholding (15%, 10%, or 0% depending on the applicable exception) and the West Virginia 2.5% from the seller's proceeds. The attorney files Form 8288 and Form 8288-A with the IRS within 20 days of closing, and Form WV/NRW-2 with the State Tax Department within 30 days.
