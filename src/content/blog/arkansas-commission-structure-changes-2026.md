## Arkansas Commission Structure Rules After the NAR Settlement 2026: The Ground Has Shifted

The phrase "Arkansas commission structure rules after the NAR settlement 2026" is no longer a hypothetical compliance scenario — it is the daily operating reality for every licensee in the state. Since the practice changes took effect in August 2024, nearly two full years of enforcement data now exist, and the Arkansas Real Estate Commission (AREC) has issued multiple interpretive guidance bulletins clarifying how these federal settlement terms interact with Ark. Code Ann. Section 17-42-101 et seq. Agents who assumed this was a national problem handled at the association level have learned, sometimes the hard way, that state-level enforcement carries its own teeth.

This post breaks down the specific forms, statutes, liability risks, and audit practices that Arkansas agents and brokers must internalize heading into the second half of 2026.

## How the NAR Settlement Changed Commission Mechanics in Arkansas

The 2024 settlement between the National Association of Realtors and plaintiff classes in Sitzer/Burnett and Moehrl altered commission practices nationwide. The two structural changes that matter most in Arkansas are the prohibition on publishing offers of buyer-agent compensation in any MLS field and the mandatory written buyer-broker agreement before any property showing.

Arkansas MLS systems — primarily CARMLS (Cooperative Arkansas REALTORS MLS) — updated their data input fields to strip compensation offer columns in Q3 2024. Any agent attempting to enter compensation data in a listing now receives a system rejection. The workaround some agents tried, burying commission language in the "Agent Remarks" section, has already triggered disciplinary complaints forwarded to AREC.

**AREC's Rule 10.14 already required written agency agreements, but the post-settlement interpretation now demands that buyer-agent compensation be stated as a specific dollar amount or percentage — not an open-ended "negotiable" placeholder.** This specificity requirement aligns with the [NAR settlement practice changes](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs) and goes further than what some neighboring states enforce.

## Key Arkansas Forms and Where They Apply

Arkansas real estate transactions rely on forms published by the Arkansas REALTORS Association (ARA) and approved for use by AREC. The settlement-era changes have modified or added requirements to several form numbers that agents handle weekly.

| Form | Name | Settlement-Related Change |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| ARA Form 1 | Exclusive Buyer-Broker Agreement | Must specify exact compensation amount or rate; signed before first showing |
| ARA Form 2 | Exclusive Right to Sell Listing Agreement | Compensation field now addresses seller's decision to offer or not offer buyer-agent payment outside MLS |
| ARA Form 7 | Disclosure of Agency Relationships | Must be paired with compensation disclosure language per AREC Rule 10.9 |
| AREC-approved Addendum C | Seller Concession / Buyer-Agent Payment Authorization | New addendum documenting seller's voluntary contribution to buyer-agent fee |

ARA Form 1 is the linchpin document. If your file does not contain a fully executed Form 1 dated before the first showing, your brokerage's errors and omissions policy may not cover a resulting commission dispute. Multiple E&O carriers operating in Arkansas have issued bulletins stating that failure to secure pre-tour written agreements constitutes a material deviation from standard practice.

The [compliance deadlines that trip up agents](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) in other states apply here with equal force — the difference is that Arkansas requires retention of these forms for five years under AREC Rule 10.5.

## What Happens When You Fail to Comply

Noncompliance carries three distinct categories of risk in Arkansas: regulatory discipline, civil liability, and deal-level consequences.

**AREC can impose fines up to $2,500 per violation under Ark. Code Ann. Section 17-42-311, and repeated violations can result in license suspension or revocation.** The Commission's investigative division received 47 settlement-related complaints between January and June 2026, according to the most recent AREC public meeting minutes — a significant jump from the 12 complaints logged in the same period of 2025.

Civil liability exposes agents to breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims. If a buyer can demonstrate that their agent failed to disclose compensation mechanics or collected a commission that exceeded the agreed-upon written amount, the buyer may pursue disgorgement of the excess plus attorney fees. Arkansas courts apply a "reasonable reliance" standard, meaning the written agreement is the controlling document.

At the deal level, a missing or defective buyer-broker agreement gives the other side ammunition to demand renegotiation. Listing agents have increasingly instructed sellers to request proof of a valid buyer-broker agreement before accepting an offer — a practice AREC has explicitly stated is permissible under current rules.

## Common Mistakes Arkansas Agents Are Making Right Now

Two years into the new regime, patterns have emerged. Here are the five most frequent errors AREC investigators and managing brokers report encountering.

**First, agents are using vague compensation language in buyer-broker agreements.** Writing "up to 3%" or "to be determined at closing" does not satisfy the specificity requirement. The agreement must state a precise figure or rate that the buyer can understand at the moment of signing.

Second, listing agents are verbally communicating buyer-agent compensation offers during broker open houses without documenting the offer in writing. AREC has stated that verbal offers do not violate MLS rules but create a "he said/she said" liability gap. Best practice is to use Addendum C or a written communication that both sides retain.

Third, agents are failing to update the buyer-broker agreement when the compensation source changes mid-transaction. If a seller initially declines to contribute and later agrees, the original Form 1 must be amended or a supplemental agreement executed. An unmatched file is an audit finding waiting to happen.

Fourth, dual-agency transactions are generating incomplete disclosures. Arkansas allows disclosed dual agency under Ark. Code Ann. Section 17-42-602, but the post-settlement environment demands that compensation to both sides be independently documented even when the same brokerage represents both parties.

Fifth, agents are neglecting to provide the buyer with a copy of the signed agreement before the showing begins. AREC Rule 10.14(c) requires delivery, not merely execution. A signed form sitting in the agent's car does not count as delivered, and [this is exactly where deals break down](/blog/where-deals-break-down) when disputes arise weeks later.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Managing brokers bear direct supervisory liability under Ark. Code Ann. Section 17-42-309. AREC does not accept "I didn't know my agent was doing that" as a defense. The Commission has publicly stated that brokers will face parallel discipline if their offices lack systems to catch noncompliance.

| Audit Point | Frequency | Risk if Missed |
|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| Signed buyer-broker agreement dated before first showing | Every transaction | License discipline, E&O denial |
| Compensation amount matches closing statement disbursement | At closing | Fiduciary breach claim, AREC fine |
| MLS listing fields contain zero compensation language | At listing input | MLS fine, AREC complaint |
| Dual-agency files contain independent compensation disclosures for each party | Every dual-agency file | Automatic AREC investigation trigger |
| Five-year document retention compliance | Annual audit | Up to $2,500 fine per missing file |

Brokers should implement a transaction-file review at three checkpoints: agreement execution, offer acceptance, and closing. Waiting until closing to review for compliance gaps means the horse has left the barn. Many Arkansas brokerages have moved to weekly file audits for active transactions, a rhythm similar to what a [weekly pipeline review system](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) would catch.

The supervisory obligation extends to independent contractor agents. The IC relationship does not insulate a broker from AREC discipline when the violation involves a form or disclosure that the broker's office procedures should have caught.

## How Compensation Negotiation Actually Works Now

The mechanical reality of getting a buyer's agent paid in 2026 Arkansas involves more steps than it did in 2023. Sellers may still choose to offer compensation to buyer's agents, but that offer lives outside the MLS — in marketing materials, on brokerage websites, or in direct agent-to-agent communication.

**Buyer's agents must now treat their compensation as a negotiation point in the offer itself, or secure it entirely from the buyer per the written agreement.** This means the purchase contract often includes a line item requesting that the seller pay a portion or all of the buyer-agent fee as a concession. ARA's standard purchase contract (Form 5) was updated in early 2025 to include a specific blank for this request, separate from the general seller concessions field.

If the seller declines to pay, the buyer remains contractually obligated under the buyer-broker agreement. Agents who fail to explain this possibility before signing the agreement face the highest concentration of post-closing complaints AREC currently tracks.
