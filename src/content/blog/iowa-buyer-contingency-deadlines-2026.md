## Why Iowa Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026 Demand Your Attention Now

Missing a single contingency deadline in an Iowa residential transaction can unravel a deal, trigger an ethics complaint, or expose your brokerage to a professional liability claim. Iowa buyer contingency deadlines 2026 carry the same statutory weight they always have under Iowa Code Chapter 543B, but form revisions from the Iowa Association of Realtors (IAR) and tighter broker oversight expectations from the Iowa Real Estate Commission (IREC) make this a topic worth revisiting with fresh eyes.

**Iowa does not have a single statute that prescribes a universal contingency timeline.** Instead, the framework lives inside the contract forms parties execute—primarily IAR Form 101 (Residential Purchase Agreement) and its associated addenda. That means the deadlines you write into the blanks are the deadlines the Iowa courts will enforce if a dispute reaches litigation or arbitration.

This post breaks down every contingency deadline agents must track, the specific forms involved, where agents keep getting burned, and what managing brokers should be auditing in their files right now.

## The Iowa Forms That Control Contingency Timelines

The IAR publishes standardized forms that the overwhelming majority of agents across the state's 99 counties use. IREC does not mandate any particular form, but the [Iowa Real Estate Commission's administrative rules under 193E](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/law/administrativeRules/rules?agency=193E) require that contracts contain certain disclosures and be prepared competently.

The primary documents where contingency deadlines appear include IAR Form 101 (Residential Purchase Agreement), IAR Form 103 (Financing Contingency Addendum), IAR Form 106 (Inspection Contingency Addendum), and IAR Form 107 (Sale of Buyer's Property Contingency Addendum). Each form has blank fields where the parties negotiate the number of days allowed for a contingency to be satisfied or waived.

One point that trips up newer agents: these forms use calendar days unless specifically noted. The time-computation clause in Form 101 states that if the last day of any period falls on a weekend or recognized legal holiday, performance extends to the end of the next business day. Agents who confuse this with business-day counting create unnecessary conflicts during appraisal and inspection windows.

## Breaking Down Each Major Contingency Deadline

Understanding what each deadline governs—and what triggers its start—is the difference between smooth closings and blown transactions.

| Contingency | Typical Form | Clock Starts | Common Duration | Consequence of Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home Inspection | IAR Form 106 | Date of acceptance | 10-15 calendar days | Buyer deemed to accept property condition |
| Financing/Appraisal | IAR Form 103 | Date of acceptance | 20-30 calendar days | Contingency waived; earnest money at risk |
| Sale of Buyer's Property | IAR Form 107 | Date of acceptance | Negotiated (often 30-60 days) | Seller may accept backup offer via kick-out clause |
| Title Review | IAR Form 101, Section 9 | Delivery of abstract or title commitment | 10-20 calendar days | Buyer accepts title as-is |
| Lead Paint Inspection (pre-1978) | Federal disclosure form | Delivery of disclosure | 10 calendar days (federal default) | Buyer waives right to independent inspection |

**The date of acceptance is the anchor point for nearly every contingency clock.** Agents must document this date precisely—down to the time of day when final signatures are executed—because a one-day miscalculation can cost your client their right to cancel.

The financing contingency under Form 103 deserves special attention in 2026 given fluctuating interest rates. The form typically requires the buyer to apply for financing within a stated number of days and to notify the seller if financing cannot be obtained by the deadline. Failure to provide timely written notice means the buyer may forfeit the contingency protection and be obligated to close without it.

## What Happens When Agents Miss or Miscalculate Deadlines

The consequences of deadline mismanagement in Iowa fall into three buckets: transactional, regulatory, and civil.

On the transactional side, a missed inspection deadline under Form 106 means the buyer loses the ability to request repairs or terminate based on property condition. The seller's agent can—and often will—argue the contingency has been satisfied by operation of the contract terms. Iowa courts have consistently upheld time-is-of-the-essence clauses in purchase agreements.

From a regulatory perspective, IREC can investigate complaints filed under Iowa Code Section 543B.29, which outlines grounds for disciplinary action including incompetence, negligence, and failure to exercise reasonable skill. Sanctions range from a formal citation and continuing education mandate to license suspension. In 2024, IREC issued 47 disciplinary actions against licensees, several involving failure to advise clients of contractual obligations.

Civil liability opens a third front. If a buyer loses their earnest money deposit—or worse, is forced into a purchase they cannot finance—because their agent miscalculated a deadline, the agent and brokerage face a potential negligence or breach-of-fiduciary-duty claim. Iowa's statute of limitations for such claims is five years under Iowa Code Section 614.1(4), meaning a sloppy 2026 transaction can haunt you into 2031.

## Five Common Mistakes Iowa Agents Make With Contingency Deadlines

After reviewing hundreds of Iowa transaction files over the years, certain errors surface repeatedly. Here are five that cost agents money, reputation, or both.

**First, agents leave contingency duration blanks empty on IAR forms.** When no number of days is filled in, the contract becomes ambiguous. Iowa courts may interpret the omission against the drafting party, or the deal may simply fall apart when neither side can agree on when the window closes. Always fill in every blank or mark it "N/A" if inapplicable.

Second, agents calculate deadlines from the wrong start date. The acceptance date is when the last party signs, not when the offer was submitted. If an offer is submitted on Monday but the seller counters and the buyer accepts the counter on Wednesday, the clock starts Wednesday. This seemingly obvious point generates a disproportionate number of disputes.

Third, agents fail to deliver written notice of contingency satisfaction or waiver in the form the contract requires. Under IAR Form 106, the buyer must provide written notice to the seller if they elect to terminate based on inspection findings. A phone call or text message does not satisfy the contractual notice requirement. Agents who rely on informal communication leave their clients exposed.

Fourth, agents confuse the inspection objection deadline with the inspection period itself. Form 106 often contains two deadlines: one for completing the inspection and a separate (sometimes shorter) period for delivering the buyer's written response. Missing the response deadline—even if the inspection was completed on time—can result in forfeiture of the contingency.

Fifth, agents neglect to calendar the "kick-out" notification period under the sale-of-buyer's-property contingency (Form 107). When a seller receives a backup offer, the original buyer typically has 72 hours to waive the contingency or terminate. Agents who do not monitor for kick-out notices on behalf of their buyer clients risk losing the deal entirely. ## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce in Every File

Managing brokers in Iowa carry supervisory responsibility under Iowa Code Section 543B.34A. IREC expects brokers to maintain policies and systems that ensure agent compliance with contractual deadlines.

| Audit Item | What Broker Should Verify | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance date documentation | Signed acceptance page with date/time stamp | Every file at intake |
| Contingency blanks completed | All duration fields filled in on Forms 101, 103, 106, 107 | Every file at intake |
| Deadline tracking log | Written or digital record showing each deadline and status | Weekly review |
| Written notices on file | Copies of all contingency-related notices with delivery confirmation | Before each deadline passes |
| Earnest money compliance | Deposit received within timeframe stated in contract (Iowa Code 543B.46) | Within 3 business days of acceptance |

**Brokers who rely solely on their agents' verbal assurances that deadlines are met are accepting unnecessary risk.** A documented audit trail is the only defensible position if IREC investigates or if a client files a civil claim.

The Iowa Real Estate Commission's [2025 broker supervision guidance](https://irec.iowa.gov) reinforced that brokers must have written office policies addressing file review procedures. The guidance stops short of mandating specific technology, but it is clear that a paper calendar taped to a wall no longer meets the standard of care in a market where digital transaction management platforms exist.

For brokers managing multiple agents across locations, a systematic approach to [weekly pipeline reviews](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) can surface deadline risks before they become liabilities.
