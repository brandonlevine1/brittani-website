## Understanding West Virginia Continuing Education Requirements 2026

The West Virginia continuing education requirements 2026 cycle catches more agents off guard than any other compliance obligation in the state. Every year, the West Virginia Real Estate Commission (WVREC) processes hundreds of lapsed licenses from agents who either forgot their deadline or miscounted their hours. The penalty structure is unforgiving, and the downstream effects on active transactions can devastate a brokerage's pipeline.

West Virginia operates under W. Va. Code Chapter 30, Article 40 — the Real Estate License Act — which grants the WVREC authority to set continuing education standards, approve course providers, and discipline licensees who fail to comply. Unlike some neighboring states that operate on two-year cycles, West Virginia requires annual CE completion, which means the margin for procrastination is razor-thin. Agents renewing in 2026 need to understand exactly what is required, when it is due, and what breaks if they miss it.

## Hour Breakdown and Mandatory Topics

**West Virginia mandates 7 hours of continuing education annually for both salespersons and brokers.** This is not a suggestion or a best practice — it is a hard statutory requirement under W. Va. Code 30-40-19. Of those 7 hours, at least 3 must cover mandatory topics selected by the Commission each year.

For the 2026 renewal cycle, the WVREC has historically required that mandatory hours address license law updates, agency disclosure obligations, and fair housing compliance. The remaining 4 hours are elective and can cover topics like contract writing, risk management, property management, or technology applications in real estate.

Brokers holding a broker's license must also complete the same 7 hours but should note that broker-specific management courses count toward both their personal CE and any supervisory training expectations. There is no separate "broker CE" category in West Virginia — the distinction lies in course content selection, not hour totals.

| Requirement | Salesperson | Broker |
|---|---|---|
| Total Annual Hours | 7 | 7 |
| Mandatory Topic Hours | 3 | 3 |
| Elective Hours | 4 | 4 |
| Renewal Cycle | Annual | Annual |
| License Expiration | December 31 | December 31 |

## Deadlines and the Renewal Timeline

All West Virginia real estate licenses expire on December 31 of each year. The renewal application — submitted through the WVREC's online portal — must be filed along with proof of CE completion and the renewal fee before that date. The Commission does not grant automatic extensions.

Agents who complete their CE early in the year still cannot apply it to the following year's requirement. Each calendar year stands alone. If you completed all 7 hours by March 2026, those hours satisfy your 2026 obligation but do nothing for 2027.

The WVREC sends renewal notices to licensees, but the responsibility falls entirely on the individual agent. Missing the December 31 deadline means your license lapses on January 1, and any transaction you touch after that date is legally questionable. This makes the [timeline management issues agents face](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) even more consequential in Q4.

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

**Practicing real estate on a lapsed license in West Virginia is a violation of W. Va. Code 30-40-20, which authorizes fines up to $5,000 per violation.** The WVREC can also pursue license revocation, suspension, or probationary conditions depending on the severity and duration of non-compliance.

Beyond fines, there are transaction-level consequences. A contract negotiated or signed by an agent whose license has lapsed may be voidable. Buyers or sellers who discover their agent was unlicensed during the transaction period can seek rescission of the contract, and opposing parties in litigation will absolutely exploit this fact. The brokerage's errors and omissions insurance may also deny coverage for acts performed by an unlicensed individual.

The Commission maintains a public license verification database. Opposing agents, title companies, and attorneys routinely check license status before closing. If your status shows "inactive" or "lapsed," expect the deal to stall — or worse, unravel entirely. ## Common Mistakes Agents Make With CE Compliance

Mistake number one: taking courses from unapproved providers. The WVREC maintains a specific list of approved continuing education providers, and only courses delivered by those entities count toward your 7-hour requirement. An agent who completes a NAR-affiliated webinar or an out-of-state online course that is not WVREC-approved will receive zero credit, regardless of the content quality.

Mistake number two: confusing post-licensing education with continuing education. New licensees in West Virginia must complete 90 hours of post-licensing education within 2 years of initial licensure under W. Va. Code 30-40-7. Those hours are separate from and in addition to annual CE. Agents in their first or second year sometimes believe their post-licensing work covers CE — it does not.

Mistake number three: failing to retain completion certificates. The WVREC can audit any licensee's CE records, and the burden of proof rests on the agent. If you cannot produce a certificate of completion from an approved provider, the Commission treats those hours as incomplete. Digital records are acceptable, but they must include the course title, provider name, date completed, and hours awarded.

**Mistake number four: waiting until December to complete all 7 hours.** Course availability tightens in Q4, online systems experience higher traffic and technical issues, and any dispute over credit takes weeks to resolve. Agents who procrastinate until the final week of December frequently discover they cannot get issues resolved before the deadline.

Mistake number five: assuming your broker will track this for you. While brokers have supervisory obligations, the license belongs to the individual agent. The WVREC holds each licensee personally responsible for their own compliance, regardless of what the brokerage's internal systems show.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Brokers in West Virginia carry a statutory supervisory duty under W. Va. Code 30-40-12. While the law does not explicitly require brokers to verify each agent's CE completion, the practical and legal exposure makes it foolish not to. A brokerage that allows an unlicensed agent to transact business faces its own disciplinary action, including potential suspension of the brokerage license.

**Every managing broker should implement a quarterly audit of agent CE progress.** At minimum, this means requesting proof of completion from each agent by October 1 and flagging anyone who has not started their required hours. The WVREC's online license lookup tool allows brokers to verify active status in real time, but it only reflects what has already been reported — it does not show courses in progress.

| Audit Action | Recommended Frequency | Responsible Party |
|---|---|---|
| Verify active license status | Quarterly | Broker/Office Manager |
| Request CE completion certificates | By October 1 | Each Agent |
| Check approved provider list | Before recommending courses | Broker |
| Document supervisory actions | Ongoing | Broker |
| Flag agents with zero hours by Q3 | July review | Broker/Office Manager |

Brokers should also maintain a written policy — ideally in the independent contractor agreement — that specifies CE completion as a condition of affiliation. This creates a contractual basis for terminating agents who refuse to comply, which protects the brokerage from regulatory spillover. ## Approved Providers and Course Formats

The WVREC publishes an updated list of approved CE providers on its website at [wvrec.wv.gov](https://wvrec.wv.gov). Only courses delivered by providers on this list generate valid credit. The Commission accepts both live classroom instruction and distance education, provided the distance education format includes an end-of-course assessment.

Popular approved providers include the West Virginia Association of Realtors Education Foundation, several community colleges within the state system, and a handful of national online platforms that have obtained WVREC-specific approval. Agents should verify approval status before enrolling — not after completing a course and expecting retroactive credit.

Course completion data is reported to the WVREC by approved providers, but reporting timelines vary. Some providers report within 48 hours; others take up to 30 days. Agents who complete courses in late December should request immediate reporting and retain their own certificates as backup documentation for their renewal application.

## Forms and Filings Agents Should Know

The primary renewal form is the WVREC License Renewal Application, submitted electronically through the Commission's online licensing portal. Agents must attest that they have completed all required CE hours and pay the applicable renewal fee — currently $100 for salespersons and $125 for brokers.

If an agent's license lapses, reinstatement requires submission of the WVREC Reinstatement Application along with proof of completed CE for the lapsed period, any outstanding renewal fees, and a late penalty fee. The reinstatement process can take 2-4 weeks, during which the agent cannot practice.

Agents who change their name, address, or brokerage affiliation during the year must file the appropriate Change of Information Form with the WVREC within 10 days of the change. Failure to update this information does not excuse CE non-compliance — the Commission sends notices to the address on file, and returned mail is the agent's problem, not the Commission's.
