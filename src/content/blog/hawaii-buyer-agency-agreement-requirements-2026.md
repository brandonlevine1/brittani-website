## Understanding Hawaii Buyer Agency Agreement Requirements 2026

The Hawaii buyer agency agreement requirements 2026 represent a significant shift in how agents on the islands establish and document their relationships with buyer clients. Following the nationwide NAR settlement changes that took effect in August 2024, the Hawaii Real Estate Commission (HREC) has issued updated guidance that adds state-specific layers on top of federal practice standards. Agents who treat this as a mere formality are exposing themselves and their brokerages to serious regulatory and financial risk.

Hawaii operates under HRS Chapter 467, which governs real estate licensees and grants HREC enforcement authority over agent conduct. The commission's administrative rules under Hawaii Administrative Rules Title 16, Chapter 99 spell out the fiduciary duties and disclosure obligations that attach once a buyer agency relationship is formed. Every agent working with buyers in 2026 must understand both the federal mandate and these state-specific requirements.

## The Legal Foundation: HRS Chapter 467 and HREC Rules

**Hawaii's real estate licensing statute requires that all agency relationships be established in writing before an agent provides substantive services to a buyer.** This is not new in concept—Hawaii has long required agency disclosure—but the 2026 enforcement posture treats the written buyer agreement as the triggering document for fiduciary duty, not merely an administrative checkbox.

Under HRS Section 467-14, the HREC can discipline licensees for acting as agents without proper authorization. The statute lists specific violations including "failure to maintain a written agreement for representation" and "failure to disclose agency relationships." These violations carry administrative penalties ranging from formal reprimand to license revocation.

HREC's administrative rules at HAR Section 16-99-3 define buyer agency and require that the agreement contain specific provisions including compensation terms, duration, scope of services, and the buyer's right to terminate. The rules were amended in late 2025 to align with post-settlement practices, requiring that compensation disclosures within the agreement be explicit and not reference MLS cooperative compensation fields.

## Required Form Elements and Approved Documents

The Hawaii Association of REALTORS (HAR) publishes the standard Buyer Representation Agreement, designated as Form BRA-1. This form was substantially revised in late 2024 and updated again for 2026 to incorporate HREC's amended rule language. Agents using outdated versions of this form are non-compliant even if the substance appears similar.

Form BRA-1 must include all of the following elements to satisfy HREC requirements:

| Required Element | Specific Requirement | Form BRA-1 Section |
|---|---|---|
| Definite expiration date | Calendar date, no auto-renewal without new signature | Section 2 |
| Compensation disclosure | Dollar amount or percentage, source identified | Section 5 |
| Scope of representation | Geographic area or property types specified | Section 3 |
| Termination rights | Conditions and process for both parties | Section 8 |
| Dual agency consent | Pre-authorization or requirement for separate consent | Section 6 |

Brokerages that use proprietary forms rather than Form BRA-1 must ensure their documents contain every element listed in HAR Section 16-99-3(b). The principal broker bears responsibility for approving any non-standard form, and HREC auditors will compare custom forms against the regulatory checklist during routine brokerage examinations.

Additionally, agents must provide the HREC-mandated Agency Disclosure form (Form AD-1) at first substantive contact. This disclosure works in tandem with the buyer agreement—it informs the buyer of possible agency relationships, while the BRA-1 formalizes the chosen relationship.

## Consequences of Non-Compliance

**Failing to secure a proper buyer agency agreement before providing services exposes agents to a cascade of consequences that extend well beyond a regulatory slap.** The HREC has signaled through its 2025-2026 enforcement bulletins that buyer agreement compliance is a priority audit item.

Administrative penalties under HRS Section 467-14 include fines of up to $2,500 per violation. Repeat offenders face license suspension or revocation. The commission can also require remedial education at the agent's expense, adding both cost and time burden.

Civil liability presents an even greater risk. Without a signed agreement, an agent's claim to compensation becomes legally tenuous. Hawaii courts have consistently held that absent a written agreement, a buyer's agent has no enforceable right to commission from the buyer. In a 2024 case before the Hawaii Intermediate Court of Appeals, a licensee's commission claim was denied entirely because the representation agreement lacked a definite expiration date—a technical deficiency that voided the contract.

Transaction cancellations also become more likely when compliance failures surface during escrow. Title companies and escrow officers in Hawaii increasingly verify that buyer representation documentation is in the file before closing. A missing or deficient agreement discovered at this stage can delay closing, trigger renegotiation of commission terms, or cause buyers to seek release from the transaction entirely. Understanding [where deals commonly break down](/blog/where-deals-break-down) helps agents recognize how documentation gaps create cascading problems.

## Common Mistakes Hawaii Agents Make

Five specific errors appear repeatedly in HREC disciplinary actions and brokerage compliance audits:

**First, agents present the buyer agreement after showing property rather than before.** The post-settlement standard requires signed agreements before touring. Showing a property and then asking for a signature creates an unenforceable agreement in many circumstances because consideration has already been provided. HREC's position is clear: the agreement must precede substantive services.

Second, agents fail to specify compensation in a concrete dollar amount or percentage. Vague language like "customary commission" or "as offered through MLS" does not satisfy the HREC requirement under HAR Section 16-99-3(b)(4). The 2026 rules demand that the buyer be able to determine their maximum financial obligation from reading the agreement alone.

Third, agents use agreements without definite expiration dates or include auto-renewal clauses that effectively create indefinite agreements. HREC considers an auto-renewal clause without affirmative buyer re-consent to be a violation. Every agreement must terminate on a specific calendar date.

Fourth, agents neglect to provide the Agency Disclosure form (AD-1) concurrently with or prior to the buyer agreement. These are separate obligations—one does not satisfy the other. An agent can have a signed BRA-1 and still face discipline for failing to provide timely agency disclosure.

Fifth, agents operating on neighbor islands sometimes execute agreements referencing Oahu-centric brokerage addresses or supervision structures without updating principal broker information for the island where services are actually rendered. Hawaii's multi-island geography creates unique compliance traps. Each office location must be properly registered with HREC, and the supervising principal broker must be correctly identified on all client-facing documents.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Principal brokers in Hawaii carry personal liability for the compliance failures of their supervised licensees under HRS Section 467-1.6. This is not theoretical—HREC has fined principal brokers for systemic documentation failures even when the individual agents were also disciplined separately.

Brokers should build an audit protocol that checks every buyer file for the following:

| Audit Item | What to Verify | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| BRA-1 execution date | Must precede first property showing | Agreement dated same day or after showing records |
| Expiration date | Must be a specific calendar date | Blank field or "until closing" language |
| Compensation terms | Specific amount or percentage stated | References to MLS offers or "customary" rates |
| AD-1 delivery | Signed and dated by buyer | Missing or dated after BRA-1 |
| Principal broker identification | Current supervising broker named | Outdated name or wrong office location |

Implementing a [weekly pipeline review system](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) that includes document verification is the most reliable way to catch deficiencies before they reach escrow. Brokers who rely on agents to self-report compliance are statistically more likely to face HREC citations during random audits.

HREC conducts both complaint-driven and random brokerage audits. During a random audit, investigators pull a sample of transaction files from the preceding 24 months. Every buyer-side file must contain the executed BRA-1 and AD-1 with proper dates and complete fields. A single deficient file can trigger a full-office audit expansion.

Brokers should also ensure their agents understand that [missing deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) on agreement renewals creates the same liability as never having an agreement at all. An expired buyer agreement means the agent is working without authorization, period.

## Duration, Termination, and Renewal Protocols

Hawaii law does not impose a statutory maximum duration for buyer agency agreements, but HREC guidance published in January 2026 recommends terms no longer than six months. Many brokerages have adopted 90-day standard terms with optional renewal provisions.

Termination rights must be clearly stated in the agreement. Under HAR Section 16-99-3(b)(7), both the agent and the buyer must have an identified path to terminate the relationship. The HREC standard requires that termination be available upon written notice, though the agreement may specify a notice period of up to 72 hours.

Renewal requires a fresh signature. An agent cannot rely on verbal agreement to extend a buyer relationship beyond the expiration date. The renewed agreement must contain all the same required elements as the original, including updated compensation terms if market conditions or the buyer's preferences have changed. Agents who continue working past expiration without renewal are operating without authorization and forfeit any claim to compensation earned during the unauthorized period.

## How Hawaii's Requirements Compare to Other States

Hawaii's approach sits in the middle of the national spectrum—more prescriptive than states that merely require agency disclosure, but less rigid than states mandating specific statutory forms with no brokerage customization allowed.

| Written agreement mandatory | Yes (HAR 16-99-3) | Yes (DSPS requirement) | Yes (WVREC requirement) |
| Specific state form required | No (BRA-1 recommended, custom allowed) | Yes (WB-36 mandatory) | No (standard forms available) |
| Maximum duration cap | None (6-month guidance) | None | None |
| Compensation specificity | Dollar or percentage required | Dollar or percentage required | Must be stated |
| Dual agency pre-consent | Required in agreement | Separate disclosure | Separate disclosure |
