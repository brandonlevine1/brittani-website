## Nevada New Real Estate Laws 2026: The October Deadline Is Closer Than You Think

The 2025 Nevada Legislative Session produced a wave of statutory changes that take effect in phases throughout 2026, and the biggest batch lands on October 1. If you hold an active license in this state, the phrase **Nevada new real estate laws 2026** should already be triggering action items on your calendar. The Nevada Real Estate Division (NRED) has published updated guidance, but the full regulatory text runs hundreds of pages across multiple chapters of NRS 645 and NAC 645.

Agents who operated under the assumption that post-NAR settlement changes were the last big disruption are in for a correction. Nevada's legislature went further than most Western states this cycle, codifying buyer representation rules, expanding seller disclosure categories, and adding enforcement teeth to wire fraud prevention.

This post breaks down every change that affects your daily practice, the specific forms you need to swap out, and the penalties you face if you don't.

## Buyer Representation Agreements Are Now Mandatory Before Showings

**Senate Bill 217, signed in June 2025, amends NRS 645.254 to require a written buyer representation agreement before any licensee conducts a property showing.** The language mirrors but exceeds the NAR settlement terms because Nevada's version applies to all licensees regardless of MLS membership. The agreement must include four elements: the scope of services, the duration of the agreement (capped at six months unless renewed in writing), the compensation structure, and a clear statement of the buyer's right to terminate.

NRED released the updated form designated as **Form 525 (Buyer Representation Agreement - Residential)** in March 2026. If your brokerage is still using a pre-2026 version or a custom form that omits any of the four required elements, the agreement is voidable and your commission claim has no statutory backing.

The timing requirement is absolute. "Before any property tour" means before the buyer walks through the door, not during the car ride home. Verbal agreements do not satisfy the statute.

## Expanded Seller Disclosure: New Categories and a Shorter Clock

Assembly Bill 104 rewrites portions of NRS 113.130 governing the **Seller's Real Property Disclosure Form (Form 547)**. The updated form adds three new mandatory disclosure categories: prior insurance claims related to water intrusion, the presence of any [short-term rental restrictions](/blog/wisconsin-hoa-condo-requirements-2026) imposed by an HOA or local ordinance, and whether the property has been used as a marijuana cultivation facility (even if legal at the time).

| Disclosure Category | Old Form 547 | New Form 547 (Oct 2026) |
|---------------------|--------------|------------------------|
| Water intrusion claims | Not required | Mandatory - last 5 years |
| STR restrictions | Not required | Mandatory |
| Marijuana cultivation history | Not required | Mandatory |
| Solar lease/PPA details | General mention | Itemized terms required |
| Septic system last service | Not required | Mandatory - date and provider |

The delivery timeline also tightened. Sellers must provide the completed disclosure within three calendar days of mutual acceptance, down from five. Buyers then receive five business days to review and may rescind without penalty if the disclosure reveals material facts not previously known.

> "The legislature's intent is to eliminate the practice of delivering disclosures at the last possible moment, which deprives buyers of meaningful review time." — Assembly Bill 104, Section 3, Legislative Counsel's Digest

Listing agents who allow sellers to delay or deliver incomplete forms expose themselves to discipline under NAC 645.605, which treats facilitation of non-disclosure as a separate violation from the seller's own failure.

## Wire Fraud Prevention Gets Regulatory Teeth

Nevada's amended NAC 645.637 creates an affirmative duty for the listing agent's brokerage to verify wire instructions before transmitting them to any party. This isn't a suggestion or a best-practice bullet point from a CE class. It is now a codified regulation with specific procedural steps.

The brokerage must: confirm the receiving institution's routing number through an independent callback to a verified phone number, document the date and time of verification, and retain that documentation for five years. Failure to perform these steps does not require actual harm to trigger a violation. NRED can impose administrative penalties of up to $10,000 per occurrence even if no funds were actually misdirected.

**This shifts the liability framework significantly.** Before this amendment, wire fraud prevention was largely the title company's domain in practice. Now, if a buyer sends funds to a fraudulent account and the listing brokerage forwarded unverified instructions, the brokerage faces regulatory action regardless of whether the title company also failed. Agents working with [wire instruction verification protocols](/blog/wire-instructions-verification-process) already have a head start, but the Nevada-specific documentation requirements go beyond generic checklists.

## Licensing Fee Changes and the New Technology Assessment

The 2026 budget cycle brought fee adjustments that hit every licensee's wallet during renewal. NRED's updated fee schedule, effective July 1, 2026, includes a new $25 technology infrastructure fee added to both salesperson and broker renewals. This funds the Division's transition to a new licensing management platform expected to launch in Q1 2027.

| Fee Type | Previous Amount | 2026 Amount | Change |
|----------|----------------|-------------|--------|
| Salesperson renewal (2-year) | $300 | $325 | +$25 |
| Broker renewal (2-year) | $400 | $425 | +$25 |
| Late renewal penalty | $100 | $150 | +$50 |
| Permit holder business license | $200 | $200 | No change |
| Duplicate license request | $40 | $40 | No change |

The late renewal penalty increase from $100 to $150 matters because it compounds with the underlying renewal fee. An agent who misses their renewal deadline now pays $475 instead of the previous $400. NRED has stated it will not grant grace periods for agents claiming they were unaware of the new amounts.

## Common Mistakes Agents Will Make Under the New Laws

Based on the transition patterns observed after previous Nevada legislative cycles, here are the specific errors most likely to trigger complaints, lawsuits, or license discipline in late 2026.

**First: using the old Form 525 after October 1.** NRED has announced that buyer representation agreements executed on superseded forms will not satisfy NRS 645.254. Agents who stockpiled pre-printed forms or whose transaction management software hasn't been updated will produce void agreements. This doesn't just risk commission disputes. It creates a situation where the agent technically practiced without authorization during every showing conducted under the deficient agreement.

Second: treating the three-day disclosure delivery window as three business days instead of three calendar days. The old five-day window was measured in business days. The new three-day window under amended NRS 113.130 uses calendar days. A contract executed on Friday means the seller disclosure must be in the buyer's hands by Monday, not Wednesday. Agents who rely on memory rather than [automated deadline tracking](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) will blow this window routinely.

Third: failing to document wire verification callbacks. The regulation requires contemporaneous documentation. Writing it down a week later from memory does not satisfy NAC 645.637. NRED auditors will look for timestamp consistency between the verification call and the documentation entry.

Fourth: omitting the compensation disclosure in buyer representation agreements. Form 525 has a specific section requiring agents to state whether they will seek compensation from the seller, the buyer, or both. Leaving it blank or writing "TBD" renders the form non-compliant. The amount or rate must be stated as a specific figure or percentage, not a range.

Fifth: assuming the six-month cap on buyer agreements means automatic renewal. It does not. NRS 645.254(3) explicitly states that renewal requires a new written agreement. Any agent who continues showing properties after the expiration date is operating without authorization.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce Before October

Brokers carry heightened responsibility under NRS 645.633, which holds the broker accountable for supervisory failures. The 2026 changes expand what constitutes a "failure to supervise" in three specific ways.

**NRED's updated audit checklist, published in May 2026, lists eleven items brokers must verify in every transaction file.** The additions for 2026 include: confirmation that Form 525 was signed prior to the first showing (with a date/time record), documentation of wire instruction verification, and evidence that the updated Form 547 was delivered within the three-day window.

Brokers should conduct a form inventory immediately. Any pre-October versions of Forms 525 and 547 must be purged from office supplies and digital template libraries. The [compliance systems agents use daily](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) need updating to reflect new deadlines and form numbers.

Transaction coordinators operating under broker supervision face the same documentation standards. If your brokerage uses TCs, confirm they have access to the 2026 form set and understand the calendar-day versus business-day distinction. A TC error is still a broker liability under NRS 645.633(1)(g).

NRED has signaled that its first enforcement sweep targeting 2026 compliance will begin in January 2027. That gives brokerages roughly three months after the October effective date to establish consistent practices before auditors arrive. The smart play is to run internal audits in November and December 2026 using the Division's published checklist.

## Dual Agency Disclosure Gets a Timing Requirement

Nevada already permits disclosed dual agency under NRS 645.252, but AB 104 adds a timing component that didn't previously exist. The dual agency consent form (Form 524) must now be signed before an offer is drafted, not merely before an offer is presented. This distinction matters in competitive markets where agents draft offers at the showing.

If an agent realizes mid-transaction that a dual agency situation has arisen and obtains consent retroactively, the consent is valid only if the buyer has not yet submitted an offer. Once an offer exists without prior dual agency consent, the agent must withdraw from representing one party. There is no cure provision for this timing defect.

The practical consequence: agents who list properties and also work with unrepresented buyers must resolve the representation question at first contact. Waiting until a buyer expresses interest in writing an offer creates an impossible compliance timeline.
