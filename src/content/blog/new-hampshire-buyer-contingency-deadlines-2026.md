## Why New Hampshire Buyer Contingency Deadlines 2026 Demand Precision

Missing a single deadline in a New Hampshire real estate transaction can cost your client thousands of dollars and expose your license to disciplinary action. The New Hampshire buyer contingency deadlines 2026 framework rests on a combination of the New Hampshire Real Estate Commission's (NHREC) administrative rules, the state Statute of Frauds under RSA 506:1, and the standardized forms published by the New Hampshire Association of Realtors (NHAR). Understanding how these three pillars interact is the difference between a smooth closing and a breach-of-duty complaint.

The NHREC has consistently disciplined licensees for "failure to exercise reasonable care" under [N.H. Admin. Rule Rea 701.01](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rules/state_agencies/rea.html), and blown contingency deadlines are one of the most cited triggers. If you are a listing agent, buyer's agent, or supervising broker, the stakes for 2026 have not dropped—they have intensified as inventory tightens and buyers stretch their financing timelines.

## The Core Contingency Deadlines in NHAR Form 107

NHAR Form 107, the Standard Purchase and Sale Agreement, is the backbone of virtually every residential deal in the state. It contains pre-printed contingency sections with blank fields where the parties fill in calendar-day deadlines. The most frequently used contingencies—and the ones most prone to missed deadlines—are the inspection contingency, financing contingency, and appraisal contingency.

**The inspection contingency (Section 10 of Form 107) defaults to a negotiated number of calendar days from mutual acceptance.** Agents commonly see 10 to 14 days in seller-favorable markets and up to 21 days in buyer-favorable conditions. During this window, the buyer must complete all inspections and deliver a written response—either acceptance, a repair request, or cancellation—before 5:00 PM on the deadline date.

The financing contingency (Section 12) typically runs 30 to 45 calendar days, aligning with the lender's underwriting timeline. If the buyer cannot obtain a mortgage commitment by the stated date, they must deliver written notice of their inability to secure financing. Failure to deliver that notice means the contingency lapses and the deposit is no longer refundable under that clause. The appraisal contingency, when written as a separate addendum (NHAR Form 116), follows similar mechanics: the buyer has a stated number of days to receive an appraisal at or above the purchase price, with specific written notice obligations on both sides.

## Calendar Days, Not Business Days: The Counting Rules

One of the most common sources of confusion among New Hampshire agents is the method of counting. NHAR Form 107 uses calendar days, and counting begins the day after mutual acceptance. If a contract is mutually executed on a Monday, day one is Tuesday.

| Scenario | Start Date | Deadline (14 Days) | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| Mutual acceptance on Monday | Tuesday = Day 1 | Following Monday | Normal calendar count |
| Mutual acceptance on Friday | Saturday = Day 1 | Friday (two weeks later) | Weekends count |
| Deadline falls on state holiday | N/A | Rolls to next business day | Per NHAR Form 107 language |
| Deadline falls on Sunday | N/A | Rolls to Monday | Per NHAR Form 107 language |

The rollover provision only applies when the deadline itself falls on a weekend or New Hampshire state holiday—not when you prefer extra time. Agents who count "business days" by habit from other states or other contract systems regularly miscalculate and miss deadlines by two or three days.

## What Happens When an Agent Fails to Comply

**License-level consequences start with the NHREC's authority under RSA 331-A:28 to impose fines up to $2,000 per violation, suspend or revoke a license, and require additional education.** The Commission can act on a consumer complaint or a referral from a supervising broker. In practice, missed contingency deadlines that cause financial harm to a party are categorized under "negligent or incompetent practice" (Rea 701.01(d)).

Beyond regulatory action, agents face civil liability. A buyer who loses their earnest money deposit because their agent failed to send written cancellation before the inspection deadline lapses has a clear damages claim. Similarly, a seller who loses a backup offer because their agent allowed an expired contingency to remain ambiguous—rather than demanding the buyer perform or release—can assert negligence. New Hampshire courts have upheld that a licensee's duty of care includes affirmative deadline management, not just passive information relay.

Deal cancellation is the third risk. Under NHAR Form 107, if a buyer's contingency deadline passes without proper written notice, the seller may—at their discretion—declare the contingency waived and demand the buyer proceed to closing. If the buyer then walks away, the seller can retain the earnest money deposit and pursue additional damages. Agents on both sides carry exposure when these situations escalate, especially when [missed deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) could have been prevented with a basic tracking system.

## Common Mistakes New Hampshire Agents Make

Mistake number one: relying on verbal communication to extend a deadline. Under RSA 506:1, any agreement related to the sale of real estate must be in writing. An email from the other agent saying "sure, take an extra week" without a signed addendum (NHAR Form 115) is legally meaningless. Agents who operate on good faith handshakes discover this reality at the worst possible moment.

Mistake number two: confusing the inspection deadline with the repair negotiation deadline. Form 107 allows the buyer a set number of days to complete inspections and respond in writing. The seller then has a separate response period. Agents routinely conflate these two windows, assuming the buyer has more time than they actually do. The inspection response must be delivered before the buyer's deadline expires—not after the seller responds to an earlier request.

**Mistake number three: failing to deliver written notice in the manner specified by the contract.** NHAR Form 107 typically requires delivery by email, hand delivery, or certified mail to the address or email listed in the contact section. A text message to the other agent's cell phone does not satisfy the contractual delivery requirement unless the contract explicitly authorizes it. Agents who send a text at 4:55 PM on deadline day and assume they have complied are taking an enormous risk.

Mistake number four: neglecting to calendar the financing contingency separately from the closing date. These are different deadlines. A contract might close in 60 days but require financing commitment in 30 days. If day 30 passes without a mortgage commitment letter and the buyer's agent has not requested an extension, the seller can terminate and retain the deposit.

Mistake number five: not confirming mutual acceptance date and time with the other party immediately. Disagreements about when the contract became binding shift every single downstream deadline. Agents should confirm in writing—same day—the exact date and time of mutual acceptance and distribute a deadline schedule to all parties.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Supervising brokers in New Hampshire carry direct statutory responsibility under RSA 331-A:16 for the actions of their affiliated licensees. The NHREC expects brokers to maintain systems that catch deadline errors before they metastasize into complaints. This is not optional oversight—it is a license condition.

| Audit Item | Frequency | What to Look For |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Deadline tracking documentation | Every transaction | Written deadline schedule distributed to all parties within 48 hours of mutual acceptance |
| Addendum execution for extensions | Per event | Signed NHAR Form 115 on file before original deadline passes |
| Delivery confirmation records | Per notice | Email read receipts, delivery timestamps, or certified mail tracking |
| Financing contingency status | Weekly after Day 20 | Lender communication confirming progress toward commitment |
| File review post-closing | Within 30 days | Complete contingency paper trail in transaction file |

**Brokers who delegate deadline tracking entirely to agents without a verification system are personally exposed under RSA 331-A:28(II)(h)—failure to supervise.** The NHREC has publicly sanctioned brokers who had no audit process even when the agent-level violation was the proximate cause of harm. A quarterly file audit is the minimum standard; a per-transaction verification system is the defensible standard.

Consider whether your brokerage's current [checklist-based approach](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory) actually triggers reminders or merely sits in a shared drive unmonitored. The distinction matters when the Commission asks what steps you took to prevent the missed deadline.

## The Role of Addenda and Special Contingencies

Beyond the standard inspection, financing, and appraisal contingencies, New Hampshire transactions frequently include sale-of-home contingencies (NHAR Form 113), well and septic contingencies, and radon testing addenda. Each carries its own deadline structure, and each must be tracked independently.

The sale-of-home contingency is particularly dangerous because it introduces a "kick-out" clause with a 72-hour notice window. When the seller receives another offer, the buyer typically has 72 hours to waive the sale-of-home contingency or release the property. That 72-hour clock is not forgiving—it runs continuously, including over weekends, unless the contract specifies otherwise. Agents who do not monitor their phone and email obsessively during a kick-out period risk losing the deal for their buyer with zero recourse.

Well and septic contingencies are common in New Hampshire's rural markets, where municipal water and sewer are unavailable. These contingencies often require test results within 14 to 21 days. The buyer's agent must coordinate the testing company, receive results, and deliver written acceptance or objection within the window—a logistics chain with multiple failure points that compounds the risk of a [deal breaking down](/blog/where-deals-break-down) if any link is slow.

## Building a Defensible Deadline Management System

The most liability-resistant agents in New Hampshire share a few habits. They generate a written deadline schedule within 24 hours of mutual acceptance and distribute it to the client, the cooperating agent, the lender, and the transaction file. They set calendar alerts at three intervals: seven days before, three days before, and the morning of each deadline. They confirm delivery of every contingency notice in writing and retain the confirmation.
