# Wyoming Wire Fraud Prevention Requirements 2026: What Every Agent and Broker Must Do Now

Wire fraud losses in real estate topped $446 million nationally in 2023, according to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center. Wyoming is not immune, and understanding the **Wyoming wire fraud prevention requirements 2026** is no longer optional for any licensee. The Wyoming Real Estate Commission has made it clear that agents who skip disclosure obligations face real consequences, from license discipline to personal civil liability.

This post breaks down the specific statutes, forms, common pitfalls, and broker-level audit requirements that apply to every residential and commercial transaction closing in the state this year.

## How Wyoming Regulates Wire Fraud Disclosures

Wyoming does not have a standalone wire fraud prevention statute the way some states have enacted specific legislation. Instead, the state's enforcement framework rests on the broader duty-of-care obligations codified in the Wyoming Real Estate License Act, specifically Wyoming Statute 33-28-303, which requires licensees to act with reasonable skill, care, and diligence on behalf of their clients.

The [Wyoming Real Estate Commission (WREC)](https://realestate.wyo.gov/) has issued guidance clarifying that failure to warn clients about wire fraud risks constitutes a breach of that duty. WREC's position aligns with the National Association of REALTORS Code of Ethics Article 1, which mandates that REALTORS protect and promote client interests while treating all parties honestly.

In practice, this means every agent must deliver a written wire fraud advisory before the buyer or seller initiates any electronic funds transfer. The advisory must explain common wire fraud schemes, instruct parties to verify wiring instructions by phone using a trusted number, and warn against relying on email alone for financial instructions.

## The Forms You Need to Know

The Wyoming Association of REALTORS (WAR) maintains a forms library that includes a Wire Fraud Advisory. While this form does not carry a state-mandated numeric designation like some contract addenda, it is the most widely recognized advisory document in the state and is integrated into most MLS transaction management platforms used by Wyoming brokerages.

**If your brokerage has created a proprietary wire fraud advisory, confirm with your broker that it meets the minimum content requirements outlined by WREC.** At a minimum, the advisory should identify the specific risks of business email compromise (BEC) schemes, provide a phone verification protocol, and include a dated signature line for all parties.

The WAR Wire Fraud Advisory is typically delivered alongside other buyer and [seller disclosure documents](/blog/wyoming-seller-disclosure-requirements-2026) early in the transaction. Many experienced TCs and agents include it in the initial document package, treating it with the same urgency as the agency disclosure required under W.S. 33-28-304.

| Document | When to Deliver | Who Signs | Required or Recommended |
|---|---|---|---|
| WAR Wire Fraud Advisory | At contract or before first wire | Buyer, Seller, Agents | Strongly recommended by WREC |
| Agency Disclosure (per W.S. 33-28-304) | Before substantive discussions | All parties | Required by statute |
| Lead-Based Paint Disclosure (pre-1978 homes) | Before contract ratification | Buyer and Seller | Required by federal law |
| Seller Property Condition Disclosure | Before or at contract | Seller | Required under WREC rules |

## What Happens If You Fail to Comply

**Agents who neglect wire fraud advisories expose themselves to a cascade of consequences that extend far beyond a WREC reprimand.** The Commission has the authority under W.S. 33-28-303 to impose disciplinary sanctions including license suspension, license revocation, and civil penalties for conduct that falls below the standard of care.

Civil liability is the bigger financial risk. If a buyer wires $350,000 to a fraudster because no one in the transaction warned them about BEC schemes, the buyer's attorney will name everyone involved: the listing agent, the buyer's agent, the title company, and potentially the brokerage. Courts in other states have already found agents negligent for failing to provide wire fraud warnings, and Wyoming's tort framework provides no safe harbor for licensees who skip the advisory.

Errors-and-omissions insurance carriers have also tightened their underwriting. Many E&O policies now include wire fraud exclusions or require proof that the insured agent delivered a written advisory. If you cannot produce a signed wire fraud form from your transaction file, your carrier may deny coverage entirely, leaving you personally liable for the full loss.

Deal cancellation is another risk, though it happens less frequently. When a wire fraud attempt is detected mid-transaction, closings get delayed by days or weeks while the FBI, the bank, and the title company investigate. Buyers sometimes walk, and sellers sometimes refuse extensions. The entire commission evaporates.

## Common Mistakes Wyoming Agents Make

Even agents who know wire fraud is a threat routinely make errors that undermine their compliance. Here are the most common failures WREC and brokerage compliance officers see in Wyoming.

**First, agents deliver the advisory too late.** Handing a wire fraud warning to a buyer at the closing table, minutes before they have already wired funds, is functionally useless. The advisory must arrive early enough for the buyer to absorb the information and implement the phone-verification protocol before any money moves. Best practice is to include it in the initial contract package.

Second, agents rely on verbal warnings instead of written documentation. Telling a buyer over coffee to "be careful with wire instructions" may be well-intentioned, but it produces zero evidence in a dispute. WREC's guidance and standard brokerage policies require a signed, written advisory. Without it, you have no defense.

Third, agents fail to update their clients when closing dates shift. Wire fraud schemes often escalate in the 24-48 hours before a rescheduled closing because the scammer monitors email traffic for date changes. When you move a closing, send a fresh reminder to all parties about verifying wire instructions by phone. A one-time advisory at the start of a 60-day transaction may not be top of mind six weeks later.

Fourth, agents use unencrypted email to send or forward wire instructions. Even when you are simply relaying information from the title company, sending account numbers and routing numbers in a plain-text email creates an interception risk. Wyoming agents should insist on using encrypted email platforms or secure portals provided by the title company. Understanding [how portals protect transaction data](/blog/what-portals-mean-for-tcs) can help you evaluate which tools your brokerage should adopt.

Fifth, agents assume the title company handles everything. Title companies in Wyoming do carry their own wire fraud prevention responsibilities, but that does not absolve the agent. Your duty to your client under W.S. 33-28-303 is independent of the title company's obligations. If the title company drops the ball, the buyer still sues you.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Brokerage owners and designated brokers in Wyoming bear a supervisory duty under W.S. 33-28-302 to ensure that all agents operating under their license comply with WREC rules and the standard of care. That supervisory obligation extends squarely to wire fraud prevention.

**Brokers should audit transaction files at least quarterly to confirm that every closed deal contains a signed wire fraud advisory.** A missing form is not just a paperwork gap; it is an undefended liability sitting in your file cabinet. If a claim arises two years after closing, the absence of that form becomes Exhibit A in the plaintiff's case.

| Audit Item | Frequency | Who Performs | Consequence of Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signed wire fraud advisory in every file | Quarterly | Broker or compliance officer | Broker liability under W.S. 33-28-302 |
| Agent email encryption verification | Semi-annually | IT or broker | Increased BEC exposure for all parties |
| Updated advisory form version check | Annually | Broker or TC | Outdated disclosures may omit current schemes |
| Agent training on wire fraud protocols | Annually (minimum) | Broker | WREC may view as failure to supervise |

Brokers should also mandate annual wire fraud training for every agent on their roster. WREC has not yet codified a specific continuing education requirement for wire fraud, but several CE providers approved by the Commission now offer wire fraud modules that count toward the 24-hour renewal requirement. Requiring agents to complete this training demonstrates a brokerage culture of compliance and strengthens the broker's defense if a claim ever arises.

Policy manuals matter too. Every Wyoming brokerage should have a written wire fraud prevention policy that specifies which form to use, when to deliver it, how to handle wire instruction communications, and what to do if an agent suspects a BEC attack. The policy should be signed by every agent annually and stored in a centralized compliance file.
