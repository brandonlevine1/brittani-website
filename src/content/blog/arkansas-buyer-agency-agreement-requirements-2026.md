## Understanding Arkansas Buyer Agency Agreement Requirements 2026

The Arkansas Real Estate Commission (AREC) has made written buyer representation agreements a non-negotiable part of doing business in 2026. If you hold an active Arkansas license and work with buyers, the **Arkansas buyer agency agreement requirements 2026** affect every showing, every offer, and every commission check you expect to receive. Ignoring them invites disciplinary action, deal cancellations, and lawsuits that could end your career.

These requirements did not appear overnight. They trace back to the NAR settlement finalized in 2024, AREC's subsequent rulemaking updates in 2025, and the Arkansas Real Estate License Law codified at Ark. Code Ann. 17-42-101 et seq. The Commission's enforcement posture in 2026 is notably stricter than in previous years, with audits specifically targeting buyer-side documentation.

This post breaks down the statutes, forms, penalties, and operational changes that agents and brokers must internalize right now.

## The Legal Framework: Statutes and Regulatory Authority

Arkansas buyer agency relationships are governed primarily by Ark. Code Ann. 17-42-313, which defines agency disclosure obligations, and AREC Rule 10.10, which specifies the written agreement requirement for buyer representation. The Commission derives its rulemaking authority from Ark. Code Ann. 17-42-205, giving it broad power to adopt rules consistent with the License Law.

**AREC Rule 10.10, as amended effective January 1, 2026, requires that a written buyer agency agreement be executed before an agent provides substantive brokerage services to a buyer.** Substantive services include showings, market analyses prepared for a specific buyer, offer preparation, and negotiation on a buyer's behalf. A casual conversation at an open house does not trigger the requirement, but the moment you schedule a private showing, you need ink on paper.

The [Arkansas Real Estate Commission's official site](https://www.arec.arkansas.gov/) publishes the full text of these rules and provides compliance bulletins that are updated quarterly. Agents who have not read the Q1 2026 bulletin are already behind.

## Required Forms and Documentation

The Arkansas REALTORS Association (ARA) publishes the standard forms that most brokerages adopt statewide. For buyer representation, agents typically execute one of the following:

| Form Number | Form Name | Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| ARA Form 1-3 | Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement | Single agent represents buyer exclusively for a defined term |
| ARA Form 1-4 | Non-Exclusive Buyer Agency Agreement | Buyer retains right to work with other agents simultaneously |
| ARA Form 1-3A | Amendment to Buyer Agency Agreement | Modifies compensation, term, or scope of existing agreement |
| ARA Form 7-4 | Agency Disclosure and Consent | Confirms agency relationship and required disclosures under 17-42-313 |

Form 1-3 is the workhorse. It specifies the agent's duties, the buyer's obligations, the compensation structure (including whether the buyer pays directly, whether seller concessions apply, or a hybrid model), the definite term of the agreement, and the geographic or property-type scope.

**Every blank in Form 1-3 must be completed or struck through.** AREC auditors flag incomplete fields as a violation, and an incomplete agreement may be deemed unenforceable—meaning you lose your compensation claim entirely. The form must also include the licensee's name, the supervising broker's name, and the brokerage's license number.

If your brokerage uses proprietary forms instead of ARA templates, those forms must contain every element required by AREC Rule 10.10, including a definite expiration date, a clear description of compensation, and a statement of the agent's duties. Brokers who draft custom forms should have them reviewed by legal counsel annually.

## Compensation Disclosure and Transparency Rules

One of the most misunderstood aspects of the 2026 requirements is how compensation must be disclosed within the buyer agency agreement itself. AREC Rule 10.10(c) mandates that the agreement state, in specific dollar or percentage terms, the total compensation the buyer's agent will receive.

Vague language like "customary commission" or "as determined at closing" is explicitly prohibited. The Commission's 2025 enforcement guidance bulletin stated that any agreement lacking a numerical compensation figure—whether flat fee, percentage, or hourly rate—is deficient and exposes the agent to disciplinary proceedings.

This connects directly to how agents handle [situations where deals break down](/blog/where-deals-break-down) over compensation disputes. When a buyer claims they never agreed to pay a certain fee, the agreement's specificity is your only defense.

## What Happens When Agents Fail to Comply

Noncompliance carries three distinct categories of risk: regulatory penalties from AREC, civil liability in court, and transactional fallout at the closing table.

AREC can impose fines of up to $2,500 per violation under Ark. Code Ann. 17-42-311. Repeated violations or patterns of noncompliance can result in license suspension for up to one year or permanent revocation. The Commission's disciplinary database shows a 40% increase in buyer-agency-related complaints between 2024 and 2025, and that trajectory has continued into 2026.

Civil liability emerges when a buyer or seller challenges the agent's right to compensation. Without a properly executed written agreement, Arkansas courts have consistently held that no enforceable compensation obligation exists. The 2023 Arkansas Court of Appeals decision in *Hensley v. Crye-Leike* reinforced that oral agreements for real estate brokerage services are unenforceable under the statute of frauds (Ark. Code Ann. 4-59-101).

At the closing table, title companies and closing attorneys increasingly refuse to disburse buyer-agent compensation absent a fully executed buyer agency agreement in the file. If the agreement is missing or deficient, your check gets held—or never cut at all.

## Common Mistakes Agents Make in Arkansas

Five errors appear repeatedly in AREC disciplinary proceedings and brokerage compliance audits. Each one is preventable.

**First, agents start showings before securing a signed agreement.** The "I'll get it signed later" mentality is the single most common violation. Once you provide substantive services without a written agreement, you have worked for free in the eyes of the law—and exposed yourself to an AREC complaint from your own brokerage or the buyer.

Second, agents use outdated form versions. ARA updated Forms 1-3 and 1-4 in November 2025 to reflect the new compensation disclosure standards. If you are still using 2024 versions pulled from a shared drive, your agreement may not satisfy current AREC requirements. Check ARA's form library quarterly.

Third, agents leave the expiration date blank or write "TBD." AREC Rule 10.10(b) requires a definite term. An agreement without an expiration date is considered indefinite and potentially unenforceable. It also creates confusion if the buyer later claims the relationship expired months ago.

Fourth, agents fail to explain the agreement to the buyer before signing. While not a strict statutory requirement, AREC's guidance strongly recommends a documented explanation. When buyers later complain they "didn't understand what they signed," the absence of any explanation record makes the agent's defense considerably harder.

Fifth, agents confuse the agency disclosure form (Form 7-4) with the buyer agency agreement itself. Form 7-4 discloses the agency relationship but does not create a contractual obligation. You need both documents—disclosure plus agreement—to be fully compliant. This distinction trips up agents who [miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) because they believe one form covers both requirements.

## What Brokers Need to Audit and Enforce

Supervising brokers carry personal liability for their agents' compliance failures under Ark. Code Ann. 17-42-308. A broker who allows agents to operate without proper buyer agency agreements faces the same fines and license jeopardy as the individual agent.

| Audit Item | Frequency | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Signed buyer agency agreement in file | Before first showing | Missing or unsigned copy |
| Compensation amount specified | Before first showing | Blank field or vague language |
| Definite expiration date present | Before first showing | "TBD" or blank |
| Current form version used | Quarterly review | Pre-November 2025 editions |
| Agency disclosure (Form 7-4) separate from agreement | Every transaction | Only one form in file |
| Agreement matches closing disbursement | At closing | Commission amount differs from agreement |

**Brokers should conduct file audits at three checkpoints: before the first showing, at contract execution, and before closing.** Waiting until closing to check for a buyer agency agreement means the violation has already occurred and persisted for weeks or months.

Implementing a structured [weekly pipeline review](/blog/weekly-pipeline-review-system) that includes a documentation compliance check prevents violations from compounding across multiple active transactions. The cost of auditing proactively is a fraction of the cost of defending an AREC complaint.
