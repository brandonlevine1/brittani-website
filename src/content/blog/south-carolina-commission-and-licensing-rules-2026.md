## Understanding South Carolina Commission & Licensing Rules 2026

The South Carolina Real Estate Commission, housed under the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR), governs every licensee in the state through Title 40, Chapter 57 of the South Carolina Code of Laws. If you hold a license here, the **South Carolina commission & licensing rules 2026** affect how you get paid, how you maintain your license, and how you avoid the kind of regulatory action that can end a career. This is not a soft-enforcement state — the Commission meets regularly, reviews complaints aggressively, and publishes disciplinary actions publicly.

The post-NAR settlement environment has added new layers to an already detailed regulatory framework. South Carolina agents now operate under both longstanding state statutes and newer practice changes that took effect in late 2024, with additional rule clarifications published in early 2026.

## Commission Structure and Payment Rules Under S.C. Code Section 40-57-350

**All commission payments in South Carolina must flow through the broker-in-charge.** This is not a suggestion. Section 40-57-350 explicitly prohibits any licensee from receiving compensation for real estate services from anyone other than their employing broker. An agent who accepts payment directly from a seller, buyer, or cooperating brokerage has committed a statutory violation.

The statute also prohibits fee-splitting with unlicensed individuals. This means referral fees to unlicensed assistants, friends, or out-of-state contacts without an active license are illegal. The Commission has fined agents $500 to $2,500 for referral fee violations in recent disciplinary actions.

Compensation negotiability remains intact. South Carolina does not set or regulate the percentage of commission charged — that remains a matter of negotiation between broker and client, documented in listing agreements (SC Form 310) or buyer-broker agreements (SC Form 540).

| Rule | Statute | Requirement |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Commission must flow through broker | S.C. Code 40-57-350 | All compensation to licensee via BIC only |
| No fee-splitting with unlicensed persons | S.C. Code 40-57-350(B) | Referral fees only to active licensees |
| Written agreement required before services | S.C. Code 40-57-370 | Brokerage relationship must be in writing |
| Buyer-broker compensation disclosure | NAR settlement compliance | Cannot offer compensation via MLS |
| Commission disputes | SC Real Estate Commission | Commission does not adjudicate civil disputes |

## Licensing Requirements and Renewal Deadlines

South Carolina licenses renew on a biennial cycle, with the renewal deadline falling on June 30 of even-numbered years for brokers and odd-numbered years staggered by last name. The 2026 renewal cycle requires completion of 8 hours of continuing education, including 4 hours of mandatory core curriculum approved by the Commission.

Failure to renew by the deadline places your license in inactive status immediately. You cannot practice, show property, negotiate contracts, or earn commission while inactive. The Commission charges a $25 late fee, but the real cost is any pending transaction where you cannot legally participate.

Reinstatement after a lapse of more than one year but less than five years requires completing all CE hours missed plus passing the state exam portion again. After five years of inactivity, you start from scratch — full pre-licensing coursework and both exam portions.

## Buyer-Broker Agreements and the New Form 540 Requirements

The South Carolina Association of Realtors updated Form 540 (Exclusive Buyer Brokerage Agreement) to align with NAR settlement requirements. Every agent representing a buyer must now have a signed Form 540 in hand before showing any property. This form specifies the compensation amount or rate the buyer agrees to pay, the duration of the agreement, and the scope of services provided.

**Failing to secure a signed buyer-broker agreement before touring a property exposes you to Commission disciplinary action and potential loss of compensation.** The Commission issued guidance in January 2026 confirming that the written agreement requirement is not optional under any circumstances, including open houses where a previously unrepresented buyer attends.

Form 540 also includes a provision explaining that the buyer's obligation to pay may be offset by seller contributions. Agents must explain this clearly — not just hand the form over and point to a signature line. The Commission has flagged "signature without explanation" as a potential violation of the duty to deal honestly under Section 40-57-135(A)(15).

If you are tracking multiple forms across dozens of transactions, this is where [missing deadlines becomes a real liability](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines). One unsigned Form 540 can mean zero compensation on a closed deal.

## Common Mistakes South Carolina Agents Make

**Mistake 1: Advertising without broker approval or missing the brokerage name.** SC Regulation 105-10 requires all advertising — including social media posts, Zillow profiles, and yard signs — to include the licensee's name as registered with the Commission and the employing broker's trade name. Agents who create Instagram accounts or run Facebook ads under a personal brand without the brokerage name visible face fines starting at $500.

**Mistake 2: Practicing outside their license level.** South Carolina distinguishes between salesperson (Form 1, initial license), broker (Form 3), and broker-in-charge (BIC designation). A salesperson cannot supervise other agents, operate a property management division, or hold escrow funds. A broker who has not registered as BIC with the Commission cannot manage a branch office. The Commission revoked two licenses in 2025 for agents operating as de facto BICs without proper designation.

**Mistake 3: Failing to disclose agency relationships at first substantive contact.** Section 40-57-370 requires disclosure of brokerage relationships at the earliest practical opportunity, using the SC Disclosure of Brokerage Relationships form. Agents who wait until contract writing have already violated the statute. The Commission interprets "first substantive contact" broadly — it includes phone conversations where property details are discussed.

**Mistake 4: Commingling earnest money deposits.** Brokers-in-charge must deposit trust funds within 48 hours of acceptance of a binding contract per Commission regulation. Agents who hold checks "until the inspection period passes" or keep deposits in personal accounts create an immediate violation. Penalties range from fines to license revocation depending on the amount involved.

**Mistake 5: Continuing to practice after a team leader or BIC departs the firm.** When a BIC leaves a brokerage, every agent under their supervision must either transfer to a new BIC or cease practicing until one is designated. The Commission has documented cases where agents closed transactions during a BIC vacancy, resulting in those agents being personally liable for compliance violations.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce

Brokers-in-charge carry personal liability for compliance failures within their office. The SC Real Estate Commission's [disciplinary actions database](https://llr.sc.gov/re/discipline.aspx) shows that BICs receive sanctions at higher rates than individual agents precisely because the state assigns supervisory responsibility upward.

Every BIC should maintain a compliance audit checklist covering these areas: active license verification for every affiliated agent, signed buyer-broker agreements in every buyer file, timely trust account deposits with corresponding ledger entries, advertising review for all agent-produced materials, and confirmation that agency disclosure was delivered and signed at first contact. This is the type of operational rigor that separates [agents who rely on memory from those who follow systems](/blog/why-checklists-beat-memory).

| Audit Area | Frequency | Key Document |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| License status verification | Monthly | LLR online lookup |
| Trust account reconciliation | Monthly | Bank statements + ledger |
| Buyer-broker agreement review | Per transaction | SC Form 540 |
| Agency disclosure confirmation | Per transaction | SC Disclosure form |
| Advertising compliance | Quarterly | Screenshots + reviews |
| CE completion tracking | Annually | Agent CE certificates |

Brokers who fail to maintain trust account records face automatic suspension proceedings. The Commission treats trust fund violations as the most serious category of offense, above advertising violations or CE lapses.

## Penalties and Enforcement Actions for Non-Compliance

The SC Real Estate Commission has authority under Section 40-57-150 to impose fines up to $10,000 per violation, suspend or revoke licenses, require additional education, and issue public reprimands. In practice, first-time minor violations typically result in a letter of caution or a fine between $500 and $1,500. Repeat offenders and trust fund violators face suspension.

**A license revocation in South Carolina is permanent absent a formal petition to the Commission after a minimum waiting period.** There is no automatic reinstatement path. Agents who lose their license must petition the Commission, demonstrate rehabilitation, and often retake all pre-licensing coursework.

Deal cancellation is another risk. If a buyer discovers their agent was unlicensed at the time of contract execution (due to a lapse, for example), the contract may be voidable. The agent loses their commission claim, and their broker may face a civil lawsuit for negligent supervision.
