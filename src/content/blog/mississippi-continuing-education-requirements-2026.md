## Mississippi Continuing Education Requirements 2026: The Rules Have Not Relaxed

Mississippi continuing education requirements 2026 carry the same weight they did last cycle — and the Mississippi Real Estate Commission (MREC) shows no signs of easing enforcement. Every active licensee, whether a salesperson or a broker, must complete 16 hours of approved continuing education before their license renewal date. Miss that window and you are not just inconvenienced; you are legally barred from practicing.

The governing authority here is the Mississippi Real Estate Brokers License Act of 1954, codified under Mississippi Code Annotated Section 73-35-1 et seq., and the [administrative rules adopted by MREC](https://www.mrec.state.ms.us/). MREC Rule 3.1 specifically outlines the CE mandate, and MREC Rule 3.2 places audit responsibility on responsible brokers. These are not optional guidelines — they carry the force of law.

## How the 16-Hour Requirement Breaks Down

**Not all 16 hours are created equal.** MREC mandates that 8 of the 16 hours come from Commission-prescribed topics, which change periodically to reflect legislative updates, fair housing developments, and license law revisions. The remaining 8 hours are elective, meaning agents can choose from any MREC-approved course provider and topic.

Here is how the hour allocation typically looks for a 2026 renewal cycle:

| Category | Hours Required | Topic Examples | Provider Restriction |
|----------|---------------|----------------|---------------------|
| MREC Mandatory | 8 | License law updates, agency law, fair housing, trust accounts | Must be MREC-designated course |
| Elective | 8 | Contract writing, ethics, marketing compliance, risk management | Any MREC-approved provider |
| Total | 16 | — | — |

Each course must carry a valid MREC course approval number. If you complete a course that has lost its approval status — even unknowingly — those hours will not count. Always verify the provider's current approval on the MREC website before paying tuition.

Agents renewing for the first time after initial licensure have a slightly different path: they must complete a 30-hour post-license course within the first renewal period, in addition to meeting the 16-hour CE standard. That 30-hour requirement is separate and does not substitute for the annual CE mandate.

## Deadlines, Forms, and the Renewal Mechanics

Mississippi real estate licenses expire on a staggered basis. Your specific renewal date depends on when you were originally licensed, and MREC sends renewal notices approximately 60 days prior to expiration. The renewal application form — MREC Form 5 for salespersons and MREC Form 6 for brokers — must be submitted with proof of completed CE and the applicable renewal fee.

**Filing late is not a gray area in Mississippi.** If your license expires without completed CE, your status flips to inactive immediately. You cannot list properties, show homes, negotiate contracts, or collect referral fees while inactive. Any transaction activity conducted on an inactive license constitutes unlicensed practice — a misdemeanor under Mississippi Code Section 73-35-29.

Reinstatement after expiration requires completing all overdue CE, submitting an MREC Reinstatement Application, and paying a reinstatement fee that is typically double the standard renewal fee. If more than one renewal cycle has lapsed, MREC may require additional coursework or even re-examination before reactivation.

## What Happens When an Agent Fails to Comply

The consequences of non-compliance are layered and affect more than just the individual agent. MREC has the authority under Mississippi Code Section 73-35-21 to impose fines, suspend or revoke licenses, and issue public reprimands. Here is the escalation path:

First, the license goes inactive. The agent cannot legally practice. Second, any deals the agent closes while unlicensed may be subject to commission disputes or contract rescission. Third, the responsible broker faces potential disciplinary action for failing to supervise compliance under MREC Rule 3.2.

Fines for practicing on an expired or inactive license can reach $1,000 per violation, and each transaction closed while unlicensed may constitute a separate violation. Beyond the regulatory penalties, agents expose themselves to civil liability — a buyer or seller who discovers their agent was unlicensed at the time of the transaction has grounds to void the agreement or pursue damages.

This is not hypothetical. MREC publishes disciplinary actions on its website, and license law violations rank among the most common infractions cited. Agents who treat CE deadlines casually are gambling with their careers and their clients' transactions. Understanding [why agents miss deadlines](/blog/why-agents-miss-deadlines) is the first step toward avoiding this trap.

## Common Mistakes Mississippi Agents Make With CE Compliance

Five errors appear repeatedly in MREC enforcement actions and audit findings. Recognizing them before they happen is the simplest form of risk management.

**Mistake one: completing courses from unapproved providers.** Agents find a cheap online course, complete it, and assume the hours will count. MREC maintains a specific list of approved providers, and hours from unapproved sources are rejected during the renewal audit. Always confirm the MREC approval number before enrolling.

Mistake two: waiting until the final week before renewal to start coursework. Sixteen hours of CE cannot be meaningfully completed in a weekend rush, and technical issues with online platforms or scheduling conflicts with classroom courses leave no margin for error. MREC does not grant extensions for procrastination.

Mistake three: failing to retain certificates of completion. MREC may audit your CE records at any time during the renewal cycle. If you cannot produce certificates showing course name, approval number, completion date, and hours earned, those hours are treated as incomplete. Digital storage is fine — just make sure the files are accessible.

Mistake four: confusing post-license education with continuing education. New agents sometimes believe their 30-hour post-license course replaces the 16-hour CE requirement. It does not. Both obligations run concurrently during the first renewal period.

Mistake five: assuming broker-provided training counts as CE. Internal brokerage training sessions, sales meetings, and coaching calls do not qualify unless the specific session carries an MREC course approval number. Good training is valuable, but it does not check the regulatory box unless MREC says it does.

## What Brokers Must Audit and Enforce Under MREC Rules

Responsible brokers carry a legal obligation that extends beyond their own CE compliance. MREC Rule 3.2 explicitly requires brokers to verify that every licensee operating under their supervision has met continuing education requirements. This is not a suggestion — it is an enforceable regulation.

**A broker who allows an agent with an expired license to conduct transactions faces the same disciplinary exposure as the agent.** MREC can fine the broker, suspend the broker's license, or downgrade the brokerage's standing with the Commission. The logic is straightforward: the broker-agent relationship is supervisory by statute, and supervision means verification.

Here is a practical audit framework brokers should implement:

| Audit Action | Frequency | Documentation |
|--------------|-----------|---------------|
| Verify each agent's renewal date | Quarterly | Spreadsheet or CRM tracker |
| Collect CE completion certificates | 30 days before each agent's renewal | Digital copies stored in agent file |
| Confirm course approval numbers against MREC list | At time of certificate collection | Cross-reference MREC provider database |
| Review MREC disciplinary bulletins | Monthly | Flag any agent-related notices |
| Submit or confirm renewal forms filed | On renewal date | Confirmation receipt from MREC |

Brokers who rely on agents to self-report compliance are accepting unnecessary risk. Building a tracking system — even a simple calendar with reminder alerts — provides a defensible record that the brokerage took its supervisory duties seriously. ## Mississippi CE vs. Neighboring States: A Quick Comparison

Agents who hold licenses in multiple states or are considering [license portability](/blog/wisconsin-license-reciprocity-portability-2026) often wonder how Mississippi stacks up against its neighbors. The answer: Mississippi sits in the middle of the pack on total hours but is stricter than average on mandatory topic requirements.

| State | Total CE Hours Per Cycle | Mandatory Hours | Cycle Length |
|-------|--------------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Mississippi | 16 | 8 | Annual |
| Alabama | 15 | 6 | 2 years |
| Tennessee | 16 | 6 (core) | 2 years |
| Louisiana | 12 | 4 | Annual |
| Arkansas | 7 | 0 (all elective) | Annual |

Mississippi's annual cycle means agents cannot coast for two years between renewals. The 8-hour mandatory block is also notably rigid — MREC dictates not just the topics but often the specific course itself. Agents licensed in multiple states need separate tracking systems for each jurisdiction's requirements, deadlines, and forms.
